The kiln breathed heat into the studio when Mara lifted the brick door. She waited, face turned aside, until the first white rush thinned.

On the shelves inside, bowls glowed in their cooling rows: blue after blue, deep as rainwater, each one familiar. She smiled despite herself. The glaze had behaved.

Then she saw the green bowl.

It sat near the back, small and plain, its surface the color of moss after a storm. Mara reached in with tongs and drew it out. For a moment she only held it, frowning—not angry, exactly, but startled by the betrayal of it. Green had no business there. She had measured everything.

She set it apart on the worktable. The blue bowls seemed suddenly rehearsed.

Later, she returned for the green one. Its color had settled in the daylight, rich and impossible to ignore. Mara carried it to the front window and placed it alone on the sill.

Outside, people passed without looking. Inside, she stood beside it, smiling now, though she could not have said why.
