A book club does not need to require that everyone finish a book before discussing it. Welcoming participants who stopped reading makes the group more inclusive: people may have limited time, dislike the book, or still have valuable reactions to its opening or central ideas. Their perspectives can reveal where the book loses readers and prevent the club from becoming an obligation.

However, discussion quality can suffer if members have read very different portions. Those who finished may want to explore the ending, character development, or larger themes, while others may encounter spoilers or lack context. A useful compromise is to invite everyone, but establish clear discussion practices. Begin with a spoiler-free section for people who have not finished, then announce when the conversation will include the ending. Participants who stopped reading can explain why, while finishers should avoid treating completion as a measure of seriousness. For especially complex books, the club might occasionally choose a “finishers’ discussion,” but inclusivity should be the default. The goal is thoughtful conversation, not compulsory reading.
