The inventor carried a small cardboard box into the village square, with strings running through holes in its sides. He placed it beside the well and pulled one string. The box opened, lowered itself into the water, filled, and rose again without anyone leaning dangerously over the edge. With another pull, it tipped neatly into a waiting bucket. Then he attached a longer cord and demonstrated how a child could haul water, lift firewood, or send tools up to a rooftop. The villagers tested it themselves: the baker raised flour, the farmer lifted a sack of grain, and an old woman drew water while sitting comfortably. It was simple, cheap, and easily repaired—the most useful invention of all.
