A workable policy is to reserve roughly **70% of daily capacity for appointments and 30% for walk-ins**, then adjust that split after a month of tracking demand.

Appointments should receive a guaranteed check-in time and a realistic service window—not a promise that every repair will finish at a fixed hour. Walk-ins should be served in arrival order within their reserved capacity, with an estimated wait provided before customers commit. A brief initial assessment can identify urgent safety issues and jobs requiring parts or specialist work.

To keep the system flexible:

- Release unused appointment slots to walk-ins shortly before the scheduled time.
- Allow a clearly stated grace period for late arrivals, after which their slot may be reassigned.
- Stop accepting same-day walk-ins when remaining capacity is full, offering the next available appointment instead.
- Keep a small time buffer for repairs that take longer than expected.

The **main compromise is predictability versus flexibility**. Reserving appointment capacity helps customers plan, but walk-ins may wait even when a technician appears available because that time is committed to an upcoming booking. Reserving walk-in capacity also means fewer advance appointments. Clear explanations, honest wait estimates, and periodic adjustments make that trade-off manageable without promising immediate service to everyone.
