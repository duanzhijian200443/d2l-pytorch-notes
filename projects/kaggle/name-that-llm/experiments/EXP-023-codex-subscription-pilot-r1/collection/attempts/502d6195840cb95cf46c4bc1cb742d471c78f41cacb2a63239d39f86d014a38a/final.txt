A sunlit sidewalk patch measures the temperature of that specific surface, not the air across a neighborhood. Concrete absorbs sunlight and can become much hotter than nearby air, while shade, grass, trees, buildings, and water stay cooler. Local conditions also matter: the patch’s color, material, moisture, exposure to wind, and time of day all affect its temperature. Air temperature is usually measured in shaded, well-ventilated locations to avoid direct heating from surfaces and sunlight. A neighborhood contains many different microclimates, so temperatures can vary from one street or yard to another. To estimate neighborhood air temperature reliably, measurements must be taken at multiple representative locations using proper air-temperature instruments.
