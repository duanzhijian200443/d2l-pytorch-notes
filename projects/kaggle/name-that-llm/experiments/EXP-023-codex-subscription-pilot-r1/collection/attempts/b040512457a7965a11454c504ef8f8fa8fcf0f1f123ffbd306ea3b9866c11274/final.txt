A soap bubble shows changing colors because light reflects from both the bubble’s outer and inner surfaces. These two reflected waves overlap, sometimes reinforcing particular colors and sometimes canceling them. As gravity drains liquid downward, the soap film becomes thinner, changing the distance the light travels inside it. That changes which colors are strengthened or weakened, so the bubble’s appearance shifts through different hues. The colors are not pigments in the soap; they are produced by **thin-film interference**, the same effect seen in oil slicks and wet pavement. When the film becomes extremely thin, some colors fade or dark patches appear, signaling that the bubble is close to bursting.
