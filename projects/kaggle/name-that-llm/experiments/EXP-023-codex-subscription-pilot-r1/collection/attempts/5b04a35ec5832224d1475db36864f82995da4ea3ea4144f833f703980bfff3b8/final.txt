A volunteer group should choose its decision process based on the stakes, urgency, and need for shared commitment.

**Consensus works best** when decisions affect the group’s mission, values, or members’ ongoing participation. It is especially useful in small groups where people trust one another, have time to discuss differences, and will share the work of implementation. Volunteers can leave more easily than employees, so genuine agreement can strengthen commitment. However, consensus needs clear ground rules; otherwise, prolonged discussion or a single holdout can stall progress.

**Assigning one person final responsibility works best** when decisions are urgent, technical, routine, or require consistent coordination. An event organizer, for example, may need authority to resolve scheduling conflicts quickly. That person should have relevant expertise, a clearly defined mandate, and accountability to the group—not unlimited control.

Often, a hybrid is strongest: agree collectively on goals, budgets, and boundaries, then delegate operational decisions to named leaders, with regular opportunities for feedback and review.
