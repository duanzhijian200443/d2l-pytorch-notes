Preserving an old building exactly as it was protects its historical authenticity, craftsmanship, and cultural meaning. However, strict preservation can be expensive and may leave the building inaccessible or underused. Adapting it for a new public use—such as a library, museum, or community center—can give it renewed social value and secure funding for maintenance. Yet alterations may weaken its original character. The best approach often balances careful conservation with sensitive, reversible changes that meet modern safety and accessibility needs.
