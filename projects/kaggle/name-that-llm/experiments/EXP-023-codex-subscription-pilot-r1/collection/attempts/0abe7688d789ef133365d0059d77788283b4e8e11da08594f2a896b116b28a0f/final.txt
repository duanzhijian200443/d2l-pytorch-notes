A local search index preprocesses documents into a structure that maps terms to relevant content. Queries are fast and responsive, making it ideal for search-as-you-type, especially across many documents. However, it uses extra storage, takes time to build, and must be updated when documents change.

Searching every document from scratch avoids index maintenance and always checks current content, but repeatedly reads and scans the entire collection. This becomes slow and resource-intensive as data grows. For small collections or infrequent searches, scanning may suffice.
