A software interface that hides complicated internal work behind a small set of actions is designed to provide **abstraction**. It lets users or other programs interact with a system without needing to understand its internal algorithms, data structures, dependencies, or implementation details.

For example, a payment interface might offer simple actions such as `pay()` or `refund()`, while internally handling authentication, fraud checks, currency conversion, database updates, and communication with banks.

This approach makes software easier to use, reduces the amount of knowledge required by developers, and lowers the risk of misuse. It also improves maintainability: the internal implementation can change without forcing every user of the interface to change their code. Such an interface can standardize common operations, hide unnecessary complexity, and create a clear boundary between a system’s public behavior and private implementation. In object-oriented design, this idea is often associated with **encapsulation** and **abstraction**; when it presents a simplified front to a complex subsystem, it may also be called a **facade**.
