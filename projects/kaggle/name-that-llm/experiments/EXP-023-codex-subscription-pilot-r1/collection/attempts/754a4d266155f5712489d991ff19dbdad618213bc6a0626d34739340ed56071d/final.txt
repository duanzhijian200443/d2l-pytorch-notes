Inviting one famous performer can give the festival a clear headline and attract larger crowds, media coverage, sponsors, and visitors from outside the area. A recognizable name may also make tickets easier to sell and raise the festival’s profile for future years. However, this approach concentrates the budget and risk on one act. If the performer cancels, disappoints, or appeals only to a narrow audience, the festival may have few alternatives. Extra security, technical, and hospitality requirements may also create hidden costs.

Booking several less-known local performers offers greater variety and can appeal to different age groups and musical tastes. It supports the local creative community, strengthens relationships with residents, and gives emerging artists valuable exposure. A longer, more diverse program may also encourage attendees to stay at the festival longer. The disadvantages are that local acts may attract fewer visitors, require more scheduling and coordination, and generate less publicity.

The best choice depends on the festival’s main goal. If it aims to increase tourism, ticket sales, or regional recognition, a famous performer may be the stronger investment. If it prioritizes community participation, cultural diversity, and long-term local development, several local performers are likely better. A compromise could be to book one moderately well-known headline act alongside a small selection of local artists.
