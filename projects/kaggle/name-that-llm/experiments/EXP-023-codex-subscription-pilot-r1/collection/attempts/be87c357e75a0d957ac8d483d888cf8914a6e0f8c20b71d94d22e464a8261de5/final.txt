Returning the borrowed ladder, Mara carried it through the rain to Mr. Vale’s porch. He opened the door before she knocked, smiling as though he had been watching the clouds.

“Perfect timing,” he said. “I’ve just made tea.”

Mara leaned the ladder against the wall. “I should apologize. I kept it for three weeks.”

“You repaired the loose rung. That makes us even.”

Inside, the house smelled of cinnamon and old books. While they talked, Mr. Vale mentioned a small garden concert he hosted every spring. Mara had always avoided neighborhood gatherings, afraid of awkward silences and unfamiliar faces. Yet his invitation sounded less like an obligation than a doorway.

“Come Saturday,” he said. “Bring anyone you like.”

Mara glanced at the rain, then smiled. “All right. I’d love to come.”
