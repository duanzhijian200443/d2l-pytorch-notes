Trees can exchange resources through mycorrhizal fungi: fungal threads connect with roots, receiving sugars from plants while helping deliver water and minerals such as phosphorus and nitrogen. Carbon or nutrients may sometimes move between plants through shared fungal networks, especially where gradients or stress favor transfer. Roots also communicate chemically through soil organisms. However, “wood-wide web” metaphors can overstate the evidence: forests do not have human-like thoughts, intentions, or conversations. These are biological exchanges shaped by ecology and evolution.
