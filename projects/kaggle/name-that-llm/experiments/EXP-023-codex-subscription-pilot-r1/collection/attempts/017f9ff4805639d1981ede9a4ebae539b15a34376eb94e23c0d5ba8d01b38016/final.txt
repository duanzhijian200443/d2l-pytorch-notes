A shadow edge is sharpest close to the object because the object blocks nearly the same rays from every part of the light source. If the light source were a tiny point, all rays would travel in one direction, producing a sharply defined shadow. A larger, extended light source sends rays in many slightly different directions. Just behind the object, much of the source is still blocked, so the shadow remains relatively dark and its edge is narrow. Farther away, rays from different parts of the source spread out, creating a wider penumbra—a region where only part of the source is blocked. The transition from light to shadow therefore becomes fuzzier. A larger source, or one closer to the object, generally produces a wider, softer shadow edge.
