Thank them warmly and be specific about what would help most. You can acknowledge the generosity while setting a smaller boundary:

“I really appreciate how willing you are to help—that’s incredibly kind. I don’t want to take up too much of your time, though. What would make the biggest difference is just [specific small task], if that feels easy for you. I can handle the rest.”

Keeping the request concrete makes it easier for them to say yes without creating an open-ended commitment. Offer options when possible: “Would you rather help for 20 minutes on Saturday, or could you send me the contact/info you mentioned?” This gives them control over the level of involvement.

If they continue offering more, repeat your appreciation without overexplaining: “That’s so generous. Truly, the smaller version is enough and would help me a lot.” You do not need to justify why you prefer less help.

You can also make the arrangement time-limited and clear: agree on a start and end time, define the one task, and avoid adding new requests during it. Afterward, thank them and, if appropriate, reciprocate in a modest way—coffee, a note, or an offer to return the favor later.

The goal is to accept their care, not their entire proposed project.
