Fresh snow contains many air pockets that absorb and scatter sound, especially higher frequencies. Its soft, uneven surface reduces reflections from the ground, while snow also blankets leaves, soil, and objects that might otherwise rattle or reflect noise. Fewer people and vehicles may be active during snowfall as well, reinforcing the impression of quiet.

As snow compacts, melts, and refreezes, it becomes denser and smoother. Ice contains far fewer air spaces, so it absorbs less sound and acts more like a hard surface, reflecting sound instead. Footsteps may also become sharper and louder as boots crunch or scrape across icy crust. Thus the muted “snow silence” often fades when fresh powder turns to ice.
