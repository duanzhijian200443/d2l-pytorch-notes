A public library should balance preserving beloved books with making room for new voices. Worn copies often reflect genuine community demand, so removing them simply because they look old could disappoint loyal readers. If they remain popular, the library should repair them or buy replacement copies when possible.

However, shelf space and budgets are limited, and newer titles can welcome readers whose interests or experiences are not yet represented. Librarians should consider borrowing records, physical condition, cultural value, and community requests rather than choosing between “old” and “new” in the abstract.

The best approach is selective renewal: retain a few well-loved books, replace copies that are beyond repair, and regularly introduce diverse titles. A library should honor its community’s attachments while helping its reading interests grow.
