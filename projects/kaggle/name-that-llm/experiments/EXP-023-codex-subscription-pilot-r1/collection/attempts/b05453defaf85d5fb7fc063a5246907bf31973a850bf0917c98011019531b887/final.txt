Try a **“small job, closed door” rule**: decide what counts as finished *before* you begin, and explicitly exclude the rest of the room.

When you notice yourself imagining a perfect room, say: “That’s a different project. This job is just ten minutes.”

Then:

1. **Name one visible action.** Replace “clean the kitchen” with “wash the dishes currently in the sink.” Avoid goals like “make it look nice,” which have no clear endpoint.
2. **Write a finish line.** For example: “Done means these dishes are washed—not that the counters, floor, or cupboards are clean.”
3. **Set a ten-minute timer.** Give yourself permission to stop when it rings, even if the task isn’t complete. The timer limits your commitment; it isn’t a speed test.
4. **Park extra tasks.** If you spot crumbs or clutter, jot them down rather than switching jobs.
5. **Actually stop.** Notice what changed, and acknowledge: “I did the task I agreed to do.”

The important practice is not squeezing more cleaning into ten minutes. It’s learning that starting one task does **not** obligate you to finish everything around it. Keeping that promise to yourself can make the next start feel less costly—and “good enough” more believable.
