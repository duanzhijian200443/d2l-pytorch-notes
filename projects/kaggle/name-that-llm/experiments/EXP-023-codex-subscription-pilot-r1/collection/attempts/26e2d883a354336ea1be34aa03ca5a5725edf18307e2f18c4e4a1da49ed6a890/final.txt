A single large annual event can be easier to organize because planning, promotion, permitting, vendor coordination, and staffing are concentrated into one project. It may attract more visitors, generate stronger publicity, and create a memorable community tradition. Large attendance can also improve sponsorship opportunities and sales. However, it requires substantial preparation, higher upfront costs, and careful contingency planning. If weather, staffing, or logistics fail, the market may lose its main opportunity for the year.

Several smaller gatherings distribute the workload and financial risk across the year. They allow organizers to test activities, refine operations, build customer relationships gradually, and respond to changing community interests. Regular events can encourage repeat visits and provide steadier sales for vendors. They may also be easier to staff and manage at a smaller scale. On the other hand, each gathering requires separate promotion, setup, permits, and coordination, which can create cumulative administrative work. Smaller events may attract fewer visitors individually and generate less media attention. The best choice depends on available staff, budget, goals, and tolerance for operational risk.
