A seed can be alive without actively growing. For example, a bean seed stored in a dry packet is alive but dormant. Its cells carry out only minimal life processes, and its embryo remains inactive because it lacks enough water, warmth, or suitable conditions. During dormancy, the seed uses very little stored energy and may remain viable for months or even years.

When the bean seed receives water, oxygen, and the right temperature, it begins actively growing. It absorbs water, swells, and breaks open its seed coat. The young root emerges first, followed by a shoot that grows toward the light. Stored food inside the seed fuels this early development. Thus, being alive means retaining the ability to grow, while actively growing means using resources to produce new cells and structures.
