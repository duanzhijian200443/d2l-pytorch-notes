Reversible editing preserves the original image and records adjustments—such as crops, color changes, or filters—as separate, editable instructions. You can revisit, modify, or remove them later without degrading the source. Repeatedly overwriting the original permanently replaces pixels and may cause cumulative quality loss, especially with lossy formats like JPEG. It also makes mistakes difficult or impossible to undo after saving. Reversible workflows require extra storage or compatible software, but they offer far greater flexibility, safety, and control.
