In a compost pile, vegetable scraps are broken down by bacteria, fungi, worms, and other organisms. These decomposers use the scraps as food, changing their complex materials into simpler substances. As they work, some matter becomes gases such as carbon dioxide and water vapor, which enter the air. The remaining matter mixes with microbes, moisture, and other compost ingredients, forming dark, crumbly humus. The scraps have not vanished; their atoms have been rearranged and distributed among the compost, organisms, air, and water.
