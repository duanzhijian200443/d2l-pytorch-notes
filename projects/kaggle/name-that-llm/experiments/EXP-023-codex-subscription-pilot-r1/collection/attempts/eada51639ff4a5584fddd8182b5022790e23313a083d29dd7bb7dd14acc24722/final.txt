Final arrival times reveal only each person’s total travel time, not how their speed changed along the route. The steady walker may have moved at one constant pace from start to finish. The other walker could have walked quickly at first, stopped halfway for several minutes, and then increased speed enough to arrive at the same time—or even earlier or later.

For example, someone might pause but sprint afterward, making their moving speed higher than the steady walker’s, while their average speed including the pause is lower. Conversely, they might walk slowly before the pause and only moderately faster afterward. Without information about the pause length, distances covered at different times, and segment speeds, the final arrival time cannot establish who was faster throughout the journey.
