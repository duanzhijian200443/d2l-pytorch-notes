Think of an art museum as a place to explore, not a test you need to pass. You don’t have to understand a painting’s meaning to enjoy its colors, atmosphere, or details—or to decide it isn’t for you.

Start with a small goal: visit one gallery or spend about an hour wandering. Let your eyes choose where to stop rather than trying to see everything. When something catches your attention, give it a little time. Ask yourself: What did I notice first? How does this make me feel? Is there anything surprising or strange?

Read labels when you’re curious, not out of duty. A title or a few sentences can add something, but your own response matters too. If you’re visiting with someone, share impressions without worrying about sounding knowledgeable.

Take breaks, sit on a bench, and leave before you’re exhausted. Finding just one artwork you remember fondly can make the whole visit worthwhile. Curiosity is enough; expertise is optional.
