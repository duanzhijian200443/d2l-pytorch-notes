Two stars can look equally bright because apparent brightness depends on both their actual light output, called luminosity, and their distance from Earth. Light spreads out as it travels, so a star twice as far away appears only one-quarter as bright, assuming the same luminosity. A very luminous, distant star can therefore look just as bright as a much dimmer, nearby star. Dust between us and a star can also absorb or scatter its light, making it appear fainter.
