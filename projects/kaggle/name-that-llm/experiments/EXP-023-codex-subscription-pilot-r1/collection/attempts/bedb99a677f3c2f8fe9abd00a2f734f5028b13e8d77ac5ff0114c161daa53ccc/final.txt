The postcard arrived with the morning supply boat, addressed in blue ink:

**THE LIGHTHOUSE  
North Head**

Mara, the keeper, read it twice. The message said only: *Your lamp saved us. We are coming to thank you.*

No name. No return address.

She pinned the postcard beside the weather log and spent the day scanning the horizon. Near dusk, a fishing boat appeared, limping badly. Mara flashed the lamp’s emergency signal, guiding it toward the sheltered cove. The boat’s crew came ashore carrying a wooden box.

Inside was the lighthouse’s original brass lens, stolen decades earlier when the old station was abandoned. The captain explained that his grandfather had taken it as a souvenir. Recently, while sorting his estate, the family found the lens and learned its history. They had sailed through worsening weather to return it.

Mara placed the lens in the lantern room, though it was too small for the modern mechanism. She polished it until it caught the beam and scattered a bright star across the walls.

The next morning, she addressed a postcard to the fishing boat: *Thank you for finding your way back.*
