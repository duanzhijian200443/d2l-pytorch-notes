Let the original square have side length \(s\). Its perimeter is \(4s\), and its area is \(s^2\). After every side is doubled, the new side length is \(2s\). The new perimeter is

\[
4(2s)=8s,
\]

which is twice the original perimeter. The new area is

\[
(2s)^2=4s^2,
\]

which is four times the original area.

The changes differ because perimeter measures one-dimensional distance around the garden. Therefore, doubling each length doubles the total boundary length. Area, however, measures two-dimensional space. Both the length and the width are doubled, so the area is multiplied by \(2 \times 2=4\). Thus, doubling the dimensions doubles the perimeter but quadruples the area.
