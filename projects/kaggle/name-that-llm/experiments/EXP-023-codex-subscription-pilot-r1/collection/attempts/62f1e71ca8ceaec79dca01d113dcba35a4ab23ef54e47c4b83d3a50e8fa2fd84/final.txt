At the community garden, Maya frowned at a row of unfamiliar green shoots. The wooden label had vanished, leaving only a pale rectangle in the soil.

“Those are beans,” said a voice beyond the sunflowers.

Mr. Alvarez, who usually gardened alone, stepped closer. Maya shook her head. “Or peas.”

They made a wager: the winner would receive the first ripe tomato from the other’s plot. Over the next few weeks, they watered the mystery plants together, trading recipes and stories while watching tendrils climb a shared trellis.

At last, small purple blossoms appeared.

“Beans,” Maya admitted, presenting him with a tomato.

Mr. Alvarez split it in half. “A garden shouldn’t have only one winner.”

Neither ever replaced the missing label.
