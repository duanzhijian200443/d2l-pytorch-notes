A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading, while setting clear expectations about spoilers and discussion depth. Requiring everyone to finish can improve discussion quality: members can analyze the full arc, compare early impressions with the ending, and avoid conversations limited to the opening chapters. It also encourages commitment and gives the group a shared foundation.

However, a strict completion rule can exclude busy readers, people who disliked the book, or participants who stopped because of difficult or upsetting content. Their reasons for quitting may themselves offer valuable criticism about pacing, style, accessibility, or representation.

A balanced approach works best. Invite everyone, but announce that the discussion will cover the entire book and include spoilers. Begin with a short section where all participants can share initial reactions, then move to themes, character development, and the ending. Those who did not finish can listen, explain why they stopped, or leave before spoiler-heavy discussion. This preserves rigorous conversation without turning reading completion into a barrier to community.
