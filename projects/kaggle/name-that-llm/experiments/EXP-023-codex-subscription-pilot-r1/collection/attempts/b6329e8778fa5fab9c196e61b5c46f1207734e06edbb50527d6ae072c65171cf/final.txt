Preserving an old building exactly as it was protects its historical character, original materials, and cultural authenticity. However, strict preservation may leave it impractical, inaccessible, or vulnerable to decay. Adapting it for a new public use can give the building renewed purpose, improve safety and accessibility, and ensure its survival through continued maintenance. The challenge is balancing change with respect: significant architectural features should remain intact, while carefully designed modern additions provide necessary services without erasing the building’s identity.
