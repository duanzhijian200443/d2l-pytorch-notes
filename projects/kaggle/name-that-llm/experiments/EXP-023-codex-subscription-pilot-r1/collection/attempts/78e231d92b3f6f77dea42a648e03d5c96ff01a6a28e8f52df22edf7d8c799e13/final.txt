A checksum is like a number calculated from a parcel’s contents before shipping and written on its label. After delivery, the recipient calculates the number again. If the numbers differ, something changed along the way—perhaps an item was damaged or lost. If they match, the contents are likely unchanged. However, different contents can sometimes produce the same number, and a dishonest sender could replace both the contents and label. So a checksum alone does not guarantee authenticity or detect every possible change.
