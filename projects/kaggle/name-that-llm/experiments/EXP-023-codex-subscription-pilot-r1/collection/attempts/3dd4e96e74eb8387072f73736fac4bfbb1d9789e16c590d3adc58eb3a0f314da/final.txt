Before abandoning reusable packaging, simplify the return journey for both customers and staff:

- **Use one clear return point:** Place a visible, labeled bin near the entrance or checkout rather than requiring customers to ask staff.
- **Make returns “scan-and-drop”:** Add a barcode/QR code to each container. Staff scan it once, and the customer’s deposit or reward is automatically processed.
- **Reduce staff decisions:** Create a simple rule: accept any reasonably empty container, then sort and inspect them later in batches.
- **Provide a one-page cheat sheet:** Use pictures showing accepted packaging, where it goes, and what staff should say. Keep it at each register.
- **Train through short practice sessions:** Run five-minute demonstrations during shifts, including common edge cases.
- **Test a small pilot:** Try the revised process in one location or for one product line for several weeks before expanding.
- **Collect quick feedback:** Ask staff which step causes delays and track return time, errors, customer questions, and participation rates.

The concept may be sound; the friction may simply come from an overly complicated operational design. A streamlined pilot can distinguish whether reusable packaging lacks demand or whether the process needs better tools, clearer ownership, and simpler instructions.
