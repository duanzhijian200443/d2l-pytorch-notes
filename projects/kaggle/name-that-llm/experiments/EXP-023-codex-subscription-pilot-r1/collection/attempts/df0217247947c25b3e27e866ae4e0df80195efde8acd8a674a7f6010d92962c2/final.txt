The postcard was addressed simply:

**THE LIGHTHOUSE  
BLACKWATER POINT**

On the back, beneath a picture of a sunny harbor, someone had written: *Please turn the light off at midnight on Thursday. Ten minutes only. Trust me.*

Mara, the keeper, did not trust anyone who mailed anonymous instructions. She called the coast guard, then studied the shipping schedule. At midnight Thursday, a freighter carrying museum artifacts was due to pass the point.

The coast guard sent two officers and quietly positioned a patrol boat beyond the rocks. Mara kept the lamp burning.

At twelve precisely, a speedboat appeared offshore. It flashed a signal toward the freighter, then veered sharply when the lighthouse beam revealed the patrol boat. The officers intercepted it before it reached open water.

Later, they learned the postcard writer had planned to fake a navigation failure, board the freighter during the confusion, and steal a crate of rare coins.

Mara pinned the postcard above her desk.

Under it she wrote: *Ten minutes is plenty of time to make a mistake.*
