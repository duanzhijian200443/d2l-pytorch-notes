A file’s **name** is the complete label assigned to it, such as `report.pdf`. It may include any characters allowed by the operating system and helps users identify the file. An **extension** is the suffix after the final period, such as `.pdf`; it is mainly a convention that suggests which application should open the file. The **actual internal format** is how the file’s data is structured and encoded inside. For example, a file named `report.pdf` should contain data following the PDF specification, including its characteristic headers and objects. However, names and extensions can be changed easily without changing the contents. Renaming a JPEG image to `photo.txt` does not turn it into text; its internal format remains JPEG, and programs may fail to open it correctly.
