Restoring missing parts of an ancient object aims to recover its original appearance or function. Conservators may reconstruct a broken handle, fill cracks, or replace lost decoration, helping viewers imagine the object as a complete whole. However, restoration involves interpretation and can blur the distinction between surviving evidence and modern additions.

Displaying gaps openly treats damage and absence as part of the object’s history. Rather than concealing losses, this approach reveals the effects of time, use, and discovery. It preserves visible evidence and acknowledges uncertainty, though viewers may find the original form harder to understand.

Both approaches can be responsible: the key is to protect surviving material, avoid misleading reconstruction, and clearly identify any modern intervention.
