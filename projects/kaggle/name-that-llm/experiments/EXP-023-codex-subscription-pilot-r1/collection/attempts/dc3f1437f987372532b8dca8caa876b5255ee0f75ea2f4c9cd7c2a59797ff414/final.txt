Standardizing units of measurement made trade between communities faster, fairer, and more reliable. Merchants could compare prices, weigh goods, measure land, and write contracts without repeatedly converting unfamiliar local units. Shared standards also reduced misunderstandings, fraud, and disputes, while helping governments collect taxes and regulate markets. As trade networks expanded, common measures allowed goods to move more easily across regions.

However, replacing local measures could also erase valuable cultural knowledge. Traditional units often reflected everyday life, local environments, crafts, farming practices, or the human body. Their names and uses carried stories about how communities understood distance, weight, and value. When these measures disappeared, communities sometimes lost distinctive vocabulary, customs, and a degree of local control to distant governments or commercial authorities.
