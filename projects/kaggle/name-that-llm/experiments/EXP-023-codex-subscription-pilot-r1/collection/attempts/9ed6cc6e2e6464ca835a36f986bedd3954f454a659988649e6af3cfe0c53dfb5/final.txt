A sealed bag of chips can puff up in the mountains because the air inside the bag is at a higher pressure than the surrounding air. At lower elevations, atmospheric pressure is greater, so it compresses the bag and helps keep it relatively flat. As the bag is carried upward, the outside atmospheric pressure decreases. The air trapped inside then expands, pushing the flexible packaging outward until the pressures become more nearly balanced.

No additional air is needed: the same amount of gas simply occupies a larger volume. This follows Boyle’s law, which states that, at roughly constant temperature, a gas expands when the pressure around it decreases. Temperature can contribute as well. If the bag becomes warmer during the trip, the gas molecules move faster and exert more pressure, causing additional swelling. The bag may eventually burst if the internal pressure and stretched packaging exceed the bag’s strength. At lower altitude, it will usually shrink again as outside pressure increases.
