Standardizing units of measurement made trade between communities easier and fairer. Merchants could price goods, compare quantities, calculate taxes, and settle contracts without arguing over different local definitions of a “pound,” “bushel,” or “mile.” Shared measurements also encouraged wider markets because buyers and sellers could trust that the amount promised was the amount delivered. However, replacing local measures could erase important cultural knowledge and traditions. Local units often reflected regional environments, crafts, and ways of life; they might be closely tied to farming practices, architecture, or community identity. Their disappearance could weaken local autonomy and language, while favoring governments or powerful commercial centers that imposed the new standards. Standardization improved efficiency, but it could also make diverse communities more culturally uniform.
