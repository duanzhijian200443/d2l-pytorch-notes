A public library should keep a few worn but beloved books while also adding newer titles. Well-used books often have lasting value: they may be classics, local favorites, or stories that readers return to for comfort and discovery. Their condition can even show how meaningful they have been to a community. However, libraries must remain welcoming and relevant to changing audiences. Newer titles can reflect current interests, cultures, and ideas, helping attract readers who might not find themselves in older collections. The best approach is balance. Replace books only when they are too damaged to use or no longer meet community needs, and preserve popular older works through repair or fresh copies.
