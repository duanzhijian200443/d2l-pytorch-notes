Mara returned the borrowed ladder on a Thursday, carrying it through the rain like an awkward silver canoe.

Mr. Bell opened his garage before she knocked.

“Roof fixed?” he asked.

“Mostly.”

He nodded toward the house next door, hers, where one upstairs window still wore cardboard.

“My wife used to handle repairs,” Mara said.

“I remember.”

Neither moved. Rain ticked against the ladder.

Inside his garage stood a table set for two, beside a chessboard.

“Birthday?” she asked.

“No. Habit.” He folded one chair, then stopped. “I make too much soup on Thursdays.”

Mara looked back at her dark kitchen. For months, people had offered help, and she had mistaken refusing it for strength.

“Would you join me?”

“Yes,” she said. “I’d like that.”
