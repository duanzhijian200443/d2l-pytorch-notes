A museum’s choice of objects strongly influences how visitors imagine everyday life in the past. Displaying cooking pots, worn shoes, children’s toys, tools, letters, and household furniture can show how ordinary people worked, ate, travelled, and spent time with family. These objects may make history feel personal and relatable, rather than focused only on famous leaders or major events. However, museum collections are never complete. If they mainly preserve expensive items or belongings from wealthy groups, visitors may wrongly assume that these represented everyone’s experience. Including objects from workers, women, children, migrants, and minority communities creates a fuller picture. Labels and arrangement also matter, because they explain whose stories are represented and which voices may be missing.
