A folktale can change through retelling because each storyteller adapts it to fit a new audience, place, or time. Details such as characters’ names, settings, language, humor, and even the ending may shift. A village might replace a forest with a city, or add local customs and beliefs. Yet the story remains recognizable because its central structure survives: the main characters face a familiar problem, experience key events, and reach a meaningful outcome. Repeated motifs, such as a clever youngest child, a dangerous bargain, or a lesson about kindness, also preserve its identity. In this way, a folktale is flexible in its details but stable in its core pattern, allowing different communities to make the story their own while sharing the same narrative.
