A public library should keep a few worn but beloved books while also adding newer titles. Well-loved books show what the community values, and their popularity may make them more meaningful than their condition suggests. They can also connect generations, preserve local reading traditions, and provide reliable access to classics that readers request repeatedly. However, libraries have limited space and budgets, so damaged or outdated copies should be evaluated carefully. Replacing a book makes sense when it is unsafe, inaccurate, or no longer usable, but popularity should guide the decision too. The best approach is balance: repair or replace heavily borrowed favorites, retain significant older works, and reserve some space for diverse new voices. In this way, the collection respects its history while continuing to attract new readers.
