A puddle can disappear through **evaporation**, which does not require boiling. Even on a cool day, some water molecules at the surface happen to have enough energy to escape into the air as water vapor. Boiling is different: it occurs when vapor bubbles can form throughout the liquid, requiring much more energy and a temperature near water’s boiling point.

Evaporation is faster when the air is dry, windy, or sunny. Dry air can accept more water vapor; wind carries humid air away from the puddle’s surface; and sunlight adds energy. As higher-energy molecules escape, the remaining water can even cool slightly. Over time, enough molecules leave that the puddle vanishes, even though the water itself never became hot.
