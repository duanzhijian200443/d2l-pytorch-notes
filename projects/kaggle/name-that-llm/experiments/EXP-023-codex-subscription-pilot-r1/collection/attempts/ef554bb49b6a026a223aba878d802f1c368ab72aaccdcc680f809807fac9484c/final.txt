Historians distinguish them because they were created under different conditions. A contemporary diary records impressions close to the events, preserving details and immediate emotions, but it may reflect limited knowledge, bias, or later alteration. A memoir written decades later benefits from hindsight and broader context, yet memory can fade, reshape experiences, or absorb later interpretations. Neither is automatically truthful: historians assess authorship, purpose, audience, consistency, corroboration, and what each source could realistically have known.
