I belong to no one, though everyone calls me theirs.

Mrs. Lin first carried me into the rain, holding me low against the wind. At Mr. Ortiz’s door, she shook the silver drops from my ribs and said, “Keep it until tomorrow.”

Tomorrow became Tuesday. Mr. Ortiz took me to the corner shop, where he lent me to Samira, whose groceries were dissolving through a paper bag. She walked beneath me carefully, whispering thanks as though I had chosen her.

Her son borrowed me next. He spun me upside down in the courtyard, pretending I was a boat. A gust nearly made me one. I landed on the balcony of the upstairs twins, who used me to shelter a stray cat.

By Friday, the cat was dry, the twins were late, and I was passed to old Mr. Vale. He leaned on my handle when the sky cleared. “You’ve traveled,” he told me.

That evening, he left me outside Mrs. Lin’s door.

She opened it and laughed. My blue cloth was patched with tape, one spoke bent, my handle tied with red string. I was not the umbrella she had lent.

Then thunder rolled. Across the hall, someone knocked.

Mrs. Lin lifted me gently and said, “Of course. Just bring it back.”

We both knew I would return changed.
