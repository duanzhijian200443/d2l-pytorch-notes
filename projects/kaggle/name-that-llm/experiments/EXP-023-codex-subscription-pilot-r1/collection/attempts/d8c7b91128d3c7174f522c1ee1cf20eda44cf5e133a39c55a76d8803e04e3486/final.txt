Every October, Bellweather sold its lost things in the town hall: umbrellas, earrings, bicycles whose owners had apparently walked home.

But nobody would sell the red mitten.

It had appeared thirty years earlier, small enough for a toddler, with a crooked yellow star stitched into its palm. Each year, someone placed it beside the cashbox. Each year, someone said, “Better give them another winter.”

Mayors changed. The hall got new windows. The mitten stayed.

Then a gray-haired woman stopped at the table.

“My mother made that,” she whispered. “I thought I’d dreamed it.”

The treasurer pushed it toward her.

“How much?”

From three tables away, the retired postman answered.

“That one,” he said, “was never for sale.”
