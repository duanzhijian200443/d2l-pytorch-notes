A password manager stores and generates unique, strong passwords for different websites and apps. Instead of memorizing dozens of credentials, the user typically needs to remember only one strong master password. The manager can fill login forms automatically, reducing reuse of weak passwords and making it practical to use long, random passwords for every account. Many also synchronize encrypted vaults across devices and can store passkeys, recovery codes, and secure notes.

However, responsibility does not disappear. The user must create and protect a strong, unique master password, enable multi-factor authentication where available, and keep recovery information secure. They should remain alert to phishing: auto-fill should occur only on the legitimate site, not a convincing imitation. Users also need to secure their devices, keep the password manager updated, review account-security alerts, and ensure trusted emergency access or recovery arrangements are in place.
