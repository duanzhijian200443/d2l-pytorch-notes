Each autumn, Bellwether held its lost-and-found sale in the firehouse lot: umbrellas, single gloves, dented lunchboxes, and bicycles whose owners had vanished years ago.

This year, the strangest item sat beneath a handwritten sign: **DO NOT PRICE.**

It was a small red mitten, faded at the thumb.

“No one wants it?” asked Mara, who ran the sale.

“No,” said old Mr. Vale. “Everyone wants it. That’s the trouble.”

The mitten had belonged to his daughter, found after the great snowstorm forty years before. Every year, someone tried to buy it for sentiment, luck, or its bright color. Every year, Mr. Vale refused.

At sunset, Mara pinned it back on the board. The next morning, it would be waiting—lost, found, and still loved.
