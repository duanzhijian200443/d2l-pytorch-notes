A city walking tour should balance celebrated landmarks with overlooked everyday places. Famous sites provide historical context, visual impact, and a shared sense of identity, especially for first-time visitors. Yet ordinary markets, apartment courtyards, neighborhood cafés, and transit stops reveal how residents actually experience the city. Focusing only on landmarks can make a tour feel predictable and polished; focusing only on hidden corners may obscure broader significance. The strongest tour connects both, showing how grand history and daily life coexist in the same streets.
