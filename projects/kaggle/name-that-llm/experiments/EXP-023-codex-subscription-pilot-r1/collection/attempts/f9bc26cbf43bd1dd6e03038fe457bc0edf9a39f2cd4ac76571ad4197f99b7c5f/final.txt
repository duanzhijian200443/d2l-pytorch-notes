A seed can be alive without actively growing. For example, a bean seed may remain dry and dormant in a packet for months or even years. Its embryo is still living, but its growth is paused because it lacks enough water and suitable conditions. When the bean seed is planted and receives moisture, warmth, and oxygen, it absorbs water, swells, and begins germinating. The seed coat splits, a root emerges, and the shoot starts developing. At this stage, the seed is actively growing because its cells are dividing, using stored food, and producing new structures. Thus, being alive means the seed remains viable and capable of development; actively growing means it has begun using energy and changing visibly into a seedling.
