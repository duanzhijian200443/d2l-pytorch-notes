Imagine a population of snails with both light and dark shells living on dark soil. Birds can spot light-shelled snails more easily, so dark-shelled snails are more likely to survive and reproduce. If shell color is inherited, their offspring are more likely to have dark shells too.

Over many generations, this difference in reproductive success can make dark shells more common and light shells less common. No snail chooses to change its color or decides to evolve. Instead, natural selection acts on variations already present in the population. Mutations can introduce new inherited variations, but they do not appear because snails need them. Evolution happens as the frequencies of inherited traits change across generations, not because individual animals deliberately transform.
