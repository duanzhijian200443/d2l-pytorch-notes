Use a **“procedure card” system** instead of a traditional manual.

Create one short card for each recurring task, stored where the team already works—such as Google Drive, Notion, or a shared folder. Each card should include:

- **Task:** What needs to be done?
- **When:** What triggers it and how often?
- **Owner:** Who is responsible?
- **Steps:** The minimum sequence, ideally five to ten bullets.
- **Definition of done:** How do we know it was completed correctly?
- **Exceptions:** What common problems require judgment?
- **Last reviewed:** Date and reviewer.

Keep cards practical: screenshots, links, checklists, and examples are usually more useful than paragraphs. Have the person performing the task draft the first version, then ask a colleague to follow it. Any confusion reveals what to improve.

To prevent outdated documentation, make review part of the workflow. The owner updates the card whenever the process changes, and a manager reviews only the most important cards quarterly. Add a simple “Suggest an update” link or comment option so employees can flag issues immediately.

Start with the five procedures that cause the most errors, consume the most time, or depend heavily on one person. This creates a useful, living reference without requiring anyone to maintain a massive manual.
