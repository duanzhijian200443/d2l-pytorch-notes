Choose a calm moment—not when either of you is waiting—and frame it as a coordination issue rather than a character flaw. You might say:

“I’ve noticed you often arrive about twenty minutes early, while I usually aim to arrive at the agreed time. I appreciate that you’re dependable, but I worry you may feel frustrated waiting for me. Could we talk about what timing feels comfortable for both of us?”

Listen for the reason behind their early arrival: they may dislike rushing, need a buffer, or interpret the meeting time differently. Then explain your perspective without defensiveness: “For me, arriving on time means getting there close to the scheduled time, and arriving much earlier is difficult or unnecessary.”

Suggest a concrete compromise. Perhaps agree that the stated time is when you will meet, and that arriving early is fine but not something they should expect you to match. Or choose a “ready by” window, such as arriving between 6:00 and 6:10. Reassure them that punctuality matters to you, while acknowledging that you manage time differently.
