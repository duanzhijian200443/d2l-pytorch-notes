Fresh snow makes an outdoor space seem quieter because its soft, porous structure contains many tiny air pockets. When sound waves reach the snow, some of their energy enters these spaces and is dissipated through friction and repeated reflections rather than bouncing back. A fresh snow layer also covers hard surfaces such as pavement, roofs, and frozen ground, reducing the echoes and high-frequency reflections that normally contribute to ambient noise.

After snow becomes compacted, partially melted, or icy, its structure changes. The air pockets collapse, and the surface becomes denser, smoother, and harder. Instead of absorbing sound effectively, icy snow reflects more of it, much like bare pavement or concrete. As a result, sounds may seem sharper, carry farther, and produce more noticeable echoes.
