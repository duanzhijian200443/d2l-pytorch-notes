A speed test mainly measures how quickly your connection can transfer data under ideal, short-lived conditions—often to a nearby server. Video calls depend on more than raw download speed.

Common causes of freezing include:

- **Packet loss:** Small amounts of lost data can disrupt real-time audio/video, even if bandwidth is high.
- **Latency and jitter:** Latency is delay; jitter is inconsistency in that delay. Video calls need packets to arrive steadily and in order. Unstable timing can cause freezes.
- **Upload limitations:** Calls send your camera and microphone upstream. A fast download result may hide weak or congested upload capacity.
- **Wi‑Fi interference:** Distance, walls, crowded wireless channels, Bluetooth devices, or an overloaded router can make Wi‑Fi unreliable.
- **Network congestion:** Other devices may be streaming, gaming, backing up files, or downloading updates. Your ISP may also be congested at peak times.
- **Device performance:** High CPU use, low memory, overheating, background apps, or an outdated browser can prevent smooth video decoding and encoding.
- **Call-service issues:** The conferencing provider’s servers, the other participant’s connection, or routing between networks may be the bottleneck.

A useful test is to check latency, jitter, packet loss, and upload speed while the problem occurs. Switching to wired Ethernet, moving closer to the router, pausing background traffic, and restarting or updating the router/device often helps.
