Storage space and working memory are different resources. A computer may have lots of disk or SSD capacity for saved files, yet still have limited RAM—the fast memory used by applications while they run. Large apps consume RAM for code, documents, images, caches, and background processes. When RAM fills up, the operating system moves some data to storage as “virtual memory” or swap space. Even fast SSDs are much slower than RAM, so frequent swapping can make the computer lag, freeze briefly, or respond slowly. CPU limits, graphics-memory demands, overheating, and background services can also contribute. Therefore, free storage alone does not guarantee smooth multitasking; sufficient RAM and processing capacity matter most.
