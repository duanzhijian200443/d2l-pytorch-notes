It’s common to remember someone’s story but lose track of their name—names are often less meaningful to the brain than personal details. A few simple habits can help:

When you meet someone, repeat their name naturally: “Nice to meet you, Maya.” Try to connect it with something distinctive, such as “Maya from the community garden.” Use the name again before the conversation ends: “I enjoyed talking with you, Maya.”

Focus closely during introductions, and avoid immediately planning what you’ll say next. If you miss the name, ask again right away: “I’m sorry—I didn’t catch your name.” Most people appreciate the effort. If you recognize the person but forget later, be honest and warm: “I remember our conversation about your travels, but could you remind me of your name?”

After the event, jot down a few notes on your phone—name, face, and one memorable detail. Review them before the next gathering. You can also ask a mutual acquaintance or check an event directory when appropriate, while respecting privacy.

Most importantly, don’t treat forgetting names as a personal failure. Showing genuine interest, remembering people’s experiences, and greeting them warmly often matters more than perfect recall.
