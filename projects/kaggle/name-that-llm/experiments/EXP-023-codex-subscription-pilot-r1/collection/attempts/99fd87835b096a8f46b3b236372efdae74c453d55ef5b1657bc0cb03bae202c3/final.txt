A short restaurant menu can be more helpful than a large one because it reduces decision fatigue and often suggests that the kitchen focuses on a few well-prepared dishes. Customers who value speed, simplicity, and quality may appreciate being able to choose quickly. A limited menu can also make ingredients fresher and staff recommendations more reliable.

However, not every customer has the same expectations. Families, large groups, vegetarians, and people with allergies may prefer a wider selection so that everyone can find something suitable. Some diners also enjoy exploring many cuisines or comparing several options before ordering. Therefore, the ideal menu is not necessarily the shortest or largest. It should offer clear, varied choices without becoming overwhelming, while matching the restaurant’s style and its customers’ needs.
