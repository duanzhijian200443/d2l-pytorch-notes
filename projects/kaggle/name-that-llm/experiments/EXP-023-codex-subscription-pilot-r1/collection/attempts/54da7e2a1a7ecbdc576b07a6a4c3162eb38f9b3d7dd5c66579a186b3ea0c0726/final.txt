Try the **“minimum viable reset”** approach: define success as making a small, visible improvement—not finishing the room.

Before starting, choose one task that fits in ten minutes and has a firm boundary: collect dishes, clear one countertop, fold laundry from one basket, or wipe the bathroom sink. Set a timer for ten minutes. Your only commitment is to work until it rings; you are explicitly *not* allowed to expand the job into organizing drawers, deep-cleaning, or redesigning the space.

It may help to say: “I’m not cleaning the kitchen. I’m resetting this counter.”

Make the next action extremely concrete. Instead of “tidy the bedroom,” write or say, “Put five items into their homes,” or “Fill one trash bag.” If you notice the urge to do everything perfectly, treat it as a cue to shrink the task further: one shelf, one surface, ten objects.

When the timer ends, stop and acknowledge what changed. You can continue only after a brief pause and a deliberate choice—not because perfection pulled you in.

Over time, this teaches your brain that starting does not create an overwhelming obligation. Ten imperfect minutes count, and repeated small resets usually make whole-room cleaning easier anyway.
