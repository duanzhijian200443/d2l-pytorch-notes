Run a **six-week “Discovery Shelf” pilot** in one section—such as contemporary fiction—without changing the store’s overall organization.

Select 20–30 books by lesser-known authors, using staff recommendations, strong reviews, and read-alikes for popular writers. Keep each book in its normal alphabetical or genre location, but place a second copy on a small, clearly labeled display near the section entrance. Organize the display by familiar reader interests—“For fans of literary mysteries,” “If you like intimate family stories,” or “Fast-paced speculative fiction”—rather than creating a new classification system.

Give each featured book a short shelf card explaining its appeal and naming one or two better-known comparison authors. Rotate half the titles every two weeks so the display remains fresh while retaining enough consistency to measure results.

Use a simple test: compare featured titles’ sales and browsing activity with their performance during the previous six weeks, or with similar unfeatured titles. Add a QR code linking to a one-question survey: “Did this display help you consider an author you didn’t know?” Staff can also tally customer comments and requests.

Define success in advance—for example, a 20% increase in sales of featured authors, positive survey responses from most participants, and no rise in questions about where sections are located. If successful, repeat the approach in another genre before expanding storewide.
