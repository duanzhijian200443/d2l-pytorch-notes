Every clock in Mara’s shop left ticking precisely—except the brass carriage clock by the window. It lost three minutes each day. Its owner, Mr. Vale, had brought it in after his wife died, explaining that its chime had always called her downstairs for evening tea.

Mara could have corrected the worn balance spring. Instead, she cleaned it and left it slow. Mr. Vale came home later now, lingering in the empty streets. Those three minutes meant the clock still welcomed him before it chimed.
