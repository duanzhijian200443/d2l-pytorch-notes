Mara had once abandoned a grocery cart because the woman ahead of her wanted to pay in coins. She watched kettles. She pressed elevator buttons already lit.

Now she sat on the kitchen floor with a piece of chicken cooling in her palm.

Under the refrigerator, two green eyes remained fixed on her.

“All right,” she said.

The shelter woman had warned her: no reaching, no dragging him out. He might hide for days.

Mara’s phone buzzed against the tiles. She turned it facedown.

Outside, a truck reversed with three sharp beeps. The eyes disappeared. Mara lowered the chicken to the floor, halfway between her bare foot and the refrigerator.

She noticed a cupboard hinge she had meant to fix. A pale square where the previous owners’ clock had hung. Her leg went numb.

After a while, a gray paw appeared, then withdrew.

Mara stayed where she was.

“You don’t have to,” she told him, and moved her foot a little farther away.
