I began with Mrs. Vale, who bought me during a rainstorm and never learned to trust weather reports. I was navy blue then, proud of my curved handle and twelve perfect ribs.

But umbrellas, I discovered, are rarely owned. We are borrowed.

First came Leo from 3B, late for an interview. He returned me to the wrong door, where Mr. Singh found me dripping against his mat. Mr. Singh carried me to the grocer’s, then lent me to Ana, who was balancing a sleeping baby and a bag of oranges.

Ana left me in the school office. The janitor recognized Mrs. Vale’s initials, though he could not remember which building she lived in. So I waited beneath a radiator, listening to children stamp puddles from their boots.

Each journey changed me. One rib bent during a windy dash to the pharmacy. My handle acquired a strip of red tape. Someone tucked a bus ticket into my folds; someone else tied a blue ribbon around my wrist.

Years passed. I knew every staircase, every leaking gutter, every neighbor’s hurried apologies.

One evening, Mrs. Vale opened her door and found me there again, folded neatly in a bucket.

“Oh,” she said, smiling as if I had merely gone for bread.

Outside, rain began. She took me in, and I dreamed of the next knock.
