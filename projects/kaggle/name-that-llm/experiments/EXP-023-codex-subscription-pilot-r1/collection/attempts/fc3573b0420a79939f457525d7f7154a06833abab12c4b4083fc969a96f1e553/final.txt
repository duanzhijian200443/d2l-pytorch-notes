Historians distinguish contemporary diaries from later memoirs because when a source was written shapes its evidence. A diary records impressions close to events, preserving uncertainty and details later forgotten, but may contain rumors, misunderstandings, or deliberate self-presentation. A memoir benefits from hindsight and broader context, yet memory can fade or be reshaped by later experiences and reputational concerns. Neither is automatically truthful: historians assess authorship, purpose, audience, and circumstances, then compare each account with independent evidence.
