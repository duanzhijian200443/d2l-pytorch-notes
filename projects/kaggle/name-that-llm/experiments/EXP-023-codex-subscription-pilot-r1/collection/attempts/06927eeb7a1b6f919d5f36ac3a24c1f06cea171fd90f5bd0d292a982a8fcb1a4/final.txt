Before judging reusable packaging a failure, treat staff confusion as a process-design problem. The idea may work well once returns are easier to handle.

Start by watching a few returns and asking staff where they get stuck: identifying eligible containers, issuing refunds, checking cleanliness, or deciding where returned items belong. Use those observations to remove unnecessary steps.

Create one standard return routine:
1. Check that the packaging is accepted.
2. Issue any deposit refund using a clearly labelled till button.
3. Place the container in a designated collection bin.

Give staff a one-page visual guide with photos of accepted packaging and short answers to common questions. Keep exception rules simple, and name someone staff can ask when an unusual case arises. Separate customer returns from cleaning and restocking so frontline staff do not have to manage everything at once.

Make the customer instructions equally clear. Put matching signs at checkout and the return point explaining what to bring back, where to go, and how refunds work.

Test the revised process in one shop or on selected shifts for two weeks. Offer a brief demonstration, collect staff feedback, and track return times, errors, and customer participation. Adjust the remaining sticking points before deciding whether the packaging scheme itself is viable.
