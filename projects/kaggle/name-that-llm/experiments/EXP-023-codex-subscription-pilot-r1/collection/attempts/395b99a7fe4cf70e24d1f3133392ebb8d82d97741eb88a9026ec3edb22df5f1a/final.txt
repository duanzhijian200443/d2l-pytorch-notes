Backing up files creates a separate, recoverable copy of your data, often preserving older versions or deleted items. Its purpose is protection against accidental deletion, corruption, theft, hardware failure, or ransomware. Synchronizing files, by contrast, keeps selected folders consistent across devices. When a file changes or is deleted on one device, that change usually propagates to the others.

Confusing synchronization with backup can cause serious trouble. Imagine storing family photos in a synced folder and assuming they are safely backed up. If you accidentally delete the folder on your laptop, the synchronization service may delete it from your phone and cloud storage too. Without version history or an independent backup, the photos could be permanently lost. Ideally, use synchronization for convenient access and backups for recovery.
