A written definition of “finished” gives everyone the same, explicit standard for completing work. In a small collaborative project, people may otherwise interpret completion differently: one person might mean the feature works locally, while another expects testing, documentation, review, and deployment. Recording the criteria removes guesswork and makes handoffs clearer.

The definition can specify required outputs, quality checks, approvals, and known limitations. Team members can use it to estimate effort, track progress, and decide objectively whether a task can be closed. It also prevents awkward disputes by shifting discussion from personal expectations to agreed criteria. When requirements change, the team can update the definition together. Even a short checklist—implemented, tested, reviewed, documented, and accepted—helps ensure that nobody celebrates completion while someone else is still waiting for essential work.
