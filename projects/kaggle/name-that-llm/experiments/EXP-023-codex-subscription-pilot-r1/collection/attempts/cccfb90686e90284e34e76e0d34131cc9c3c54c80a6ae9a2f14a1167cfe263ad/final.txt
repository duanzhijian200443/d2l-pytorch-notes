Final arrival times tell us how long each friend took overall, but not how their speed changed along the route. The steady walker maintains one speed, while the other’s journey includes walking, a pause, and faster walking afterward. The pause adds time without adding distance, so the second friend might walk faster while moving yet still arrive later. Alternatively, speeding up enough after the pause could let them arrive together or even finish first. For the same route, arrival times can establish overall average speeds if both friends started together, but they cannot reveal speeds at every moment. To know who was faster during particular stretches—or whether either was faster throughout—we need more information, such as their positions at different times.
