Borrowing rarely used household tools is often more economical than owning them. Beyond avoiding the purchase price, borrowers save storage space, maintenance time, repair costs, and the hassle of disposing of outdated or broken equipment. Borrowing can also provide access to higher-quality, specialized tools through neighbors, libraries, rental shops, or community tool banks.

However, borrowing has drawbacks: the tool may be unavailable when needed, pickup and return take time, rental fees can accumulate, and users may be liable for damage. Familiarity and convenience also favor ownership, especially for emergencies or recurring projects.

A practical rule is to borrow bulky, specialized, or expensive tools used once or twice a year, while owning inexpensive, versatile tools that are needed frequently, urgently, or require careful customization and upkeep.
