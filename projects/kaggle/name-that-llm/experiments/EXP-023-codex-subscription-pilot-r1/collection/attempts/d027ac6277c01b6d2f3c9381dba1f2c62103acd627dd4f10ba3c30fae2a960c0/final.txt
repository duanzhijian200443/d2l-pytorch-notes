Preserving an old building exactly as it was protects its historical character, craftsmanship, and authenticity, allowing visitors to experience the past. However, maintenance can be costly, and the building may serve limited practical purposes. Adapting it for a new public use, such as a library or community centre, keeps it relevant and accessible while potentially funding its upkeep. Yet alterations may erase important historical features. The best approach often balances conservation with carefully designed changes that respect the building’s identity.
