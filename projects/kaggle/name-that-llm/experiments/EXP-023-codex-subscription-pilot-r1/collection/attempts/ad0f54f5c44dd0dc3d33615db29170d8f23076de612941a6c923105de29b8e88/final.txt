Closure counts measure throughput, not whether customers received effective, lasting help. A team can raise its numbers by quickly closing simple tickets, splitting one issue into several tasks, or marking cases resolved before the customer confirms success. Complex, high-impact problems often take longer and may make a strong team appear less productive than one focused on easy work.

The metric also ignores quality dimensions such as accuracy, empathy, response time, resolution time, repeat contacts, and customer effort. A prematurely closed ticket can reopen later, creating extra work and frustration that the original closure count hides. It can also incentivize agents to avoid difficult cases or transfer them elsewhere. A more balanced view combines closure volume with first-contact resolution, reopen rates, customer satisfaction, severity-adjusted outcomes, and quality reviews.
