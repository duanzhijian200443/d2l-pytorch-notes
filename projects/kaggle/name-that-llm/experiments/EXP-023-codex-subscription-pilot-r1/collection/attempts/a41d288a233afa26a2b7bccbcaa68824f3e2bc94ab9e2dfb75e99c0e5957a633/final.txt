The workshop had six benches, with tools hanging where everyone could reach them and scrap wood sorted into bins. Those details made it a practical place to learn: benches provided work surfaces, tools were accessible rather than tucked away, and leftover wood was organized rather than piled together. Learners could find a piece of scrap without digging through a heap and reach for a tool without asking someone else to retrieve it. The space supported the work through its arrangement—somewhere to work, tools within reach, and materials kept in order.
