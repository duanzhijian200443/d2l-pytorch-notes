Before dawn, Mara found the recipe beside a column of debts: *flour, water, salt; wait for the living thing to rise.* The book smelled of dust and old apples. She measured by spoonfuls, as the faded hand instructed, and kneaded until the dough warmed beneath her palms.

The bakery’s oven coughed smoke, then settled into a steady breath. Outside, carts began to rattle over wet stones. Inside, yeast gave off its sour, lonely smell, and Mara wondered whether she had spoiled everything.

But when she opened the oven, the loaves burst gold along their seams. The same sourness filled the room now—bright, tender, unmistakably alive. She laughed, tore off a heel, and wrote in the margin beneath the recipe: *Enough for morning.*
