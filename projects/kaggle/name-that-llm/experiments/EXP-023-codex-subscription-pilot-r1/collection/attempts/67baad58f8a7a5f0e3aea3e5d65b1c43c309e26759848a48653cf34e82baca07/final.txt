A school art exhibition can be organized either by age group or by mixing work from students of all ages. Both approaches have advantages.

Grouping artwork by age makes it easier for visitors to understand students’ development. They can see how artistic skills, ideas, and techniques become more sophisticated over time. It may also feel fairer to students, since younger children are not directly compared with older, more experienced artists. Teachers can use this arrangement to highlight the learning goals of each year group.

However, mixing all ages together can create a more exciting and inclusive exhibition. Artwork could be grouped by theme, color, material, or technique instead of age. This encourages visitors to focus on the ideas and emotions in each piece rather than the age of its creator. Younger students may feel proud to see their work displayed beside that of older students, while older students can appreciate the imagination and freedom often found in younger children’s art.

A mixed exhibition is probably the better choice if the aim is to celebrate creativity across the whole school. Labels can still include each artist’s age or year group, allowing visitors to notice differences in development. Ultimately, the exhibition should value every student’s work and make all participants feel equally included.
