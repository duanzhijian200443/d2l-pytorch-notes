Fresh snow makes an outdoor space quieter because it is a porous blanket filled with tiny air pockets. When sound waves enter those pores, air vibrates against the intricate surfaces of the ice crystals. Friction and heat exchange dissipate some of the sound’s energy as tiny amounts of heat, so less sound reflects back into the surroundings. Snow also covers hard, reflective ground, reducing echoes, especially at higher frequencies.

As snow ages, compacts, or melts and refreezes, its pores shrink or become sealed beneath an icy crust. The surface becomes harder and less absorbent, reflecting more sound rather than letting it penetrate. Consequently, the same place may sound less muffled, with footsteps, voices, and other noises carrying more clearly.
