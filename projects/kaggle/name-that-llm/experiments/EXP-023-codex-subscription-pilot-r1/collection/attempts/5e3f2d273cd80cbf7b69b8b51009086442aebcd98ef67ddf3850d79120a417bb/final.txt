On her first morning as the town’s baker, Mara found the recipe beside a column of unpaid debts: flour, water, salt, a spoon of honey. No name. Beneath it, in smaller writing: Give it longer than you think.

The kitchen smelled of scorched sugar. She blamed the old oven.

While the dough rose, she studied the accounts. Each winter, the same families appeared; each spring, their balances vanished beneath a single penciled line.

She let the loaves darken, though her hands kept reaching for the peel.

At opening, a boy asked what yesterday’s bread cost.

“Come back tomorrow,” she said, handing him a warm loaf.

Behind her, the scorched-sugar smell rose again: honey at the crust’s edge, almost burned, still sweet.
