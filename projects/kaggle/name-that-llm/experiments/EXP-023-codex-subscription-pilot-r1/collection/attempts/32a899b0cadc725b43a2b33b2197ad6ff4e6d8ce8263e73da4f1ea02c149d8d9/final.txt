Mara dropped the blue cup into the charity box.

Jonah snatched it out. “Not that.”

“It’s chipped.” She held out her hand. “And ugly.”

He tucked it behind his back, suddenly eight years old again. Dust striped his black sleeve.

Mara reached for the cup. Jonah twisted away, and it slipped. Both lunged. His palm hit the floor beneath it; the cup landed safely in his hand.

For a moment, neither moved.

Then Mara saw his thumb rubbing the faded soccer-ball sticker near the handle—the one she had peeled from a hospital vending-machine prize sheet.

She sat back on her heels.

Jonah wiped the cup with his shirt and set it carefully beside the photograph of their mother, bald and grinning between them.

Mara lifted the charity box.

“Kitchen?” she asked.

He nodded.
