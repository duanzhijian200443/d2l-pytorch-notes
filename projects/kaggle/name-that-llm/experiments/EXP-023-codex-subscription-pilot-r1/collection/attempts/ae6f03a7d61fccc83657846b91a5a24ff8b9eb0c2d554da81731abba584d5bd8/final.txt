Dividing a project by task count assumes that every task requires roughly the same amount of work. In practice, tasks can differ greatly in complexity, time, effort, and the expertise needed to complete them. Therefore, two teams finishing the same number of tasks may contribute very different amounts of labor.

For example, imagine a project with twenty tasks. One team completes ten routine updates, each taking an hour. The other completes ten difficult engineering tasks, each requiring research, careful design, and several hours of testing. Both teams have completed half the tasks, but the second team has carried a much heavier workload.

Harder tasks may also involve greater uncertainty, responsibility, and coordination, which task counts fail to capture. A fairer division would consider estimated effort, technical difficulty, dependencies, and available skills rather than simply counting assignments. Task count can measure how many items are finished, but it cannot, by itself, establish that work has been divided equally.
