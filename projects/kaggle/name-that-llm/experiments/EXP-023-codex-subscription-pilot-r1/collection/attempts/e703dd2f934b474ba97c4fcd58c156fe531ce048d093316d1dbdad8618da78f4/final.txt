The arrangement of a theatre’s audience strongly shapes the relationship between performers and spectators. In a thrust or arena stage, spectators surround the actors, creating intimacy, immediacy, and a sense of shared participation. A proscenium stage separates the audience from the performers, encouraging observation and illusion. In promenade theatre, spectators move through the performance space, becoming more active and sometimes part of the action. Thus, seating design can influence emotional closeness, audience involvement, performers’ awareness, and the overall atmosphere of the production.
