When Mara pulled back the curtains, she found not the promised city view but a brick wall, close enough to touch. Its red bricks were darkened by rain, and a thin green plant had rooted in the mortar. At first she laughed, then sighed, and set her suitcase on the bed.

While brushing her teeth, she noticed a pigeon perched on a ledge between the windows. It tilted its head as if inspecting the room. Later, sunlight moved slowly across the wall, warming one patch of brick to orange. Someone above shook out a towel; someone below hummed.

By evening, Mara had stopped looking for the city. The wall had become a quiet calendar of weather, footsteps, shadows, and small lives.
