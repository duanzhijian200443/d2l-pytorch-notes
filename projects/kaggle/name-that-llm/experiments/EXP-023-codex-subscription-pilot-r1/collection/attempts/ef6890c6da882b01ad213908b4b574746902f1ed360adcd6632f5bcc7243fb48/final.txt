Mara hated waiting. She drummed her fingers at red lights, refreshed tracking pages, and once abandoned a bakery line because the woman ahead was choosing between two muffins.

But tonight she sat on the porch without her phone.

The garden gate stood open. Beyond it, the road faded into dusk, empty except for moths circling the streetlamp. Her brother had promised to come when he could—not at seven, not after work, not by any sensible timetable. Just when the hospital let him go.

Mara placed two mugs on the step. Steam rose from one and vanished from the other.

A car passed. She looked up, then smiled at herself and settled back into the chair. The crickets began their uneven chorus. Somewhere in the dark, a dog barked twice.

Hours softened around her.

When headlights finally appeared, they moved slowly, cautiously, as if afraid of arriving too soon. Mara stood, but did not rush down the path. She waited until the car stopped, until the door opened, until her brother stepped out beneath the porch light.

Then she crossed the grass.
