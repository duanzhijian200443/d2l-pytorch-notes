Choose recipes around what you already have, rather than shopping for a single dish. Before planning meals, inventory your pantry, freezer, and fridge; then search for flexible recipes using key ingredients you need to finish.

Favor “component” ingredients—beans, grains, canned tomatoes, spices, eggs, seasonal vegetables—that work across many cuisines. Plan two or three meals that share perishables: herbs can become pesto, salad dressing, soup, or grain bowls; roasted vegetables can appear in tacos, pasta, and frittatas.

Try a rotating theme: one new recipe each week, balanced by familiar pantry meals. Buy unusual ingredients only when you can identify at least two additional uses, or split them with a friend. Finally, keep a small list of successful “use-it-up” recipes for leftovers and odds and ends.
