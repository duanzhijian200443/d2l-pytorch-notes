Satire that exposes a powerful institution directs laughter toward authority, revealing contradictions between its public ideals and actual behavior. A joke about a bank preaching responsibility while receiving a bailout, for example, makes hypocrisy visible and invites audiences to question who benefits from the system. Its force comes from a specific target and a critical insight.

Humor that merely repeats a familiar stereotype often substitutes recognition for discovery. Rather than exposing how power works, it treats a group’s supposedly shared traits as the punchline, reinforcing assumptions the audience already holds. Context matters: stereotypes can themselves be quoted or exaggerated to undermine prejudice. The key distinction is whether the joke challenges an established way of seeing or simply rewards its repetition.
