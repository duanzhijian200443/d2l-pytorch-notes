“Where’s the blue dot?” the teenager asked.

“There isn’t one,” said the retired mapmaker, spreading the paper across the picnic table.

“Then how do you know where you are?”

“You look around.”

She glanced at the river, the church spire, the road climbing behind them. He watched her turn the map until the shapes agreed.

“Oh,” she said. “I’m the blue dot.”

“More or less.”

She traced a thin red line with her finger. “Did you draw this?”

“Forty years ago.”

“This road’s gone now.”

“Yes. They flooded the valley.”

Her finger stopped over a tiny square beneath the reservoir’s pale blue ink.

“What was that?”

“My school.”

She studied the square, then carefully flattened a crease that ran through it.
