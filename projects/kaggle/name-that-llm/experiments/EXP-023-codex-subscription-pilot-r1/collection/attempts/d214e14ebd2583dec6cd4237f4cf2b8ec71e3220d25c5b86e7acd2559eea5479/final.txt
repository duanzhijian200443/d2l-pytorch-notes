The booking had promised a city view. Technically, the bricks belonged to the city.

Martin stood at the window with his suitcase still upright behind him. Across the narrow gap, a wall rose six stories, interrupted by one drainpipe and a weed growing from the mortar.

He photographed it for a complaint.

Then a sparrow landed on the drainpipe with a length of string. It tried to carry the string into a crack, failed, and tried again from an angle Martin considered plainly worse.

“Other end,” he said.

The bird ignored him. Eventually, it succeeded.

Martin unpacked. When reception called to ask whether everything was satisfactory, he glanced at the crack.

“The room’s fine,” he said. “Someone next door is still moving in.”
