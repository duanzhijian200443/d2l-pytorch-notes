A spinning ice skater turns faster by pulling their arms inward because their **angular momentum stays approximately constant**, as long as external twisting forces (torques) are negligible. Bringing their arms closer to the rotation axis reduces their **moment of inertia**, a measure of how mass is distributed around that axis. Their angular speed therefore increases to conserve angular momentum. Their rotational kinetic energy also increases: the extra energy comes from the work their muscles do while pulling their arms inward.
