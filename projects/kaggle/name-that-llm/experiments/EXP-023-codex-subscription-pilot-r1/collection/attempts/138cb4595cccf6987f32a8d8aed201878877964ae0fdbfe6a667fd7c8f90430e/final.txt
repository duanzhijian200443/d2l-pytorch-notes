A school art exhibition can be organised by age or by mixing students of different ages, and both approaches have advantages.

Grouping work by age makes the exhibition easier to understand. Visitors can see how artistic skills develop as children grow older, from simple drawings to more sophisticated techniques and ideas. It also allows teachers to compare work within similar age groups and recognise students’ progress fairly. Younger pupils may feel more confident when their work is displayed alongside that of classmates with similar abilities.

However, mixing all ages can create a more varied and exciting exhibition. It encourages students to appreciate different styles and ideas rather than focusing only on technical skill. Younger children may be inspired by older students, while older pupils may value the creativity and imagination often found in younger children’s work. A mixed display can also make the exhibition feel more inclusive and like a celebration of the whole school community.

The best choice may be a combination of both approaches. Work could be displayed in themed sections, such as nature, people, or imagination, while labels show each artist’s age. This would highlight individual development without separating students too strictly. Overall, the decision should depend on whether the main aim is to show artistic progress or to celebrate creativity across the entire school.
