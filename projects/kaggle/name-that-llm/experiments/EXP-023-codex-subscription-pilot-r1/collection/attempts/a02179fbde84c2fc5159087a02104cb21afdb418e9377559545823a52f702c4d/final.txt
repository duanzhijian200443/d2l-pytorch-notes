The orchestra was ready, the audience was quiet, and the conductor had raised his baton when the concert manager stopped him.

“One chair is missing.”

It seemed ridiculous. The musicians could easily share a little space, but the manager insisted they search. The absent chair belonged at the back of the second violin section, beside seventeen-year-old Lina, who had been nervous all afternoon.

At last, a stagehand found it in the loading corridor. Its seat was cracked, and beneath the cushion lay a small metal box.

Lina recognized it immediately. Her late grandfather, a retired violinist, had hidden the box there years ago. Inside was a letter addressed to her, explaining that the chair had once belonged to him and that he had donated it after surviving an accident that ended his career.

He had written, “If you ever sit here, play for the people who can no longer.”

Lina wiped her eyes. When the concert began, she played the opening passage alone—and beautifully. The missing chair had delayed the music, but it had also given her the reason to play.
