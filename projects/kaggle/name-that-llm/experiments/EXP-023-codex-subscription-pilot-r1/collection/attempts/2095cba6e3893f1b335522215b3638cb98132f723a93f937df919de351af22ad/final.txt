A geologist applies relative-dating principles to reconstruct the sequence. Sedimentary layers are usually deposited one atop another, so lower layers are generally older than layers above them (superposition). If the layers have been tilted or folded, that deformation occurred after deposition. A crack that cuts across several layers is younger than every layer it interrupts; this is the principle of cross-cutting relationships. If the crack is filled with a different rock, such as mineral-rich material or an igneous dike, the filling formed after the crack opened and is therefore also younger than the cut layers. Any faulting, erosion, or later fractures that offset or cut the filled crack must be younger still. Together, these relationships establish a relative timeline without requiring numerical ages.
