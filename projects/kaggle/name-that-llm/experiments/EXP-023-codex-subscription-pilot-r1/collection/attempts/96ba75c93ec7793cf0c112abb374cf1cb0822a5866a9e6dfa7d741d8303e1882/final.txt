Materials spring back or stay bent because their internal structure responds differently to force. Imagine bending a rubber band, a plastic ruler, and a paper clip.

In a rubber band, the long molecular chains stretch and uncoil when pulled. When released, they return to their original arrangement, so the band springs back. A plastic ruler behaves similarly during a gentle bend: its particles shift only slightly and then recover.

Every material has a limit to this recovery. If you bend the ruler too far, its internal structure changes permanently, leaving a crease or causing it to snap. A paper clip illustrates this well. A small bend may spring back, but a strong bend makes tiny layers inside the metal slide past one another. Once they settle into new positions, the clip stays bent.

Temperature and repeated use also matter. Warm plastic often bends more easily, while a paper clip repeatedly bent back and forth becomes weaker and eventually breaks. Thus, springiness depends on the material and how far, how often, and under what conditions it is bent.
