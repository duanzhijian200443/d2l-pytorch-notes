A shadow’s edge depends on the apparent size of the light source. A very small, nearly point-like source produces a sharp shadow because each point is either fully illuminated or fully blocked. An extended source produces both an **umbra**, where all parts of the source are blocked, and a **penumbra**, where only part is blocked.

Close to the object, the penumbra is narrow, so the shadow edge looks sharp. Farther from the object, light rays arriving from different parts of the source spread across a wider region, making the penumbra—and therefore the edge—fuzzier. Increasing the source’s size widens the penumbra and softens the shadow more strongly. Conversely, a smaller or more distant source has a smaller apparent size and creates sharper-edged shadows.
