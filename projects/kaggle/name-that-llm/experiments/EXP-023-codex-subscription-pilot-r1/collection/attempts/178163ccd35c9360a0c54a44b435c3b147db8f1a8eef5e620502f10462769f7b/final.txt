A soap bubble’s colors come from light reflecting off both its outer and inner surfaces. These two reflected rays travel slightly different distances before reaching your eyes. Depending on the bubble’s thickness, some colors’ light waves line up and become brighter, while others cancel each other out.

As the soap film slowly drains and evaporates, it becomes thinner in different places. That changes which colors are strengthened or canceled, so swirling bands of color shift across the surface. Near the end, parts of the film can become so thin that most visible light cancels out, making those areas look dark or black.
