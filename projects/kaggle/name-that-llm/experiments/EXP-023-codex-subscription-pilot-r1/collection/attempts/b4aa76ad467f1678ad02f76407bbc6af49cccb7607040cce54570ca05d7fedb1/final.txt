Use a simple prioritization process rather than choosing the loudest request.

1. **Agree on the main goal:** more volunteer sign-ups, donations, or easier access to services.
2. **Score all ten improvements** on expected benefit, number of people helped, urgency, and developer effort. Include accessibility, security, and maintenance needs—not just visible features.
3. **Validate assumptions** with brief feedback from volunteers and website users, plus any available analytics.
4. **Prioritize critical fixes first**, then improvements offering the greatest benefit for the least effort. Consider dependencies that could change the order.
5. **Choose one small, achievable improvement**, define a success measure, and protect the developer from competing requests.

Keep the remaining items in a transparent backlog. Review priorities after each release, using results and updated effort estimates.
