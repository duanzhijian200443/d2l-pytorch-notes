Borrowing rarely used household tools saves money and avoids storing bulky equipment. It also lets you try different models before buying. However, availability may not match your schedule, and collecting and returning tools takes time. You may need to pay deposits or rental fees, replace consumables, and cover damage. Borrowing from friends also requires clear expectations about return dates and condition.

Owning offers immediate access, familiarity, and freedom to work at your own pace. But ownership brings maintenance, repairs, storage needs, and eventual disposal—not just the purchase price. Batteries can deteriorate between uses, while neglected tools may become unsafe.

For occasional jobs, borrowing often makes sense; buying is more attractive when reliability, repeated use, or urgent access matters.
