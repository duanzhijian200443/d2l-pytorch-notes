A project leader should communicate the delay promptly, clearly, and honestly. Explain what has changed, the current impact, and why a reliable revised completion date is not yet available. Avoid vague reassurance or an overly optimistic estimate. Instead, share the best-known facts, the key factors causing uncertainty, and what is being done to resolve them.

For example:

“We’ve identified issues that will delay completion beyond our original target. We are assessing their full impact, so I can’t responsibly provide a firm new date yet. The main uncertainties involve [briefly state causes]. The team is prioritizing corrective work, and we will review progress on [specific date or frequency]. I will provide an updated forecast as soon as the information is reliable, even if that means reporting further changes. In the meantime, we are protecting the highest-priority deliverables and will communicate any immediate effects on dependent teams.”

Invite questions, acknowledge the inconvenience, and maintain regular updates. Clear ownership and a predictable communication cadence help preserve trust while the timeline is being recalculated.
