Natural selection changes shell-color frequency through differences in survival and reproduction, not conscious choice. Suppose a snail population includes both light- and dark-shelled individuals, and shell color is inherited. On pale ground, birds may spot and eat dark snails more easily. Light-shelled snails therefore tend to survive longer and produce more offspring. Because their offspring often inherit the light-shell genes, the next generation contains a slightly higher proportion of light shells. If the same environmental pressure continues, this difference accumulates over many generations, making light shells increasingly common. Individual snails do not change color because they “need” to, nor do they decide what traits their offspring will have. Instead, existing inherited variation is filtered by the environment, gradually changing the population.
