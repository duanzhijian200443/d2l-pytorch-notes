Bring it up at a relaxed moment, rather than when your friend has already been waiting. Frame it as a difference in habits, not a question of who is more considerate.

You might say: “I’ve noticed you often get there about twenty minutes before our agreed time, while I usually arrive at that time. I appreciate that you’re ready to meet, and I want to check whether you feel kept waiting.”

Listen before offering a solution. Your friend might prefer arriving early because travel feels unpredictable, or simply enjoy having a little time alone. If they’re not upset, there may be nothing to fix.

If they do expect you earlier, clarify gently: “For me, meeting at six means arriving at six. Could we agree that coming early is optional, with no expectation that the other person will?”

You could also choose somewhere comfortable to wait. The goal is a shared expectation—not persuading either person that their habit is wrong.
