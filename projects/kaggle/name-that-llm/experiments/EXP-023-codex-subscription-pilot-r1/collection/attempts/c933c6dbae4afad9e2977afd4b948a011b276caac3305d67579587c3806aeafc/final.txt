A password manager reduces the need to remember many passwords by storing login details in an encrypted digital vault. Instead of memorizing a different password for every website or app, you generally remember just one strong master password that unlocks the vault. The manager can also generate long, unique passwords and automatically fill them in when you sign in. This makes it easier to avoid weak passwords or reusing the same password across multiple accounts. If one website suffers a breach, unique passwords help prevent attackers from accessing your other accounts.

However, the user still has responsibility for protecting access to the password manager. Choose a strong, unique master password, keep it private, and enable multifactor authentication where available. You should also secure your devices, install updates, watch for phishing attempts, and store recovery information safely. A password manager simplifies password security, but it does not eliminate the need for careful judgment or make your accounts completely immune to attack.
