An error log can reveal **what the application observed when it failed**: timestamps, error messages, exception types, stack traces, affected components, request or user context, and sometimes resource or dependency states. These details help locate the failure, reconstruct the execution path, identify patterns, and form likely root-cause hypotheses. Repeated entries can also show frequency, scope, and correlation with deployments or external events.

By itself, however, a log usually cannot establish the definitive root cause, prove who or what is responsible, or show everything that happened before and after the error. Logs may be incomplete, misleading, sampled, misconfigured, or missing relevant context. They also rarely prove data integrity, security impact, or the correctness of a proposed fix. Those conclusions generally require corroboration from metrics, traces, configuration, code, deployment history, reproduction, and testing.
