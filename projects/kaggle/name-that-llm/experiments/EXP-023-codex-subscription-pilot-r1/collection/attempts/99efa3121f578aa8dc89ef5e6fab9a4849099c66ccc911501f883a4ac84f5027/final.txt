The concert hall held its breath while ushers searched for chair 42.

“It’s only one seat,” the conductor said, checking his watch.

“It’s not,” said Mara, the stage manager.

The missing chair belonged in the second row, directly beneath a cracked plaster rosette in the ceiling. Months earlier, Mara had noticed a faint stain there and quietly moved the subscription holder, an elderly structural engineer named Mr. Vale, to another seat while arranging an inspection.

Tonight, Mr. Vale had arrived early, found his usual chair gone, and pointed upward. “That crack has widened,” he said.

The building manager fetched a ladder. Behind the rosette, a rusted pipe joint had been leaking into a beam; vibration from the orchestra could have shaken loose a heavy section of plaster.

The audience grumbled through the delay, then watched workers cordon off three rows and install a temporary support.

At last, the orchestra began.

Afterward, Mr. Vale thanked Mara for remembering his seat.

She smiled. “I didn’t remember the seat.”

She had remembered who always looked up.
