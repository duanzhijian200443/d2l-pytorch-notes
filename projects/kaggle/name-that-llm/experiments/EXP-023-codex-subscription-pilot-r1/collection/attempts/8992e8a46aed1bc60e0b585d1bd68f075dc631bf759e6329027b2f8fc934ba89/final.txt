Frame the request around decisions, not open-ended opinions. Briefly state the plan’s purpose, constraints, and what is already settled. Then ask for feedback on a few specific areas—for example: “What risks am I missing?”, “Which assumption seems weakest?”, and “What would prevent your team from executing this?”

Set a deadline and channel, and explain how input will be used: “Please send concise comments by Thursday; I’ll incorporate themes and publish the final plan.” Distinguish feedback from approval and make clear that not every suggestion will be adopted.

You can also invite alternative proposals only when they include the trade-offs they introduce. This keeps feedback practical, surfaces genuine concerns, and avoids turning a draft into a line-by-line negotiation.
