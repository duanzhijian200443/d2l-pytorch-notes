An unreliable narrator makes readers active participants because the account cannot be accepted at face value. Gaps, contradictions, exaggerations, and evasions encourage readers to compare details, question motives, and infer what may really have happened. Rather than simply receiving a fixed version of events, readers must distinguish between the narrator’s perceptions and the author’s implied reality. This creates suspense: a seemingly minor inconsistency can become important evidence. It also invites ethical and emotional judgment, since readers may sympathize with the narrator while recognizing their self-deception or dishonesty. As readers revise their interpretation throughout the novel, they help construct the story’s meaning, making the reading experience more investigative, imaginative, and personally engaging.
