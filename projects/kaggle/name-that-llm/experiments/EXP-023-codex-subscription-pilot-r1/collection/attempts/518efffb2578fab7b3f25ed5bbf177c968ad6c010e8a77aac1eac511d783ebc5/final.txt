A workable policy is to reserve most capacity for appointments while protecting a smaller block for walk-ins. For example, the shop could allocate 70% of daily technician time to scheduled appointments and 30% to same-day walk-in work. Customers booking appointments receive a time window, such as 9:00–10:00, rather than an exact minute, and are asked to arrive with any relevant details or parts information. Walk-ins are accepted until the walk-in capacity is full and are handled in the order received, with an honest estimated wait time. If a walk-in job is more complicated than expected, the shop can offer to schedule it for a later appointment instead.

The shop should also set limits: appointments are held for perhaps 15 minutes, cancellations should provide reasonable notice, and urgent or safety-related repairs may be prioritized regardless of booking status. The daily capacity should be reviewed regularly and adjusted based on demand.

The main compromise is utilization versus predictability. Reserving capacity for walk-ins gives flexibility and captures customers who need immediate help, but that capacity may sit unused if no one walks in. Conversely, accepting too many walk-ins can delay appointments and frustrate customers who planned ahead. A clearly published split, transparent wait estimates, and periodic adjustment provide the best balance.
