Learning one craft deeply offers the satisfaction of sustained progress. Repetition sharpens judgment, difficult problems become approachable, and skills can develop into a distinctive personal style. Depth may also support professional opportunities, though it requires patience and can leave less room for other interests.

Trying many crafts casually offers variety, discovery, and the freedom to enjoy making things without pursuing mastery. Different activities can spark unexpected connections and help you learn what you like. The trade-off is that some techniques and rewards remain inaccessible without sustained practice.

Neither approach is inherently better: their value depends on whether you seek expertise, relaxation, exploration, or some combination. You can also move between them, exploring widely before committing—or keeping one serious practice alongside several playful ones.
