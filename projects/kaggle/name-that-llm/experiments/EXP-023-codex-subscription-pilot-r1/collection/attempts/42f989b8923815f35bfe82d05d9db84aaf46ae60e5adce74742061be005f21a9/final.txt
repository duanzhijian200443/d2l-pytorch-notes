The candle is predicted to be **5 centimeters tall after five hours**, assuming it continues to burn down by the same amount each hour.

To find the hourly decrease, compare its initial height with its height after two hours. It starts at 20 centimeters and falls to 14 centimeters, so it loses:

20 − 14 = 6 centimeters.

That loss happens over two hours. Dividing by two gives a constant burning rate of:

6 ÷ 2 = 3 centimeters per hour.

Over five hours, the candle would therefore lose a total of:

3 × 5 = 15 centimeters.

Subtracting this loss from its original height gives:

20 − 15 = **5 centimeters**.

We can describe the same relationship with the equation **h = 20 − 3t**, where *h* is the height in centimeters and *t* is the time in hours since the candle was first lit. This prediction depends on the stated assumption that the rate stays constant throughout all five hours.
