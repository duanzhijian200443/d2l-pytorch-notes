Choose a calm moment, not while one of you is waiting or rushing. Start with appreciation and describe the difference neutrally: “I’ve noticed you often arrive about twenty minutes early, while I tend to arrive at the agreed time.” Avoid framing either habit as wrong.

Ask what arriving early means to your friend. It may help them feel relaxed, prepared, or respectful. Then explain your perspective: being “on time” means arriving at the scheduled time, and you do not intend to inconvenience them.

Focus on expectations rather than blame. You might say, “When we agree on 7:00, should we both treat 7:00 as the meeting time, or would you prefer a window?” Agree that if your friend chooses to arrive early, they will bring something to do and won’t expect you before the scheduled time. Alternatively, set two times: an optional arrival time and a firm start time.

Keep the tone curious and collaborative. The goal is not to decide whose approach is correct, but to create an arrangement that prevents resentment.
