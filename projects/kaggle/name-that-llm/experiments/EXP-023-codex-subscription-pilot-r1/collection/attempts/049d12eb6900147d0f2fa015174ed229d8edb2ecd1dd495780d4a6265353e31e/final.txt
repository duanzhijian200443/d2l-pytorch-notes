A fixed daily routine reduces decision fatigue, builds habits, and makes responsibilities predictable. It works best when deadlines are tight, tasks recur, or consistency matters, such as studying, exercising, or managing medication. Substantial unplanned time supports spontaneity, exploration, and responsiveness to unexpected opportunities. It suits creative work, vacations, changing circumstances, or periods when recovery matters more than output. Neither approach is universally better: a useful compromise is to anchor essential commitments while leaving generous open blocks for rest, curiosity, and surprises.
