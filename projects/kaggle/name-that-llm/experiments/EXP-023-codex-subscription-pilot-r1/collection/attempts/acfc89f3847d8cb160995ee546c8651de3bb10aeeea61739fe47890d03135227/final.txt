You can appreciate your friend’s generosity without accepting the whole offer. The key is to separate your gratitude from the size of the commitment: acknowledge their kindness, explain what would actually help, and suggest a clear, smaller arrangement.

For example:

“Thank you—that’s incredibly thoughtful. I don’t need the whole setup, but I’d really appreciate help with just [specific task]. Would you be up for doing that for about [amount of time]? That would be plenty.”

Be concrete about the limits. “Could you give me a ride there? I’ve got the trip home covered” is easier to navigate than “Don’t go to too much trouble.” A specific task, time window, or endpoint keeps both of you from having to guess.

If they insist on doing more, you can stay warm while repeating your preference:

“I really appreciate it. Keeping this simple would work best for me, so just that one thing would be great.”

You don’t need to invent an excuse or promise an equally elaborate favor in return. A sincere thank-you and a brief message afterward can express genuine appreciation.

Your friend can also decline the smaller version. The goal is an arrangement you both freely choose—not accepting more than you want to protect their feelings.
