Mara took Bell Street instead of Belle Street because rain had blurred the address on her delivery slip.

Halfway down, she found six neighbors staring at a drain.

“Wedding ring?” she asked, braking.

“Mrs. Patel’s gate key,” said a boy. “Third time this week.”

Mara leaned her bicycle against the fence. From her repair pouch, she pulled a spoke magnet tied to a shoelace. The key emerged wearing a beard of wet leaves.

Everyone cheered quietly; a baby was sleeping upstairs.

Before leaving, Mara slipped an old reflective ankle band through the key’s tiny ring.

“Harder to drop now.”

Mrs. Patel fastened it around her wrist.

Mara checked the address again. Her customer was still two streets away, but at least the soup was warm.
