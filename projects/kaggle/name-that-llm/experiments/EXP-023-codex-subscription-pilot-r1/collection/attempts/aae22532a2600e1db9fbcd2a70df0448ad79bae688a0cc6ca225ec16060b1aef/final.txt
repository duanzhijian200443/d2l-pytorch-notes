At the community garden, Maya found a tomato plant with no label. She asked the gray-haired man kneeling beside it, “Do you know what this is?”

“Only that it survived three storms,” he said. “I’m Harold.”

Maya introduced herself and suggested they call it Mystery Tomato. Together, they built a small sign from a scrap of cedar. When the first fruit ripened, they cut it open and discovered golden flesh.

“Sweet,” Harold said, surprised.

“Like a tiny sunset,” Maya replied.

They saved the seeds, then planted them in a shared bed. By autumn, Mystery Tomatoes filled the garden. The missing label had cost them a name, but given them something better: Saturday mornings, muddy hands, and an unexpected friendship.
