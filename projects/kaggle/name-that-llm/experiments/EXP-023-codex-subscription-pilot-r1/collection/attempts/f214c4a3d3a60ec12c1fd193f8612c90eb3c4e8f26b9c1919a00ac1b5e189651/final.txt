A battery stores usable energy in the chemical arrangement of materials inside its cells. Chemical reactions create a difference in electric potential between the battery’s terminals. When the flashlight circuit is closed, this potential difference establishes an electric field through the circuit, causing electrons already present in the metal wires to drift.

As charges move through the bulb or LED, the battery’s chemical reactions continue, converting chemical potential energy into electrical energy. The lamp then transforms much of that energy into light and heat. Meanwhile, the battery’s internal materials change composition: reactants are consumed, products accumulate, and the potential difference gradually falls. Thus, the battery does not contain a supply of “electricity” that gets poured out; it provides energy and maintains conditions that drive charge already in the circuit.
