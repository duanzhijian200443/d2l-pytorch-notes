Keeping a shared workplace very tidy creates clarity, reduces distractions, and makes tools and materials easy to find. It also communicates professionalism and ensures that common areas remain welcoming and safe. However, strict tidiness can impose hidden costs: employees may spend time repeatedly packing away active projects, lose useful visual cues, or feel that experimentation is discouraged.

Allowing visible works in progress can support creativity and continuity. Models, sketches, notes, and partially assembled materials help people resume tasks quickly and invite spontaneous feedback from colleagues. Yet too much visible work can become clutter, occupy shared space, expose confidential information, or make it unclear whether materials are important or simply abandoned.

A balanced policy is usually best. Teams can keep walkways, meeting rooms, and communal equipment consistently orderly while designating specific shelves, boards, desks, or project zones for ongoing work. Clear labels, owners, review dates, and confidentiality rules preserve accountability. This approach treats tidiness not as the absence of activity, but as an organized environment where active work remains visible without inconveniencing others.
