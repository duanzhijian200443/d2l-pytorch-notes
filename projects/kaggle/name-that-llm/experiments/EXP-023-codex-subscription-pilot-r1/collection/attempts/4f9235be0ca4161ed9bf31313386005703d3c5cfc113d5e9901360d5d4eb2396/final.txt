An accessible reading order means a document’s content is arranged in a logical sequence that assistive technologies, such as screen readers, can follow—typically headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, and captions in their intended order. Visual placement alone does not guarantee this because appearance and underlying document structure are separate. Text boxes, columns, floating images, or manually positioned elements may look correct on screen but be encoded in a confusing sequence, causing assistive technology to read content out of context.
