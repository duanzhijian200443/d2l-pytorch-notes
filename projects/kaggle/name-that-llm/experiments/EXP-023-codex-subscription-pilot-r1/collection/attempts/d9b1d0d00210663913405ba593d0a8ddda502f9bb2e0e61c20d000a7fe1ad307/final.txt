A written definition of “finished” gives everyone the same target instead of relying on assumptions. In a small collaborative project, it can specify what must be completed, reviewed, tested, documented, and approved before work is considered done. This prevents one person from thinking a feature is complete when another expects additional testing, cleanup, or user instructions. It also makes responsibilities clearer: contributors know what evidence to provide, and reviewers know what to check. A shared definition reduces repeated discussions, last-minute surprises, and disagreements about whether work is ready to use or release. Because the criteria are visible, the team can identify gaps early and resolve them objectively. It should remain short, practical, and easy to update as the project’s needs change.
