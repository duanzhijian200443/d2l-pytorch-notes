Mara returned the borrowed ladder just before dusk, carrying it across the lane with paint still flecked on her sleeves. Old Mr. Vale inspected its rungs, then nodded approvingly.

“Your roof survived?”

“Barely,” Mara said. “Though I found a nest in the gutter.”

“A sign you need better company than pigeons.”

She laughed, surprised by the warmth in his voice. From his porch came the scent of cinnamon and the soft clink of cups. Mr. Vale hesitated, suddenly shy.

“My daughter’s visiting,” he said. “We’re playing cards, making too much tea. Would you join us?”

Mara looked toward her quiet house, its repaired roof catching the last gold light. Then she set down her empty hands, smiled, and accepted the invitation.
