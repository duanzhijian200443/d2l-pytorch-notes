Shell color can become more or less common through differences in survival and reproduction, not because any animal chooses to change. Imagine a population with light and dark shells living on dark rocks. Birds may spot light-shelled animals more easily, so those animals are eaten more often. Dark-shelled animals are more likely to survive long enough to reproduce. If shell color is inherited, their offspring will tend to carry genes for darker shells. Over many generations, the proportion of dark-shell genes—and therefore dark shells—can rise in the population. Individual animals do not evolve during their lives; instead, the population’s inherited traits change gradually because some variants leave more offspring than others.
