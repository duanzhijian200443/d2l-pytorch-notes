Remembering people’s stories is a strength: it shows you listen and care. Forgetting names doesn’t cancel that out. The goal is to give names a little more attention without making conversations feel like memory tests.

Try these habits at your next community event:

- **Pause when you’re introduced.** Focus on the name rather than planning your response. If you didn’t hear it clearly, ask immediately.
- **Use it naturally once or twice.** “Nice to meet you, Maya” helps connect the sound to the person. Avoid repeating it so often that it feels rehearsed.
- **Link the name to their story.** Silently think, “Maya, who’s organizing the neighborhood garden.” You already remember stories well; let that strength support name recall.
- **Make a brief note afterward.** Record a name and one non-sensitive detail, keeping any notes private.
- **Review before returning.** A quick look at names from previous events can make recognition easier.

When you forget, be straightforward: “I remember you were training for a half marathon, but I’m blanking on your name. Would you remind me?” That communicates genuine recognition rather than indifference.

Skip elaborate apologies or pretending you remember. Most people appreciate warmth and honesty. Success isn’t perfect recall—it’s helping people feel seen while gradually getting better with names.
