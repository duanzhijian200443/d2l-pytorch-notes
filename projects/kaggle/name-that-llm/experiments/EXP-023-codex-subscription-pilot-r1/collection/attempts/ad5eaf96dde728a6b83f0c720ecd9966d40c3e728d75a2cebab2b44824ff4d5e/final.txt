Two stars can appear equally bright because apparent brightness depends on both a star’s true light output, called luminosity, and its distance from Earth. A very luminous star located far away may look as bright as a much dimmer star that is nearby. Interstellar dust can also absorb or scatter some starlight, making a powerful star appear fainter. Astronomers determine distance and account for dust to calculate intrinsic luminosity, revealing how much light each star actually produces.
