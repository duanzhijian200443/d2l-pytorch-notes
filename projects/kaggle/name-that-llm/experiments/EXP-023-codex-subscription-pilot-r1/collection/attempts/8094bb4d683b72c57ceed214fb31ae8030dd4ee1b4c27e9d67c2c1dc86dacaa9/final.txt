Captions and a written transcript both make a recorded lecture’s spoken content available as text, supporting accessibility and helping learners understand unfamiliar terms or unclear speech. However, they serve different viewing and study needs.

Captions appear in sync with the video, allowing viewers to follow the lecture while watching demonstrations, slides, and gestures. They are especially useful for deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers and anyone watching without sound. Good captions also identify speakers and relevant sounds.

A transcript presents the lecture as a separate, readable document. It makes it easier to scan, search, quote, review key ideas, or study at one’s own pace. Timestamps can connect passages to the recording, but a transcript generally does not provide captions’ immediate connection between text and visuals.
