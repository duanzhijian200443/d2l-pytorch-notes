A short menu can be more helpful because it reduces decision fatigue and often signals that the restaurant focuses on a few dishes made well. Customers seeking speed, clarity, or a curated dining experience may appreciate this approach, especially if ingredients are fresh and the staff can explain each item confidently. It can also make ordering easier for first-time visitors.

However, a large menu may better serve groups with varied tastes, families with children, or diners with dietary restrictions. These customers may value choice and feel disappointed if a limited menu lacks familiar options. Ultimately, neither format is always superior: a short menu suits customers who want quality and simplicity, while a larger one helps those expecting flexibility and variety.
