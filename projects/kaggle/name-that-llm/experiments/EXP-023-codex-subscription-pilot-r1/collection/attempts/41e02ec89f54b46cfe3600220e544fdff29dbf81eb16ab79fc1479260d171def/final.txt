Let the number of blue buttons be \(b\). Since there are twice as many red buttons, the number of red buttons is \(2b\).

All buttons together total 18, so add the two quantities:

\[
b + 2b = 18
\]

Combine like terms:

\[
3b = 18
\]

Divide both sides by 3:

\[
b = 6
\]

So there are 6 blue buttons. The red count is twice that amount:

\[
2 \times 6 = 12
\]

Therefore, the jar contains **12 red buttons and 6 blue buttons**.

You can also reason without an equation: the buttons are in a ratio of 2 red to 1 blue. That means every group of 3 buttons has 2 red and 1 blue. Since 18 divided by 3 is 6 groups, there are \(6 \times 2 = 12\) red buttons and \(6 \times 1 = 6\) blue buttons.
