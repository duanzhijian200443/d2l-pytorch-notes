The plant is showing **positive phototropism**, the growth response in which a shoot bends toward a light source. Light is detected mainly at the shoot tip. When light comes from one side, the plant redistributes a growth hormone called **auxin** to the shaded side of the stem. Auxin stimulates cells there to elongate more than the cells on the brightly lit side. This unequal growth makes the stem curve toward the window, allowing the leaves to receive more light for photosynthesis.

The bending does not happen instantly because it depends on a chain of biological processes. The plant must detect the direction of light, produce or redistribute chemical signals, transport auxin through its tissues, and activate changes in cell growth. Cells then need time to absorb water, expand, and build or adjust their cell walls. Since the stem bends through gradual differences in growth on opposite sides, the curvature becomes noticeable over hours or days rather than seconds. The response is therefore a controlled growth process, not a rapid movement like an animal turning toward something.
