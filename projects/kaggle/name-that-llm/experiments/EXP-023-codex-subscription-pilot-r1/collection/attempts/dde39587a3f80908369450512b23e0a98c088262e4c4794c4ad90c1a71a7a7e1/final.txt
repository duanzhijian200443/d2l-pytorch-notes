Use a flexible “meal components” approach instead of assigning every dinner to a date.

1. Check your calendar and note how many nights you’ll likely cook, eat out, or need fast food.
2. Inventory fridge, freezer, and pantry; prioritize perishables and leftovers.
3. Choose 3–4 versatile proteins, several vegetables, fruit, grains/starches, and easy backup meals.
4. Plan a few loose meal ideas that share ingredients—e.g., roast chicken can become salads, tacos, or soup.
5. Buy only enough highly perishable items for the first half of the week; rely on frozen or shelf-stable options later.
6. Leave one or two dinners unplanned for leftovers, social plans, or takeout.
7. Keep a running list during the week of staples you finish.

At midweek, reassess what remains and make a small top-up trip only if needed.
