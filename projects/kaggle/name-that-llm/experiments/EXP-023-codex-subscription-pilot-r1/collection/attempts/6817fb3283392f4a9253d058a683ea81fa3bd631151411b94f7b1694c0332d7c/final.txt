A workable policy is to reserve about **70% of daily repair capacity for appointments and 30% for walk-ins**. Appointment customers receive a scheduled check-in time and an estimated completion window, while walk-ins are handled in order of arrival within the reserved capacity. Each morning, the shop should review staffing and job complexity and adjust the walk-in quota if necessary.

To prevent unused capacity, any appointment slot not confirmed by the prior day—or not claimed within a 15-minute grace period—can be released to walk-in customers. Likewise, if the walk-in queue fills, staff should offer the next available appointment rather than promising same-day service. Emergency or very quick repairs may be prioritized at the service manager’s discretion, but this exception should be clearly communicated.

The main compromise is between **predictability and flexibility**. Reserving most capacity for appointments gives the shop a manageable workload and gives scheduled customers confidence about when their repairs will begin. However, it limits how many walk-ins can be served immediately. Keeping capacity open for walk-ins attracts customers who need urgent or convenient service, but that capacity may sit unused on slow days and can delay appointments if walk-in jobs take longer than expected. A fixed starting ratio, reviewed using actual demand data, balances both needs without guaranteeing instant service to everyone.
