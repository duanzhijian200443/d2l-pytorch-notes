The candle’s height decreases at a constant rate. It starts at 20 centimeters and measures 14 centimeters after two hours, so it has burned down:

20 − 14 = 6 centimeters.

Over two hours, that is a burn rate of:

6 ÷ 2 = 3 centimeters per hour.

After five hours, the candle would therefore have burned:

3 × 5 = 15 centimeters.

Subtracting that amount from its original height gives:

20 − 15 = 5 centimeters.

So, under the assumption that the candle continues burning down by exactly the same amount each hour, its predicted height after five hours is **5 centimeters**. This model treats the rate as perfectly steady; in reality, a candle’s burn rate can vary somewhat because of wick length, wax composition, airflow, or melting patterns.
