Explaining a historical person’s choices in context means reconstructing the pressures, beliefs, institutions, and limited knowledge that shaped their decisions. It helps us understand why an action may have seemed reasonable, necessary, or normal at the time, even when it appears harmful today. Excusing every consequence goes further: it treats context as a blanket defense that removes responsibility or minimizes suffering.

Good historical judgment avoids both presentism and moral surrender. We should not assume people in the past shared all modern values, but neither should we pretend they had no alternatives, critics, or capacity for choice. Context can complicate blame without erasing it. A balanced account asks what options were available, what the person knew, whom their choices harmed, and how contemporaries themselves judged those actions.
