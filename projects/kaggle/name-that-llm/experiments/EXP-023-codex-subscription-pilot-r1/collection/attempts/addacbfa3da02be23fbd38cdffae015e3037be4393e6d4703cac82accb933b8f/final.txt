By Saturday morning, the rain had washed every plant label clean except one: MINT, standing proudly beside a tomato vine.

“That can’t be right,” said Nora.

“It’s ambitious,” replied the man kneeling in the neighboring plot.

Nora had gardened beside him for three months without learning his name. Now they bent over an unmarked seedling, debating whether it was parsley, carrot, or something neither should eat.

“My wife knew all these,” he said, gently brushing dirt from a leaf.

“My husband just called everything lettuce.”

He laughed. She did too.

They never identified the seedling. But by noon, they had shared a thermos of tea and written a new label together.

ASK US IN JULY.
