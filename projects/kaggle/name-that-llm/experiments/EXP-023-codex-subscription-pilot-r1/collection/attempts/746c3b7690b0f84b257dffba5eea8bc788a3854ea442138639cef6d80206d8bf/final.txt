A checksum is like recording a parcel’s weight before shipping. When it arrives, the recipient weighs it again. If the weight differs, something was probably lost, added, or damaged in transit. Likewise, a checksum summarizes data so accidental changes can be detected by comparing the original and received values. However, matching weights do not guarantee the parcel’s contents are intact or authentic: items could be swapped for others of equal weight. Similarly, a checksum does not necessarily prevent deliberate tampering or prove who sent the data.
