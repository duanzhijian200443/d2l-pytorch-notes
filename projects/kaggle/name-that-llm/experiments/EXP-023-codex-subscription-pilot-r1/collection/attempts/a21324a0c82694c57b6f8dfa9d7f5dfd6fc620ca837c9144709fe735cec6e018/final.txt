In a compost pile, vegetable scraps are broken down by microbes, insects, and fungi. Peels, leaves, and stems soften, darken, and lose their original shapes as they mix with soil-like particles. Over time, they become crumbly, brown compost rich in nutrients. The material has changed form rather than vanished: much of it remains in the pile as humus, while some becomes water vapor and gases released during decomposition.
