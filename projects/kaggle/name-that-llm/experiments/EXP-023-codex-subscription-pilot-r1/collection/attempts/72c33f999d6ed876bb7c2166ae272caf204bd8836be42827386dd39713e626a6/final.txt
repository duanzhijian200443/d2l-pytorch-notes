A digital photograph is made of tiny colored squares called pixels. On a phone’s small screen, those pixels are packed closely together, so your eyes see a smooth, sharp image. When the same photo is enlarged into a poster, the existing pixels must cover a much bigger area. Fine details can look soft, and edges may appear jagged or blocky.

The main limitation is resolution: the amount of detail captured in the original image. Enlarging a photo does not automatically add real detail. Software can estimate extra pixels to make the enlargement look smoother, but it cannot reliably recover details that were never recorded. A higher-resolution original generally makes a sharper poster, especially when viewed up close.
