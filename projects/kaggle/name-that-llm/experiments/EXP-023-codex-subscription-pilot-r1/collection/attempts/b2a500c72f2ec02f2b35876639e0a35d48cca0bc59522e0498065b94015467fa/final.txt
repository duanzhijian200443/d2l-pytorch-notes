A train scheduled every fifteen minutes does not mean that every passenger waits exactly seven and a half minutes. Seven and a half minutes is the **average expected waiting time**, not a guaranteed waiting time.

If a passenger arrives at a completely random moment between two train departures, the time until the next train could be almost zero seconds if the train has just left, or almost fifteen minutes if it is about to arrive. Every waiting time between these extremes is possible. Under the usual assumption that arrivals are uniformly distributed throughout the fifteen-minute interval, the average of all possible waiting times is

\[
\frac{0+15}{2}=7.5\text{ minutes}.
\]

Thus, some passengers will wait one or two minutes, while others may wait twelve or fourteen minutes. Over many randomly timed arrivals, these different waits balance out, producing an average of seven and a half minutes. A single passenger’s wait, however, depends entirely on where their arrival falls within the current interval.
