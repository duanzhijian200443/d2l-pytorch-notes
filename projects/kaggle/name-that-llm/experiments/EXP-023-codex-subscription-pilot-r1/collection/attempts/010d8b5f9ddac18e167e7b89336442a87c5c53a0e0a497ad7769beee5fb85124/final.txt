Run a six-week “Staff Discovery Shelf” pilot in one visible but limited section of the store. Each week, staff members select 10–15 books by authors with low name recognition, while preserving familiar genre labels (mystery, romance, history, and so on). Arrange the books within those categories rather than creating an entirely new system, so customers can browse naturally.

Give each selected book a consistent shelf card containing: a one-sentence description, “If you like…” comparisons, and a short note from the recommending employee. Add a small sign explaining that the display is designed for discovering new authors, not ranking books. Include a QR code or paper slip where customers can mark whether they browsed, purchased, or want to hear more.

To avoid disrupting navigation, keep the pilot physically separate from core shelves, cap its size, and return books to their regular locations after the trial. Compare results with a similar nearby section without the display. Track sales of featured titles, browsing interactions, customer comments, and whether shoppers report finding an author they had not previously known.

Success would mean increased sales and engagement without more customer confusion or staff workload. If effective, expand gradually by genre; if not, adjust the recommendation cards or selection criteria before enlarging the display.
