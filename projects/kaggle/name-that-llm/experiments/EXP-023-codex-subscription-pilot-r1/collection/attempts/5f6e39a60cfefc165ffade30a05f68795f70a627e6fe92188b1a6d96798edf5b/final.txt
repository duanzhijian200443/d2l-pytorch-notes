A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading while encouraging everyone to finish when possible. Requiring completion can support richer discussion: readers can examine the ending, trace character development, and judge the book as a whole. It also recognizes the effort of members who prepared. However, a strict rule may exclude people with limited time, accessibility barriers, or strong negative reactions to the book.

Not finishing can itself produce valuable discussion. Asking where someone lost interest, felt uncomfortable, or found the argument unconvincing can reveal weaknesses that enthusiastic readers overlook. Still, those impressions should not be treated as judgments about sections they have not read.

A practical compromise is to make meetings open to everyone, announce that spoilers are expected, and begin with general reactions before exploring the complete work. Members who stopped can contribute without dominating or asking others to avoid the ending. The goal should be thoughtful engagement, not a completion test—but inclusion works best alongside shared expectations.
