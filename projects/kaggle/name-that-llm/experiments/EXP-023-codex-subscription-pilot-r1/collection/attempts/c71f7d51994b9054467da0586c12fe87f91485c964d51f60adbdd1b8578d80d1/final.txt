A list of five numbers with an average of 10 has a total sum of:

\[
5 \times 10 = 50
\]

When one number increases by 5, the total sum also increases by 5, becoming 55. The number of values remains five, so the new average is:

\[
55 \div 5 = 11
\]

Therefore, the average increases by 1, from 10 to 11. In general, when one value in a group changes, the average changes by the amount of that change divided by the number of values. Here, that is \(5 \div 5 = 1\). The other four numbers do not change, but their combined effect means the increase of 5 is distributed across all five numbers when calculating the new average.
