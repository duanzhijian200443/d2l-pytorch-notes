Start by acknowledging the generosity behind the offer, then clearly name the smaller help you actually need. This lets your friend feel appreciated without committing either of you to an arrangement that may become burdensome.

You might say:

> “That’s incredibly kind of you, and I really appreciate how willing you are to help. I don’t think I need the full plan, though. What would help most is just [specific small favor]. Would you be comfortable with that?”

Be concrete about the scope, timing, and endpoint. For example: “A ride to the appointment on Tuesday would be perfect; I can handle everything after that.” Specific boundaries prevent the favor from quietly expanding.

If your friend insists on doing more, affirm the intention while repeating your preference:

> “It means a lot that you’d offer all of that. Keeping it simple would actually make me feel most comfortable, so the smaller version is genuinely best for me.”

You do not need to accept every part of an offer in order to accept it graciously. Appreciation and limits can coexist. Afterward, thank them sincerely—perhaps with a message or a small reciprocal gesture—but avoid turning it into a debt you must immediately repay.

The goal is a warm, manageable agreement: recognize their care, accept the useful portion, and define the boundary kindly.
