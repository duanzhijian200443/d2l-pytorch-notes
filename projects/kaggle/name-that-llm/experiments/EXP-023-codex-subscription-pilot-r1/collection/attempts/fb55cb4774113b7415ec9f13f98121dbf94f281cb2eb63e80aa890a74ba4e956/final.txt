Historians distinguish them because timing shapes evidence. A contemporary diary records what the writer noticed, believed, or felt close to the event, often before knowing the outcome, but it may contain bias, rumor, omissions, or self-deception. A memoir written decades later benefits from hindsight and broader context, yet memory can fade, merge, or be reshaped by later experiences and public narratives. Neither is automatically truthful; historians compare each source with other evidence and examine its purpose, audience, and circumstances.
