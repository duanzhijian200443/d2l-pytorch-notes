An unreliable narrator makes readers more active because the story cannot be accepted at face value. Readers must notice contradictions, omissions, exaggerations, and gaps between what the narrator claims and what other details suggest. Instead of simply receiving a clear account, they become detectives, comparing evidence and forming judgments about what actually happened. This uncertainty also encourages readers to question the narrator’s motives, biases, memory, and self-awareness. Different readers may interpret the same clues in different ways, creating multiple possible meanings and inviting discussion. Moreover, as readers revise earlier assumptions in light of new information, they participate in constructing the story’s truth. Thus, an unreliable narrator transforms reading from passive observation into a process of investigation, interpretation, and continual reassessment.
