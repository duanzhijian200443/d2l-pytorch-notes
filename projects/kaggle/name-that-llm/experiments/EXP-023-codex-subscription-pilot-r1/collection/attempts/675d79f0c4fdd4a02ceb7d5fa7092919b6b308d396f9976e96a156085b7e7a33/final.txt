Let the number of blue buttons be one “part.” Because there are twice as many red buttons as blue buttons, the red buttons make up two equal parts. Altogether, the jar therefore contains three equal parts: one blue part plus two red parts.

Since there are 18 buttons in total, divide 18 by 3:

\[
18 \div 3 = 6
\]

Thus, each part represents 6 buttons. The blue buttons account for one part, so there are **6 blue buttons**. The red buttons account for two parts, so there are:

\[
2 \times 6 = 12
\]

Therefore, the jar contains **12 red buttons and 6 blue buttons**.

This result can also be found algebraically. If \(b\) is the number of blue buttons, then the number of red buttons is \(2b\). Their total gives the equation \(b + 2b = 18\), so \(3b = 18\) and \(b = 6\). Doubling 6 gives 12 red buttons. Finally, \(12 + 6 = 18\), confirming the counts.
