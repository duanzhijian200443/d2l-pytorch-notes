The shortest route on a map is based mainly on distance, but it may ignore real-world restrictions. If the path between the first two stops passes through a closed gate, the driver cannot use it, even though it appears quickest. A slightly longer route may avoid the gate, remain on accessible roads, and reduce delays or backtracking. Therefore, the best usable route should consider access restrictions, road conditions, traffic, vehicle limits, and reliability—not just the shortest mapped distance.
