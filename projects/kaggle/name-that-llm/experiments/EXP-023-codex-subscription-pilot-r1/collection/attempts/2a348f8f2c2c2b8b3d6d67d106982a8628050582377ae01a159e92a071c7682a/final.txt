The potter lifted the kiln door and leaned back from the breath of heat. When the first shimmer cleared, she peered inside.

Blue bowls crowded the shelves, their glazes deep as evening water. She smiled despite herself. The firing had held steady; the cobalt had pooled cleanly along the rims. One by one, she drew them out with padded hands, checking each foot for cracks.

Then she stopped.

At the rear sat a single green bowl.

She lifted it into the light, turning it slowly. The glaze was not the muddy green of a mistake, but bright and layered—moss at the base, jade along the lip. Her brow tightened. She checked the shelf, the neighboring bowls, even the label on the glaze jar, as if an answer might still be warm there.

None came.

She set the bowl apart and finished unloading, though her gaze kept returning to it. At last, she carried it through the quiet shop and placed it in the front window. In daylight, the green deepened. Her puzzled frown softened into a smile.
