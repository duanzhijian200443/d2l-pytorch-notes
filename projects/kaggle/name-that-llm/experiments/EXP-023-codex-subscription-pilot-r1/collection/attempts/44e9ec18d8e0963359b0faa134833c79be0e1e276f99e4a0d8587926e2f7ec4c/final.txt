At first, she thought the curtains were still closed.

Then she saw the brick wall: close enough to count its chipped red faces, interrupted by a single rusted fire escape and a narrow strip of sky. The booking photos had promised “city views,” which was technically true. Somewhere beyond the wall, traffic sighed and a siren faded.

She made coffee and sat by the window anyway. At nine, a pigeon landed on the fire escape rail. At ten, a man across the alley watered a plant balanced on his sill. By noon, sunlight slid down the bricks, turning them briefly orange.

The wall had not improved. But the room had acquired a small, stubborn world.
