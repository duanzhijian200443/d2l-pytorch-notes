A workshop owner should compare the cheaper supplier’s immediate savings with the wider cost of uncertain delivery. A lower purchase price can improve margins, preserve cash, or allow more competitive customer pricing. However, unpredictable deliveries may force the workshop to hold extra safety stock, tying up cash and storage space. If materials arrive late, employees and machinery may sit idle, urgent replacement orders may cost more, and customer deadlines could be missed. Repeated delays can also damage the workshop’s reputation and reduce repeat business.

The decision therefore depends on the workshop’s ability to absorb uncertainty. If materials are easy to store, demand is stable, and adequate inventory can be maintained cheaply, the lower-priced supplier may be worthwhile. If orders are time-sensitive, storage is limited, or production depends on precisely scheduled inputs, the more reliable supplier may provide greater overall value. The owner could calculate the total expected cost—including inventory, downtime, expedited shipping, and lost sales—and perhaps split orders between suppliers to balance savings with resilience.
