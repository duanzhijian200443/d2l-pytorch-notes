Because the candle burns down by the same amount each hour, its height decreases at a constant rate. Initially, it is 20 centimeters tall. After two hours, it is 14 centimeters tall, so it has lost:

20 − 14 = 6 centimeters

over two hours. Therefore, it burns at a rate of:

6 ÷ 2 = 3 centimeters per hour.

After five hours, the total amount burned will be:

5 × 3 = 15 centimeters.

Subtracting this from the original height gives:

20 − 15 = 5 centimeters.

Thus, under the assumption of a constant burning rate, the candle is predicted to be approximately **5 centimeters tall after five hours**. This is a linear model: every additional hour reduces the candle’s height by another 3 centimeters. The prediction assumes the candle burns uniformly and that factors such as wax composition, airflow, wick behavior, and temperature do not change the rate. In reality, a candle may not burn perfectly evenly, but based on the information given, 5 centimeters is the appropriate predicted height.
