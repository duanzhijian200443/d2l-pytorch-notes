A spinning ice skater turns faster by pulling their arms inward because angular momentum is conserved when external torque is negligible. Their angular momentum—rotational inertia multiplied by angular speed—stays the same. Pulling the arms closer to the body decreases rotational inertia, concentrating mass near the spin axis. To compensate, angular speed increases, so the skater spins faster. Their rotational kinetic energy may increase, supplied by the skater’s muscles as they pull their arms inward.
