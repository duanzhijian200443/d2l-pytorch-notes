Thank your friend warmly and acknowledge the generosity behind the offer before narrowing the request. You might say:

“Thank you—that’s incredibly kind of you, and I really appreciate you wanting to help. I don’t want this to become a big undertaking, though. What would help most is just [specific small task], and it should only take about [amount of time]. If you’re comfortable doing that, I’d be very grateful. Please don’t feel obligated if it’s inconvenient.”

Being specific keeps the arrangement manageable and prevents vague expectations. If their offer involves several steps, extra expense, or an ongoing commitment, clarify what you are and are not accepting:

“I’m happy to accept the ride there, but I’ll arrange my own way home,” or “I appreciate the offer to handle everything, but I only need help reviewing this one page.”

You can also make it easy for them to decline by adding, “No pressure at all—I’ll understand if you’re busy.” Afterward, thank them again and follow through on your part promptly. This approach accepts the kindness without taking advantage of it, while preserving your friend’s ability to set boundaries and keeping the favor proportionate to your actual need.
