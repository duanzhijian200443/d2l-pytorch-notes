A folktale can change with each retelling because storytellers adapt details to their audience, place, language, and time. A character’s name, the setting, or even particular events may shift, while new lessons or local customs are added. Yet people still recognize the tale when its central pattern remains: familiar main characters, a key conflict, memorable actions, and a similar ending or moral. For example, many versions of Cinderella differ in clothing, helpers, and cultural setting, but all involve a mistreated young person whose kindness or worth is eventually recognized. Folktales are therefore both stable and flexible: their core identity survives, while their surface details evolve.
