A puddle disappears through **evaporation**, which does not require boiling. Water molecules are always moving, even on a cool day. Some molecules at the puddle’s surface happen to have enough energy to escape into the air as water vapor. The remaining liquid may become slightly cooler because the fastest-moving molecules leave first.

Air movement carries this vapor away, allowing more water to evaporate. Dry air speeds the process because it can accept more water vapor, while sunlight and warmth also increase the molecules’ motion. Boiling is different: it occurs when water vapor forms bubbles throughout the liquid, typically near 100°C at sea level. Evaporation happens only at the surface and can occur at almost any temperature, so a cool puddle gradually vanishes.
