Counting how many tasks a support team closes measures activity, not necessarily service quality. A team can increase its total by resolving simple requests quickly while leaving complex, high-impact problems unresolved. If closure targets are emphasized, agents may rush conversations, provide superficial answers, split one issue into multiple tickets, or close cases before customers confirm that the problem is fixed. This can reduce customer satisfaction and encourage repeat contacts, making the workload appear productive while actually increasing it. Closure counts also ignore factors such as issue difficulty, resolution accuracy, response quality, empathy, and long-term outcomes. A better assessment combines completion data with customer satisfaction, first-contact resolution, reopen rates, resolution time by complexity, quality reviews, and evidence that customers’ underlying problems were genuinely solved.
