Every winter, Mr. Vale repaired the town hall clock, returning its hands to perfect time. But in the bakery window, he left Mrs. Imani’s small brass clock two minutes slow. Her husband had set it that way before he died, always arriving home “with two minutes to spare” for supper. Customers complained gently. Mr. Vale only smiled and adjusted everything else around it. Some measurements, he thought, were not errors at all—they were promises.
