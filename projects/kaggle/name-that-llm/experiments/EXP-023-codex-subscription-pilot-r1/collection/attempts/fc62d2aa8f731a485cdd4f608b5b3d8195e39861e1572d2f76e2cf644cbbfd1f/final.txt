The retired mapmaker spread a creased road map across the kitchen table.

“So you pinch it to zoom?” the teenager asked.

“You unfold it,” she replied, smiling. “And sometimes you fight the wind.”

He traced a blue line with one finger. “How does it know where I am?”

“It doesn’t. You have to know.”

“That seems inefficient.”

“It can be,” she admitted. “But look here: rivers, hills, old railways. A map shows more than the fastest route. It shows how places belong together.”

The teenager studied the faded symbols. “What if you take a wrong turn?”

“Then you compare the road, the compass, and the landscape.”

“No voice telling you to turn around?”

“Only your own.”

He folded the map badly. “Could you teach me?”

She gently reopened it. “First lesson: never fold it like that.”
