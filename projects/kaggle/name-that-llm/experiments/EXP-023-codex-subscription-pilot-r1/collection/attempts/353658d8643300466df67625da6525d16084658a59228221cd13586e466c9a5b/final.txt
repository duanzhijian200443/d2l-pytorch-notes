A checksum is like a short identifying code written on a parcel before it is shipped. When the parcel arrives, the recipient recalculates the code from its contents and compares it with the original. If the codes differ, the parcel was probably damaged or altered in transit. However, matching checksums do not guarantee that every bit is correct: different contents can occasionally produce the same checksum, and a deliberate attacker may manipulate both the contents and checksum. Thus, checksums detect many accidental errors, but are not strong proof of authenticity.
