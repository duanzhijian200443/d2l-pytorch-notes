Every autumn, Bellweather held its lost-and-found sale beneath the clock tower. Umbrellas, gloves, spectacles, and lonely shoes covered long wooden tables while neighbors bargained and laughed.

But one object never received a price tag: a small red suitcase, scuffed at the corners and painted with a faded bluebird.

No one remembered finding it. Yet everyone claimed it mattered. Mrs. Vale said it had belonged to her brother. The baker remembered carrying it on his first journey. Even the mayor insisted it had waited beside him when he proposed to his wife.

At sunset, a traveler approached and touched the handle.

“Is this for sale?”

The whole town answered, “No.”

So the suitcase returned to the lost-and-found office, holding nothing inside but everyone’s favorite memory.
