A spinning ice skater speeds up by pulling their arms inward because angular momentum stays nearly constant when there is little external torque. Angular momentum equals moment of inertia times angular velocity. Pulling the arms closer reduces the skater’s moment of inertia, so angular velocity must increase to compensate. The skater’s mass remains the same, but its distribution moves closer to the rotation axis. Rotational kinetic energy increases because the skater’s muscles do work while pulling the arms inward.
