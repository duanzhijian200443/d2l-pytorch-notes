Use a **“one process, one page” checklist** in a shared tool your team already uses—Google Docs, Notion, or your existing project system. Avoid creating a separate handbook.

Start with three procedures that repeat often or cause costly mistakes, such as onboarding a customer, sending invoices, or closing the shop.

Give each page the same simple structure:
- **When to use it:** What triggers the procedure?
- **Owner:** Who keeps it accurate?
- **Steps:** Five to ten short, actionable instructions.
- **Done means:** What must be true when the task is finished?
- **Exceptions:** Who to ask when something unusual happens.
- **Links:** Relevant templates, forms, or screenshots.

Have the person doing the work draft the checklist during a real run, rather than asking someone to write it from memory. Then let a colleague follow it once and flag anything unclear.

Make maintenance part of using it: **if a step is wrong, fix it while doing the task—or leave a comment for the owner.** Put the checklist link directly in the recurring task or calendar reminder so people actually find it.

Once a quarter, spend 15 minutes reviewing your most-used checklists and archiving obsolete ones. Measure success by fewer questions and mistakes, not by how much documentation you produce.
