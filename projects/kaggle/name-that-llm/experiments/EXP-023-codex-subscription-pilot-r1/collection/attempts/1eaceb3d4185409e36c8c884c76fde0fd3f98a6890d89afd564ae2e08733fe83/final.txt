Dust swam in the attic light as Mara lifted the blue cup from a crate of chipped dishes.

“Donate pile,” Eli said.

“It’s nothing,” she agreed, but set it beside her knee.

He reached for it. She caught his wrist.

The cup was plain, its rim crazed with tiny white lines. Eli turned it over and saw the faded marker beneath: **M + E**, two crooked letters.

“You drank cocoa from it,” Mara said.

“You made me.” His laugh broke halfway through.

From another box, she pulled a yellowed hospital bracelet and tucked it quickly under a blanket. Eli looked away, then wiped his palm on his jeans.

“Fine,” he said. “Keep the stupid cup.”

Mara carried it downstairs herself, holding it carefully with both hands.
