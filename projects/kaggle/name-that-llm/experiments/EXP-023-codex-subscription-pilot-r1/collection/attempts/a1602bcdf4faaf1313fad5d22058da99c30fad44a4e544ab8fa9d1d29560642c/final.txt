A project leader should communicate the delay early, clearly, and without inventing a new deadline. Explain what has changed, why it affects the schedule, and what impact stakeholders should expect. Take responsibility where appropriate, but avoid blame or excessive detail.

Since the revised completion date is uncertain, provide a realistic range or identify the factors that must be resolved before a reliable date can be set. Describe the recovery actions underway, such as reallocating resources, reducing scope, or addressing a dependency. Most importantly, commit to a specific date for the next update—even if the final delivery date remains unknown.

For example:

> We will not meet the planned completion date of May 15 because integration testing uncovered issues that require further investigation. We are assessing the extent of the rework and cannot yet provide a dependable revised date. The team has prioritized diagnosis and added engineering support. We will share our findings, delivery options, and the best available forecast by May 10. Until then, I will communicate any material changes promptly.

This approach is transparent, accountable, and avoids false certainty.
