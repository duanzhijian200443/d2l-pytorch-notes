A seed can be alive without actively growing. It contains living cells and a tiny plant embryo, but it may remain dormant while conditions are unsuitable—such as when it is dry, cold, or lacks oxygen. During dormancy, its metabolism is very slow, helping it survive until conditions improve.

For example, a bean seed in a packet is alive if it can still germinate, even though it looks unchanged. Once planted in warm, moist soil, it absorbs water and begins active growth. The seed coat splits, a root emerges, and then a shoot grows upward toward light. So, being alive means having the capacity to grow; actively growing means using that capacity right now.
