“Charity,” Leah said, dropping the blue cup into the box.

Ben caught it before it struck the plates.

“It’s chipped.”

“I can see.”

They worked beneath the attic’s single bulb. Leah folded curtains; Ben wrapped the cup in a pillowcase.

“You’ve kept three boxes already.”

He unwrapped it and rubbed his thumb over the pale ring inside.

Leah reached for it. Ben turned away so sharply his elbow hit a rafter.

Neither laughed.

Downstairs, the kettle clicked off.

“I’ll make tea,” Leah said.

Ben held out the cup.

She took it, then paused at the stairs. Every morning, he had carried that cup up to their mother, one hand cupped beneath it.

“Come with me,” she said.
