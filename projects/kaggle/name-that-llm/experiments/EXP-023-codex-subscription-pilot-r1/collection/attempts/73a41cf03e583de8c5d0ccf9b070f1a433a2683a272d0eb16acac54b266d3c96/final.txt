Compressing a photograph reduces file size by discarding or simplifying visual information. Higher compression makes images quicker to upload, download, store, and share, but it can erase subtle texture, fine edges, smooth color gradients, and low-contrast details. In lossy formats such as JPEG, aggressive compression may also introduce blockiness, ringing around edges, or smeared areas, especially after repeated saves.

Lower compression preserves more of the original detail and supports larger prints, close cropping, and later editing, but requires more storage and bandwidth. The best balance depends on use: a small web thumbnail can tolerate noticeable compression, while archival images, professional prints, medical imagery, or photos intended for extensive editing should use minimal compression or a lossless/high-quality format.
