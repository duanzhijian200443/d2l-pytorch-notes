A pilot project often benefits from selection bias: enthusiastic volunteers are more motivated, adaptable, and willing to tolerate problems than the wider workforce. They may also receive extra training, resources, and attention that cannot be sustained at scale. When the project expands, it encounters varied skills, competing priorities, resistance to change, and different working conditions. Coordination and support become harder, too. Therefore, pilot success demonstrates what is possible under favorable conditions, not necessarily what will happen across the whole organization.
