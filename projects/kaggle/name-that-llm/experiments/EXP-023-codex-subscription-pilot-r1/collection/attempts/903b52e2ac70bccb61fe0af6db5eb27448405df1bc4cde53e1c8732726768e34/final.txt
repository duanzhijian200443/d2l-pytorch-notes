Explaining a historical person’s choices in context means examining the beliefs, pressures, institutions, and information available at the time. Context can clarify why an action seemed reasonable, necessary, or advantageous to that person. It does not, however, erase the action’s effects or remove moral responsibility. Excusing every consequence treats historical circumstances as a complete defense, implying that harm need not be judged because it was typical or understandable in its era. A balanced approach holds both ideas together: people are shaped by their contexts, yet they still make choices within those contexts, sometimes resisting prevailing norms or ignoring foreseeable harm. Historical understanding should therefore replace simplistic condemnation with analysis—not replace moral evaluation with unconditional forgiveness.
