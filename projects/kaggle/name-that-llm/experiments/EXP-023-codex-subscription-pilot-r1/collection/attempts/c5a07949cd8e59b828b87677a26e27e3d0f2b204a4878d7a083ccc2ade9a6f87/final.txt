A local search index is usually much faster and more scalable: documents are preprocessed, tokenized, and organized so queries return results in milliseconds. It also reduces CPU, disk, and battery usage during searches. Searching every document from scratch is simpler and guarantees results reflect the latest content, but it becomes increasingly slow as the collection grows and repeats expensive parsing work for every keystroke. Indexes require storage, update logic, and occasional rebuilding, so they add complexity and may briefly become stale.
