A folktale can change because each storyteller adapts it to a particular audience, place, and time. Names, settings, dialogue, and minor events may shift. A dangerous forest might become a crowded city, while a clever fox might become another locally familiar animal. Storytellers may also emphasize different lessons, add humor, or give a minor character a larger role.

Yet the tale remains recognizable through its underlying pattern: a similar conflict, key events, character roles, and resolution. For example, a story about an underestimated youngest child who passes three tests can retain its identity despite many surface changes. These stable elements act like a skeleton, while each retelling gives the story new clothing, keeping it both familiar and relevant.
