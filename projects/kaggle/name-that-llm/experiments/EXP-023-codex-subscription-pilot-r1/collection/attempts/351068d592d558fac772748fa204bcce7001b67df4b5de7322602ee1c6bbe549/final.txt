Satire that exposes a powerful institution uses humor to challenge authority, reveal hypocrisy, and encourage audiences to question systems that shape their lives. Its target is usually a government, corporation, religious organization, media outlet, or social elite, and the joke “punches up” by making power look absurd or corrupt. By contrast, humor that merely repeats a familiar stereotype depends on assumptions audiences already recognize about a group, often reducing people to traits based on race, gender, class, nationality, or other identity. Such humor may produce quick laughter, but it rarely offers insight or criticism; instead, it reinforces existing prejudices. The key difference is purpose and direction: meaningful satire scrutinizes power, while stereotypical humor often burdens the less powerful with ridicule.
