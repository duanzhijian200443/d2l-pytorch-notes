Mara was thirty when she found the book again, shelved under Natural History where it had always belonged. At eleven, she had written on a torn index card: *I come here when my mother is ill. Does anyone else hide in libraries?*

The reply, in blue ink, had appeared three years later.

*Yes. Tuesdays, mostly. I hope your mother gets better.*

They exchanged notes until Mara left for university. No names, only dates.

Now, at the returns desk, a man was arguing gently about a fine. When he turned, she noticed the blue ink on his fingers.

“This is unlikely,” she said, holding up the book.

He read the title, then looked at her for a moment. “Tuesdays,” he said.

They sat by the tall windows. Her mother had recovered, then died much later. His father had been the reason he hid. Neither story surprised the other.

Outside, rain ticked against the glass.

“I wondered what happened to you,” he said.

Mara nodded. “I wondered too.”

Then they began with their names.
