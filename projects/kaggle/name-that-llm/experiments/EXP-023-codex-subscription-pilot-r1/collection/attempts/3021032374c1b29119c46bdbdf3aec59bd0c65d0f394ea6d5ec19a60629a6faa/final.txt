An accessible reading order means assistive technologies, such as screen readers, encounter the document’s content in a logical sequence that matches its meaning and intended flow. This order should support users navigating headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, forms, and other elements. Visual placement alone does not guarantee accessibility because screen readers typically follow the document’s underlying structure or tagged sequence—not simply what appears highest, leftmost, or most prominent on screen. Poorly structured content can therefore sound confusing even when it looks visually correct.
