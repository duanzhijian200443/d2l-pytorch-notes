External dimensions describe only how much space each box occupies, not how much it can hold. Storage capacity depends on the internal dimensions, which are reduced by the thickness of the walls, base, and lid. A box with thicker walls has less usable length, width, and height inside. Because capacity is calculated by multiplying these three internal measurements, even modest increases in wall thickness can significantly reduce volume. Therefore, the boxes must be compared using their internal dimensions or measured internal volume.
