A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading, while being clear about how discussions will work. Requiring everyone to finish every book can create pressure and exclude people with limited time, different reading speeds, accessibility needs, or a genuine dislike of a selection. Someone who quit halfway may still offer valuable insight: what lost their interest, which assumptions felt unconvincing, or how the book compares with others they have read.

At the same time, a discussion centered only on partial impressions can frustrate members who finished and want to explore the ending, structure, and full themes. A good compromise is to divide the meeting. Begin with a spoiler-light conversation open to everyone, including those who stopped. Then move into a clearly announced spoiler section for readers who completed the book or do not mind knowing key plot points.

The club can also normalize brief check-ins: participants may say where they stopped and why, without needing to defend themselves. This approach protects discussion depth while making the group more welcoming and sustainable.
