Tree roots and mycorrhizal fungi often form mutualistic partnerships. Fungal threads extend beyond roots, improving access to phosphorus, nitrogen, water, and minerals; in return, trees supply the fungi with sugars or other carbon compounds. Networks may connect multiple plants, allowing some transfer of nutrients or carbon, especially between related trees or shaded seedlings, though the amounts and ecological importance vary. Evidence does not show forests possessing human-like thoughts, intentions, or communication. “Wood-wide web” is a useful metaphor, but claims of conscious forest behavior are exaggerated.
