Mara lifted the kiln door and leaned back as the heat rushed over her face. Inside, rows of bowls glowed beneath the ash, their familiar blue glaze deepening toward the rims. She smiled—tiredly, automatically—then began removing them one by one.

The last bowl made her stop.

Green.

Not the pale, deliberate green she had tested in the winter, but a rich, living color, like moss after rain. She turned it in her hands, searching for a mistake: a drip, a crack, some flaw that would explain the surprise. There was none. The bowl seemed to hold its own quiet light.

Mara set it apart from the others. For several moments she stood with her hands suspended, unwilling to touch anything else. Then she carried the green bowl across the studio to the front window.

Daylight found it. The glaze brightened, and a soft reflection trembled across her face.

Mara laughed once, almost in disbelief. She had opened the kiln expecting proof that she knew what she was doing. Instead, the bowl had given her something better: permission to be surprised.
