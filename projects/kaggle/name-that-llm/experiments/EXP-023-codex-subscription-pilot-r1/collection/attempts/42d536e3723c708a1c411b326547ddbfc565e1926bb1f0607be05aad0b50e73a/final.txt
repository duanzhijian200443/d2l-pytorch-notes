Use **one-page “living checklists”** for recurring work instead of a large operations manual.

For each procedure—opening the store, invoicing, onboarding a client, handling returns—create a short page with:

- **When to use it**
- **Who owns it**
- **Step-by-step checklist**
- **Links/templates** needed to complete it
- **Common exceptions**
- **Last reviewed date**

Keep these where staff already work: a shared Google Drive folder, Notion workspace, or project-management tool. Avoid a separate system that people must remember to open.

Make updating part of the procedure itself. Add a final checklist item: **“If anything was unclear or changed, update this page before closing the task.”** Assign one owner per procedure, but let anyone suggest edits.

Start with only the 5–10 processes that cause the most repeated questions, mistakes, or training time. Record a quick screen video for software-heavy tasks and link it from the checklist rather than writing long explanations.

Review each checklist briefly every quarter—or immediately after a problem, policy change, or new hire struggles with it. Delete obsolete steps instead of preserving history.

The goal is not perfect documentation. It is a practical, current aid someone can follow successfully on a busy day.
