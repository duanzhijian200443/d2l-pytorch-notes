“You fold it wrong,” Mara said, watching Eli crease the map into a bright, crumpled star.

“It has too many screens,” he replied.

“No screens. Rivers, roads, contour lines.”

Eli held it up. “So it doesn’t tell you where you are?”

“It tells you where you might be, if you learn to look.”

She traced a blue thread with one finger. “This river bends beside the old mill. That hill has three ridges. Find those, and the world answers back.”

“My phone does that faster.”

“Your phone gives directions,” Mara said. “A map gives shape. It lets you see the road you didn’t take.”

Eli studied the faded paper again. “Did you make this?”

“I did.”

He folded it more carefully. “Then maybe it should come with instructions.”
