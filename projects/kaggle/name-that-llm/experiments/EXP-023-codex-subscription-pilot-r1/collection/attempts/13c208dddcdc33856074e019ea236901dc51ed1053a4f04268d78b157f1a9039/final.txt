A geologist can determine the relative sequence of events by applying the principles of superposition and cross-cutting relationships. In an undisturbed stack of sedimentary rocks, the lowest layer formed first, and each higher layer is progressively younger. The geologist first orders the layers from oldest at the bottom to youngest at the top. Next, the crack and the rock filling it are examined. Because the crack cuts across existing layers, it must have formed after those layers were deposited and hardened. The material filling the crack is younger still, because it entered after the crack opened. If later layers cover the crack without being cut, those layers formed afterward. Thus, the sequence is: deposition of the cut layers, cracking, filling of the crack, and deposition of any uncut overlying layers.
