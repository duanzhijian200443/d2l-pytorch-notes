A famous performer can create instant visibility. Their name may boost ticket sales, attract sponsors, generate media coverage, and draw visitors who would not otherwise attend. For a newer or struggling festival, that headline appeal can reduce financial uncertainty and establish credibility. However, the festival becomes highly dependent on one act: a cancellation, weak audience fit, or unexpectedly high production demands can undermine the whole event. It may also leave little budget for programming, local vendors, or community activities.

Several local performers offer a different kind of value. They can showcase the region’s culture, build goodwill, and create a more varied program that appeals to different audiences. Multiple acts also spread risk: if one performance disappoints, the event still has others. Local artists may be easier to collaborate with on workshops, school events, and promotional activity, helping the festival become a year-round community asset. The downside is that marketing may be harder, especially if the festival needs tourists or major press attention.

The best choice depends on the festival’s goal. If the priority is rapid growth, tourism, or relaunching the event, the famous performer may justify the cost. If the priority is community identity, long-term sustainability, and broad participation, several local performers are likely the stronger investment.
