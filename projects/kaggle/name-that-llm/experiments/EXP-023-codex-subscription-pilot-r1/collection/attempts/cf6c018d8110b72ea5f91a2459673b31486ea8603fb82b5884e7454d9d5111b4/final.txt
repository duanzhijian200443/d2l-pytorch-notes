A seed can be alive without actively growing. A living seed contains a tiny plant embryo whose cells are still viable, but its activity may be extremely slow. In this resting state, called dormancy, it uses very little energy and can survive until conditions are suitable.

For example, a dry bean seed in a packet is alive, even though it does not get bigger or produce leaves. When it receives enough water, oxygen, and warmth, it begins to germinate. The seed absorbs water, its cells become more active, and it uses stored food for growth. A root emerges first, followed by a shoot.

So, being alive means the seed can sustain life; actively growing means it is developing new cells and structures.
