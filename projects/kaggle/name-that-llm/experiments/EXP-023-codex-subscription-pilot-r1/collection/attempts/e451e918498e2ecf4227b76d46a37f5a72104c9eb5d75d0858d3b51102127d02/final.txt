Restoring missing parts of an ancient object aims to recreate its original appearance or function. Conservators may add new material to stabilize a vessel, complete a sculpture’s form, or make inscriptions easier to understand. Ideally, these additions are documented, distinguishable on close inspection, and reversible, so future specialists can reassess them.

Displaying gaps openly takes a different approach. It preserves the object’s surviving material without speculating about what has been lost. Breaks, missing fragments, and worn areas remain visible as evidence of age, use, excavation, and historical change. This can feel less visually complete, but it emphasizes authenticity and avoids presenting uncertain reconstructions as fact. The choice depends on an object’s condition, purpose, cultural context, and the degree of confidence in any proposed restoration.
