Decide first whether the meeting has a clear purpose that requires real-time collaboration. A meeting may be worthwhile for making a decision, resolving complex issues, coordinating interdependent work, or building shared understanding. It is usually unnecessary when the goal is simply to share information, provide status updates, or collect routine input; use a concise written update, dashboard, or asynchronous discussion instead.

Estimate the meeting’s total cost, including preparation and participants’ time, and compare it with the value of the expected outcome. Before scheduling weekly, test a shorter or less frequent format, define a specific agenda, assign an owner, and require preparation. After several sessions, assess whether decisions are being made and work is improving. If not, cancel, redesign, or replace it asynchronously.
