Restoring missing parts of an ancient object aims to recreate a sense of completeness. Conservators may fill cracks, replace fragments, or reconstruct lost forms using compatible, often distinguishable materials. This can make the object easier to understand and closer to its presumed original appearance, but it also introduces interpretation and risks obscuring its history.

Displaying gaps openly takes a more restrained approach. Missing sections, breaks, and wear remain visible as evidence of age, damage, excavation, or earlier use. Supports may stabilize the object without imitating what has been lost. This approach emphasizes authenticity and allows viewers to distinguish surviving material from absence. Ultimately, restoration prioritizes visual coherence and interpretation, while open display prioritizes transparency, material evidence, and the object’s changing history.
