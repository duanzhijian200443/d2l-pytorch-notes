A city walking tour should balance celebrated landmarks with overlooked everyday places. Famous monuments provide historical context, shared reference points, and memorable architecture. Yet markets, courtyards, transit stops, corner cafés, and residential streets reveal how residents actually experience the city. Emphasizing only landmarks can make a tour feel predictable; focusing solely on ordinary spaces may disappoint first-time visitors. The strongest route connects both, using iconic sites as anchors while letting quieter places illustrate local routines, social change, and the city’s distinctive character.
