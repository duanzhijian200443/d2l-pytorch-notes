A battery stores energy chemically, in materials arranged so that chemical reactions can occur at two electrodes. When a circuit is completed, those reactions separate and move charged particles within the battery, maintaining a voltage difference between its terminals. That voltage establishes an electric field through the flashlight’s wires and bulb.

In a small flashlight, electrons already present in the metal conductors begin drifting through the circuit in response to that field. Energy is transferred through the electrical circuit to the bulb’s filament or LED, where it becomes light and heat. Meanwhile, the battery’s chemical reactants are gradually converted into lower-energy products. The battery does not contain a supply of “electricity” that gets used up; it contains chemical energy and a structure that can drive charge motion until the reactants can no longer sustain the voltage.
