A city walking tour should balance celebrated landmarks with overlooked everyday places, but give the latter more attention. Famous monuments provide historical context and shared reference points, especially for first-time visitors. Yet neighborhood markets, modest cafés, public gardens, and ordinary streets reveal how residents actually live. These places can make a tour feel less like a checklist and more like an encounter with the city’s character. Ideally, landmarks anchor the route, while everyday spaces supply its texture, surprises, and human stories.
