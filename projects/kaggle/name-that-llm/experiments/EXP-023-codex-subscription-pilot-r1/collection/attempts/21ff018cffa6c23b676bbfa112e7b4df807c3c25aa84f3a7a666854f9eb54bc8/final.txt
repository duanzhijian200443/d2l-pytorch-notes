A queue buffers incoming requests when an online service is temporarily busier than its workers can handle. Instead of rejecting every extra request or overwhelming the application, requests are placed in an ordered backlog. Workers remove jobs from the queue at a controlled rate as capacity becomes available.

For example, an e-commerce site might queue email confirmations, image processing, or payment follow-up tasks. The customer-facing request can finish quickly, while slower work happens asynchronously.

Queues smooth traffic spikes, protect downstream systems, and allow workers to scale independently. They also support retries when a job fails. However, queues add delay, so services need limits, monitoring, and clear handling for requests that expire or cannot be completed.
