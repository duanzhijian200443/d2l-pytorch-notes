Satire that exposes a powerful institution “punches up.” It uses irony, exaggeration, or parody to reveal hypocrisy, corruption, or structural injustice in governments, corporations, media, or other influential systems. Its humor invites audiences to question authority and may give language to experiences that power would rather conceal.

Humor that merely repeats a familiar stereotype usually “punches down” or sideways. Instead of uncovering something hidden, it recycles assumptions about a social group—often reducing complex people to a predictable trait. Even when presented as harmless, such jokes can normalize prejudice because their punch line depends on the stereotype already being accepted.

The key distinction is not whether anyone feels offended, but what the joke illuminates: abuses of power, or inherited bias. Strong satire challenges easy beliefs; weak stereotype humor relies on them.
