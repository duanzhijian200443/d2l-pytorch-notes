A historian may use several incomplete sources because no single account is entirely neutral, accurate, or complete. Each source reflects its author’s perspective, purpose, knowledge, and limitations. By comparing diaries, official records, newspapers, letters, material evidence, and later interpretations, historians can identify agreements, contradictions, omissions, and biases. These gaps can themselves reveal whose voices were ignored or what people wanted to conceal. Combining sources also allows historians to cross-check facts and build a more balanced explanation of the past. A supposedly “perfect” account is unlikely to exist, since historical events are complex and witnesses experience them differently. Therefore, using multiple imperfect sources produces a more reliable and nuanced understanding than relying on one apparently authoritative narrative.
