A battery stores usable energy in the chemical arrangement of its materials, not as a supply of “electricity.” Its separated reactants have the potential to undergo reactions that release energy. The battery’s structure keeps those reactions from proceeding freely until a conducting path connects its terminals.

When you switch on a flashlight, you complete that path. Chemical reactions inside the battery drive electrons through the external circuit, while ions move within the battery to maintain charge balance. The electrons are already present in the wires; the battery does not create them. Energy is transferred electrically to the flashlight’s bulb or LED, which converts it into light and heat. As the reactions proceed, the battery’s chemical composition changes and its remaining usable energy decreases.
