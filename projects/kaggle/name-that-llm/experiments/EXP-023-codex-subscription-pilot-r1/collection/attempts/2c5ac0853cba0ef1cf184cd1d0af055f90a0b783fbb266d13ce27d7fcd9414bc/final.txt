An unreliable narrator makes readers active interpreters because the story cannot simply be accepted at face value. Whether the narrator lies, misunderstands events, forgets details, or reveals personal biases, readers must look for gaps between what is reported and what may actually be happening. Contradictions, other characters’ reactions, and subtle clues become evidence to weigh. This turns reading into a process of questioning, comparing, and revising judgments rather than merely receiving information. Readers may also examine their own assumptions: why did they trust this voice, and what persuaded them to doubt it? Because uncertainty can support several plausible interpretations, readers help construct the story’s meaning. That intellectual and emotional participation can make its discoveries more compelling and its ambiguities linger.
