A queue acts as a waiting area between incoming requests and the workers that process them. When traffic briefly exceeds the service’s processing capacity, requests are stored instead of immediately overwhelming servers or being rejected. Workers pull requests from the queue at a sustainable rate, smoothing traffic spikes and keeping resource usage stable.

Queues also support retries when processing fails, distribute work across multiple workers, and enable capacity to scale independently. However, they do not create unlimited capacity. If requests arrive faster than they are processed for too long, the queue grows, latency increases, and storage may fill. Services therefore use queue limits, timeouts, prioritization, monitoring, autoscaling, and backpressure. Together, these mechanisms improve reliability while ensuring overload is controlled rather than allowed to cascade through the system.
