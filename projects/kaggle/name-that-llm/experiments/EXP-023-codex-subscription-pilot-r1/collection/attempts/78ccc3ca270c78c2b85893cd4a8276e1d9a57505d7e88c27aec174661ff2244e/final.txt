By noon, Daniel had apologized to the kettle, the bathroom mirror, and the empty passenger seat.

“I should have asked before telling them.”

No excuses. He’d crossed those out on the back of an electricity bill.

At the café, his sister was already pulling the paper sleeve off her cup.

“Before you say anything,” he began, “I know it wasn’t mine to—”

“Do you remember Mum’s green suitcase?”

He stopped. “The one with the broken wheel?”

“I’ve been looking everywhere. I thought you might have it.”

“It’s in my cupboard.”

“Can I borrow it?”

Daniel folded the bill beneath the table. “You’re going, then?”

“I think so.” She flattened the cardboard sleeve. “I nearly didn’t apply. Then everyone knew, and I hated that. But I also couldn’t keep pretending I didn’t want it.”

“I’m sorry I told them.”

“I know.” She looked up. “Next time, let me have something for a bit before it belongs to the whole family.”

“I will.”

For a moment neither spoke. Daniel had prepared for anger, forgiveness, several kinds of silence. He hadn’t prepared for the suitcase.

“The wheel catches,” he said.

“I remember.”

“I could fix it before you leave.”

“You could try.”

She pushed her untouched biscuit across the table. He broke it in two and pushed half back.
