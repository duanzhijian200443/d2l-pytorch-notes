At dawn, Mara found the recipe penciled into the margin of her grandfather’s account book, between debts for flour and coal. She measured by its old instructions—“a fist of yeast, patience, and enough water to make the dough remember itself.” The bakery filled with the warm, sweet smell of rising bread. At first, the scent made her think of customers and coins, of keeping the shop alive. By sunrise, loaves browned in the oven, and she heard a faint tapping from the back room. It was only the cooling pipes, she told herself. Yet the smell had changed: beneath the butter and crust was her grandfather’s tobacco, the same scent that clung to his coat when he bent to teach her how to knead.
