Compressing a photograph into a smaller file saves storage space and makes uploads, downloads, and sharing faster. However, compression—especially lossy compression such as JPEG—reduces image information by simplifying colors, textures, and fine edges. At moderate levels, the loss may be barely noticeable, but stronger compression can produce blurred details, blocky artifacts, banding, and unnatural textures, particularly in shadows, hair, foliage, and other complex areas. Lossless formats preserve every pixel but usually create larger files and offer less space savings. The best balance depends on the image’s purpose: higher compression may suit web thumbnails or casual sharing, while lower compression or lossless storage is preferable for printing, editing, archiving, and photographs where subtle detail matters.
