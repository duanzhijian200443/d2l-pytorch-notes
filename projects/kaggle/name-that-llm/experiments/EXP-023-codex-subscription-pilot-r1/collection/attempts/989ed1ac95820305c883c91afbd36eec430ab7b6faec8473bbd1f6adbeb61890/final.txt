Natural selection can change shell-color frequency through inherited variation and differences in survival. Imagine a snail population containing light- and dark-shelled individuals. If the environment makes dark shells harder for predators to see, dark-shelled snails are more likely to survive and reproduce. Their offspring inherit the genes influencing dark shells. Over many generations, dark-shelled snails contribute more genes to the population, so dark shells become more common. If the environment changes—for example, if the background becomes lighter—the advantage may shift to light shells instead. No individual snail chooses or tries to evolve. Instead, random inherited differences already exist, and natural selection automatically increases traits that help organisms survive and reproduce in a particular environment.
