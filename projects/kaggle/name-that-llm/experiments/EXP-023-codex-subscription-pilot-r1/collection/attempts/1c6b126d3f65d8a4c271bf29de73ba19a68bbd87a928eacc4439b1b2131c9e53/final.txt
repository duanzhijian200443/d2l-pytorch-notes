A geologist can reconstruct the relative sequence using several principles of stratigraphy. First, in an undisturbed stack of sedimentary layers, the law of superposition says the oldest layer is at the bottom and progressively younger layers lie above it. If a crack cuts across those layers, the crack is younger than every layer it crosses, according to the principle of cross-cutting relationships. If the crack later became filled with an igneous vein or another deposited rock, that filling occurred after the crack formed. The filled vein is therefore younger than the surrounding layers, although the crack and its filling may represent separate events. Any rock fragments enclosed in the filling are older than the material that surrounds them.
