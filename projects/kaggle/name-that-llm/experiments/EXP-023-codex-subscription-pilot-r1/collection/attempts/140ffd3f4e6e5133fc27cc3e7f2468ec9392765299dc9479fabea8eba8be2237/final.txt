Reversible image editing preserves the original and stores changes separately, using layers, masks, adjustment settings, or edit history. You can undo decisions, refine individual effects, and create alternative versions without losing source detail. Repeatedly overwriting the original permanently commits changes, making earlier versions difficult or impossible to recover without backups. With lossy formats such as JPEG, repeated saving can also degrade image quality. Reversible workflows may require more storage and compatible software, but they offer greater flexibility, safety, and control.
