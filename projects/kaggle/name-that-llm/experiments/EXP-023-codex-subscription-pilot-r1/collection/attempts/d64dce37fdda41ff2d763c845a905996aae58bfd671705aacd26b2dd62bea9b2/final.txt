Before dawn, Mara unlocked the bakery and opened her grandfather’s account book. Beside a column of unpaid flour bills, he had penciled a recipe: *three handfuls, warm milk, patience*. No temperature, no timing—only a small star beside the word *wait*.

She kneaded until the dough felt like an earlobe, then watched it rise beneath a towel. The room smelled faintly of smoke. Alarmed, she checked the oven twice, the chimney once.

By sunrise, twelve loaves cooled on the counter, their crusts crackling like kindling. The first customer tore one open and smiled.

“That smell,” he said. “Just like your grandfather’s.”

Mara breathed again. The smoke was not something burning. It was the old brick oven remembering.
