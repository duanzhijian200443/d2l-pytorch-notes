A public library should balance preservation with renewal. A few worn but beloved books deserve to remain if readers still borrow them, especially when they hold local significance or are difficult to replace. Rather than discarding them automatically, the library could repair or rebind usable copies. However, shelf space and budgets are limited, so keeping every aging title may prevent the collection from serving changing interests. Circulation data, reader requests, and community feedback can guide decisions about which books stay. Newer titles should be added to attract different age groups, reflect diverse experiences, and provide current information. The best approach is not simply old versus new: retain meaningful favorites, replace damaged copies when possible, and regularly refresh the collection so it remains both familiar and inviting.
