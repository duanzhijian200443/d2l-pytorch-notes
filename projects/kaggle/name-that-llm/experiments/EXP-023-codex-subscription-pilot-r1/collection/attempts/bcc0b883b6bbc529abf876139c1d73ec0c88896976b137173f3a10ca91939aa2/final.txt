Every evening, Mara practiced alone in the old concert hall.

After the last audience had gone and the city darkened beyond the tall windows, she climbed onto the stage with her violin. She played scales first, then the difficult concerto she had been preparing for months. The empty seats absorbed her mistakes without judgment. Only the steady hush of the cleaning machines accompanied her.

One night, halfway through the final movement, she noticed a figure near the back doors. It was the cleaner, Mr. Ilyan, holding his mop and listening.

Mara lowered her bow. “I’m sorry. Am I keeping you late?”

He shook his head. “No. I wait for this part.”

“The concerto?”

“The moment when you stop being afraid of it.”

She stared at him, embarrassed. “You’ve been listening?”

“Every evening,” he said. “At first, I heard someone trying to play perfectly. Then I heard someone trying to tell the truth.”

Mara looked across the rows of empty seats. She had imagined that her music disappeared into the dark as soon as it left her instrument. Yet someone had been carrying it home, night after night.

“Would you like to hear the ending?” she asked.

Mr. Ilyan leaned on his mop and smiled. “I’ve been waiting.”

So Mara raised her violin again. This time, the hall did not feel empty at all.
