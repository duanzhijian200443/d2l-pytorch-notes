A puddle disappears through evaporation, which does not require boiling. Even on a cool day, water molecules have a range of speeds because they are constantly moving and colliding. A small fraction at the surface occasionally has enough energy to overcome attractions from neighboring molecules and escape into the air as water vapor. As these higher-energy molecules leave, others gain energy through collisions and can escape in turn. Wind carries vapor away, low humidity allows more vapor to enter the air, and sunlight or warmth can speed the process, but none is essential. Boiling is different: it occurs when vapor bubbles can form throughout the liquid, requiring much higher temperatures at ordinary pressure. Evaporation happens only at the surface and can occur at almost any temperature.
