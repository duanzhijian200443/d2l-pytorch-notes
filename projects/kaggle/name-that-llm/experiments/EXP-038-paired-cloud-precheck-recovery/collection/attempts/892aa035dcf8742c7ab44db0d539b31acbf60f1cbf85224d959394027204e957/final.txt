Test a “Staff Discovery Shelf” in one high-traffic section for six weeks, featuring 12 unfamiliar authors selected to match the genres already popular in that area. Keep the regular shelving system unchanged so customers can still find known authors easily; the display should be clearly labeled as an optional browsing stop and include a short, practical tag for each book, such as “For readers who like Tana French” or “Fast historical mystery, under 300 pages.” Rotate four titles every two weeks while retaining eight, allowing the store to compare steady versus newly introduced recommendations. Use a similar nearby section without a discovery shelf as a control. Track unit sales, browsing interactions observed during two scheduled sample periods each week, and whether displayed authors generate follow-up purchases elsewhere in the store. At checkout, staff can ask a small sample of customers whether they noticed the display and whether it helped them find something new, without pressuring them to answer. Success would mean increased sales of featured unfamiliar authors without a drop in sales of established authors or customer complaints about finding books. If results are positive, expand the concept gradually to additional genres.
