Historians distinguish them because they were created under different conditions and preserve different kinds of evidence. A contemporary diary may record immediate perceptions, uncertainties, and details before later events reshape interpretation, but it can still be selective, mistaken, or self-serving. A memoir benefits from hindsight and may explain broader significance, yet memory can fade, merge events, or be influenced by later beliefs and audiences. Both require comparison with other sources, attention to purpose, and contextual analysis.
