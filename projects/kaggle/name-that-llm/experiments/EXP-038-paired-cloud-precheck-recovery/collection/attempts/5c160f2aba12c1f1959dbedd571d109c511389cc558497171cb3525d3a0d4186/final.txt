Historians distinguish a contemporary diary from a memoir written decades later because each reflects different conditions of remembering and recording. A diary is usually closer in time to events, preserving immediate perceptions, emotions, and uncertainties, but it may be incomplete, private, or shaped by the writer’s circumstances. A later memoir can offer broader context and reflection, yet memory may fade, change, or be influenced by later beliefs, reputational concerns, and knowledge of outcomes. Historians therefore compare both with other evidence rather than treating either as automatically truthful.
