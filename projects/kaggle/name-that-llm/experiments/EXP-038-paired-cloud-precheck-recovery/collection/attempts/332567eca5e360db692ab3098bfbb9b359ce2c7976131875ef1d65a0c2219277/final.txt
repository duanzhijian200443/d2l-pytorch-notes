The first room begins with twelve chairs. When four chairs are moved to the second room, subtract four from twelve: 12 − 4 = 8, so there are eight chairs left in the first room at that intermediate point. Next, three of the chairs are brought back from the second room into the first room. Add those three chairs to the eight already there: 8 + 3 = 11. Therefore, after both movements have occurred, eleven chairs remain in the first room. The key is to account for each action in order: first the number decreases because chairs leave, and then it increases because some chairs return. The second room’s final total is not needed to determine the answer.
