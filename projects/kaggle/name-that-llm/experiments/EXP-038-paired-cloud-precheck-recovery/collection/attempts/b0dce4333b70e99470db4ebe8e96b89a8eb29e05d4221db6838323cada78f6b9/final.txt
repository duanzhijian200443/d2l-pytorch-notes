Historians distinguish a contemporary diary from a later memoir because each was created under different circumstances and offers different kinds of evidence. A diary may record immediate impressions, details, and uncertainties close to the events, but it can be incomplete, biased, or shaped by what the writer knew then. A memoir benefits from hindsight and broader context, yet memory can fade, change, or be influenced by later experiences and public narratives. Historians therefore compare both sources with other evidence and examine their purposes, audiences, and reliability.
