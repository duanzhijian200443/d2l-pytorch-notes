A puddle disappears through evaporation, not boiling. Even on a cool day, water molecules are constantly moving with a range of speeds. At the surface, some have enough kinetic energy to overcome the attractive forces holding them in the liquid and escape into the air as water vapor. This can happen at any temperature; boiling is different because it occurs when vapor bubbles can form throughout the liquid. As faster molecules leave, the remaining water loses energy and cools slightly, while heat from sunlight, air, ground, or surroundings replaces that energy. Dry air, wind, and a large exposed surface speed evaporation by carrying vapor away, whereas humid, still air slows it. Eventually, the water spreads, evaporates, and becomes invisible vapor.
