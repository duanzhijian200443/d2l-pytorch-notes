Historians distinguish them because they were created under different conditions and answer different questions. A contemporary diary may preserve immediate impressions, uncertainties, and details later forgotten, but it can still be biased, selective, mistaken, or written for an expected audience. A memoir benefits from hindsight and may explain broader significance, yet decades of memory, later beliefs, political motives, and public reputation can reshape recollection. Neither is automatically reliable; historians compare each with other evidence and consider purpose, audience, timing, and context.
