A password manager reduces the burden of remembering many passwords by securely storing login credentials in an encrypted vault and filling them in when needed. Instead of inventing and memorizing a different password for every website, the user can generate long, random, unique passwords and let the manager retain them. This improves security because a password stolen from one service is less likely to unlock accounts elsewhere. Many managers also organize accounts, synchronize credentials across approved devices, warn about weak or reused passwords, and may store recovery codes or payment details. However, the user still has important responsibilities. They must create and protect a strong, unique master password, enable multifactor authentication where available, keep recovery information safe, and avoid sharing vault access carelessly. They should also remain alert to phishing sites, since a password manager cannot fully protect someone who willingly enters credentials into a convincing fake page.
