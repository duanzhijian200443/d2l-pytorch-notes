Run a six-week “neighbor authors” experiment in one clearly marked section of the bookstore, keeping the main shelving system unchanged. Next to a small selection of familiar, high-traffic authors, place one or two books by less familiar authors whose genre, themes, or style are genuinely similar, using shelf talkers that briefly explain the connection, such as “If you enjoy Octavia Butler, try N. K. Jemisin.” Keep the unfamiliar books in their normal alphabetical or subject locations as well, so customers can still navigate predictably and staff can restock easily. Choose comparable titles in a similar price range, and alternate which recommendations appear every two weeks to reduce the effect of particular books or seasonal demand. Track weekly sales of the featured unfamiliar authors, sales of comparable books in a similar section without the display, and the number of books sold per featured title. Ask customers at checkout, through a one-question card or QR code, whether the recommendation helped them discover something new. Staff should record notable customer comments but avoid aggressively directing shoppers, preserving natural behavior. After six weeks, compare featured and non-featured sales, survey responses, and any effect on neighboring books. If discovery rises without causing confusion or reducing overall category sales, expand the approach gradually; otherwise, revise the pairings, wording, or placement and repeat the test.
