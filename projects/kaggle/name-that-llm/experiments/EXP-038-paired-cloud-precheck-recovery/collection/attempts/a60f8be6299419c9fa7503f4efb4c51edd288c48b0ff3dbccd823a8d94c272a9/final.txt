Dust feathered the attic light as Mara filled the donation box and Eli pried open old cartons. He found the blue cup beneath a stack of schoolbooks, plain except for a chipped rim. “Trash,” Mara said, reaching for it. Eli pulled it against his chest. “It’s a cup.” “Exactly.” She tugged; it slipped, clinking on the floor. Both froze. Eli knelt first, turning it upright with shaking fingers. Mara crouched beside him and rubbed a thumb over the faded marker line near the bottom: MOM’S TEA, in their father’s blocky handwriting. Eli set the cup on the keep pile. Mara said nothing, but moved the donation box farther away. Then she carried the cup downstairs herself, cupped carefully in both hands.
