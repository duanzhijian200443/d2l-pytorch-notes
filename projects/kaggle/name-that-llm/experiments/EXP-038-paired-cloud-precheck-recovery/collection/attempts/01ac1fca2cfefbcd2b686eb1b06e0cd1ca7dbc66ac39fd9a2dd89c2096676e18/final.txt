Historians distinguish a contemporary diary from a memoir written decades later because time affects perspective, memory, and purpose. A diary usually records impressions close to when events occurred, preserving immediate details but also confusion, limited knowledge, and personal bias. A memoir benefits from reflection and broader information, yet memories may fade, change, or be shaped by later experiences, reputational concerns, and hindsight. Neither source is automatically truthful; historians compare each with other evidence, examine its context, and consider the author’s motives and access to information.
