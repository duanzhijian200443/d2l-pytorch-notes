A puddle can disappear on a cool day because evaporation happens at all temperatures, not only when water boils. Boiling occurs throughout the liquid when its vapor pressure becomes high enough, but evaporation happens only at the surface, where some water molecules are moving fast enough to escape into the air. Even in cool water, a small fraction of molecules have enough energy to leave. Wind removes the moist air above the puddle, while dry air, sunlight, and a large exposed surface can increase the rate. As the fastest molecules escape, the remaining water loses energy and may cool slightly, but evaporation continues. Over time, enough molecules leave the puddle and mix with the atmosphere that the puddle gradually vanishes without ever approaching boiling temperature.
