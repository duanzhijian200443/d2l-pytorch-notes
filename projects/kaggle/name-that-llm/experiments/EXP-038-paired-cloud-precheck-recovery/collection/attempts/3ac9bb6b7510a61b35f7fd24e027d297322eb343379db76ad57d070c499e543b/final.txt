Historians distinguish them because they were produced under different conditions and therefore offer different kinds of evidence. A contemporary diary may preserve immediate impressions, uncertainties, and details that later fade, but it can still reflect limited knowledge, self-censorship, or personal bias. A memoir written decades later benefits from hindsight and broader reflection, yet memory may be reshaped by later experiences, public narratives, and the author’s purposes. Neither is accepted uncritically; each is compared with context and other sources.
