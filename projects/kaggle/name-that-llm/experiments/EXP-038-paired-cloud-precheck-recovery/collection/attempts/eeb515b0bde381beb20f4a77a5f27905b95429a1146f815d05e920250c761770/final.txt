The room initially has twelve chairs. When four chairs are moved to the second room, subtract four from the original twelve: 12 − 4 = 8. At that intermediate point, eight chairs remain in the first room, while four chairs are in the second room. Then, three chairs are brought back from the second room to the first room. Add those three returning chairs to the eight already there: 8 + 3 = 11. Therefore, after both movements are complete, eleven chairs remain in the first room. The second room has one of the original chairs left there, because it received four chairs but returned three. The final number in the first room is thus eleven chairs, not twelve, since one chair ultimately stayed in the second room.
