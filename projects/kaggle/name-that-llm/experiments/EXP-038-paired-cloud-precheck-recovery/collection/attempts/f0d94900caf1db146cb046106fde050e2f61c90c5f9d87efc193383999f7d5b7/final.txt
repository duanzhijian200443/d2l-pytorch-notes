Run a six-week pilot in one high-traffic fiction section: keep the existing alphabetical shelves unchanged, but add a small, clearly labeled “Try Someone New” face-out display at the aisle entrance featuring 12 books by authors customers are less likely to know. Select titles across popular genres and price points, refresh half the display every two weeks, and use simple shelf cards stating “If you like [well-known author], try [new author]” plus a one-sentence appeal. Avoid moving regular stock or creating extra category systems, so customers can still browse normally. Alternate the display by week between the pilot section and a comparable control section, or compare it with the same section’s prior six weeks. Track unit sales, browsing interactions through brief staff tallies, QR-code scans linking to short recommendations, and the share of purchases containing a featured author. At checkout, invite a small random sample to answer one optional question: whether the display helped them find an author they had not read before. Success would mean a meaningful lift in featured-author sales and discovery responses without lower sales of established authors or more requests for help locating books. If results are positive, repeat the format in another department.
