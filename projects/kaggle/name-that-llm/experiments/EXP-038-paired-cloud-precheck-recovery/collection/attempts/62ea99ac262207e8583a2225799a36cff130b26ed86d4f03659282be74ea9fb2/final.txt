A puddle can disappear on a cool day because evaporation happens at temperatures far below water’s boiling point. Boiling occurs throughout the water when its vapor pressure matches the surrounding air pressure, but evaporation happens only at the surface. Even in cool water, some molecules move faster than others. A few energetic surface molecules can escape into the air as vapor. As these molecules leave, the remaining water may cool slightly, but evaporation continues as long as the air is not fully humid. Wind, dry air, sunlight, and the puddle’s large exposed surface can speed the process by carrying away water vapor and replacing it with drier air. Given enough time, evaporation removes all the water without requiring it to become hot.
