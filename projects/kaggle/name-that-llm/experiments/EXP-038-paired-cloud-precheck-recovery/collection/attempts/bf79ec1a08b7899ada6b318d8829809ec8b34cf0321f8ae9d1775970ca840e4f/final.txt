Historians distinguish them because they were created under different conditions and serve different evidentiary purposes. A contemporary diary may preserve immediate impressions, uncertainty, and details later forgotten, but it can still be biased, selective, mistaken, or written for an anticipated audience. A memoir benefits from hindsight and may explain longer-term meanings, yet memory can reshape events through later beliefs, personal interests, and public narratives. Neither is accepted uncritically; historians compare each with other sources, context, authorship, and internal inconsistencies.
