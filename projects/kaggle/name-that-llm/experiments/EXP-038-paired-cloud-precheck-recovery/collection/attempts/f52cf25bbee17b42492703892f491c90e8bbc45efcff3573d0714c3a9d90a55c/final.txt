A puddle disappears because evaporation occurs at all temperatures, not only at boiling. Water molecules in the liquid are constantly moving with a range of speeds; some near the surface have enough energy to overcome attractions to neighboring molecules and escape into the air as water vapor. Boiling is different: it happens when vapor pressure throughout the liquid matches external pressure, producing bubbles. On a cool day, surface molecules can still evaporate gradually. If the surrounding air is dry, it can accept more vapor; if wind blows, it carries vapor away and speeds the process. Sunlight can add energy, but it is not required. As molecules leave, the puddle loses mass and may cool slightly, eventually vanishing into invisible atmospheric moisture.
