Dust rose around Mara’s knees as she tipped a box of chipped dishes into the donate pile. “Not that,” Leo said, catching the plain blue cup before it landed. Mara laughed. “It’s a cup.” He wiped its rim with his sleeve and set it carefully beside the lamp they were keeping. When she reached for it again, he turned away, shoulders tight. In the quiet, Mara noticed the faded white crescent near the handle, the place their mother’s thumb had rubbed smooth. Leo filled it from a dusty water bottle, took one sip, then placed it on the windowsill where afternoon light warmed the blue glaze. Mara moved the donate box farther away. “Fine,” she said softly, folding a dish towel around it.
