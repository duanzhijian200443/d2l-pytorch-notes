Mara lifted the unremarkable blue cup from a box of dust-coated dishes. “This goes in my pile.” Eli reached for it. “You don’t even drink tea.” She held on, knuckles whitening. “Neither do you.” Around them, the attic filled with their mother’s abandoned things: scarves, photo albums, a lamp with no shade. Eli tugged harder, and the cup slipped, striking the floor. Before it could break, Mara dropped to catch it, pressing it against her chest. Eli froze. Inside the cup, faded beneath the rim, were two crooked pencil lines and a date. He looked away. “She measured our heights with that,” Mara whispered. Eli knelt beside her, picking up the broken box. “Then we both keep it.” Mara shook her head, but handed him one handle.
