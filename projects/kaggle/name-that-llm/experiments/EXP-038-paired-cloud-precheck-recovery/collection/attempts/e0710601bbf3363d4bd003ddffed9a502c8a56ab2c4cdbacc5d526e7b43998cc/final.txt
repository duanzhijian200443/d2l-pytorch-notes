The first room starts with twelve chairs. When four chairs are moved to the second room, subtract four from twelve: 12 − 4 = 8, so eight chairs remain in the first room at that intermediate point. Next, three of the chairs are brought back from the second room to the first room. Since chairs are returning, add three to the eight already there: 8 + 3 = 11. Therefore, after both moves have happened, eleven chairs remain in the first room. The second room would have one chair left, because it received four and then sent three back. The key is to account for each change in order: first subtract the chairs that leave, then add the chairs that return.
