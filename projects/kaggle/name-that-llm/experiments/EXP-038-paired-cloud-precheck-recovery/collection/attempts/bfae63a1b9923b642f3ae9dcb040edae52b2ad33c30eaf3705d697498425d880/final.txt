A password manager reduces the burden of memorizing many passwords by securely storing login details in an encrypted vault and filling them in when needed. It can also generate long, random, unique passwords for every account, so a person does not need to invent or reuse passwords across websites. This improves security because a breach at one service is less likely to expose other accounts. Instead of remembering dozens of separate credentials, the user generally needs to remember one strong master password, or use a secure sign-in method to unlock the manager. However, the user remains responsible for protecting that master password, choosing one that is strong and not reused elsewhere, and enabling multi-factor authentication where available. They should also keep recovery information safe, secure their devices, update the password manager, and remain alert to phishing sites that may try to steal credentials even when a manager is used.
