Historians distinguish contemporary diaries from later memoirs because each reflects different relationships to time, memory, and purpose. A diary is usually recorded near the events, so it may preserve immediate impressions, uncertainties, and details later forgotten, but it can be incomplete, private, or shaped by what the writer then believed. A memoir, written decades later, may offer broader perspective and fuller context, yet memory can change, and later identities, loyalties, or audiences may influence the account. Neither source is automatically truthful; both require corroboration and critical analysis.
