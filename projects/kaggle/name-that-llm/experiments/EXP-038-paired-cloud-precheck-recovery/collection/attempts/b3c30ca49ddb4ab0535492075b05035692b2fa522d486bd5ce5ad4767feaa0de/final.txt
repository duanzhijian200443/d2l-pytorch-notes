Mara held up the blue cup, its glaze dulled by dust. “Trash,” she said, dropping it into the box. Theo snatched it back. “Keep.” “We have six better ones.” “Then keep this one.” He rubbed his thumb over a thin crack near the handle and tucked the cup beneath his sweater. Mara watched him, then turned to the boxes. She opened one marked WINTER, pulled out a faded scarf, and folded it carefully around the cup. Theo’s face tightened. “Don’t.” “You’ll break it hiding it.” “Mom gave it to me.” Mara stopped. From the bottom of another box, she lifted a child’s red mitten, stiff with age. She placed it beside him. “She gave me this.” Theo set the cup down. Together, silently, they wrapped both.
