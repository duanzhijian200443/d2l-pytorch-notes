A puddle can disappear on a cool day because water molecules do not all have the same energy. Even far below the boiling point, some molecules at the surface happen to be moving fast enough to overcome the attractions holding them in the liquid and escape into the air as water vapor. This process, evaporation, occurs at any temperature. Boiling is different: it happens when vapor bubbles can form throughout the liquid, which requires much more vapor pressure and usually much higher temperature. Dry air, wind, and sunlight speed evaporation by carrying away escaped vapor, leaving room for more molecules to leave. Evaporation also removes higher-energy molecules, so the remaining water may become slightly cooler as the puddle gradually vanishes.
