Run a four-week pilot in one clearly marked section of the bookstore, such as contemporary fiction, while leaving a comparable section unchanged as a control. In the pilot section, keep books organized by the existing genre and alphabetic or author-based system, but add a small “Staff Discovery Picks” shelf at the end of each relevant aisle. Each pick should feature one unfamiliar or less frequently purchased author, with a simple card explaining why the book was selected and naming two nearby books with similar themes or styles. Limit the display to two or three titles per aisle so customers can still navigate normally. Choose authors using baseline sales data, excluding bestsellers and authors already heavily promoted. Before the pilot, record weekly unit sales, browsing time, and sales of selected authors in both sections for two weeks. During the trial, track the same measures, along with the number of books taken from the discovery shelves and brief optional customer feedback collected at checkout. Compare changes in discovery-title sales between the pilot and control sections, while checking whether sales of regularly stocked books or customer comments indicate confusion. If discovery sales rise without reduced overall sales or navigation complaints, extend the experiment to another genre; otherwise, revise the signage, shelf size, or recommendation criteria and retest.
