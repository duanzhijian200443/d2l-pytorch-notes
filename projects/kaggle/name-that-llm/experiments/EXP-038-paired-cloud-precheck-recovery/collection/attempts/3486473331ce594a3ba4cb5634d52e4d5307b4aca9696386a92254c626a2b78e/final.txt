Run a four-week pilot in one clearly defined section, such as contemporary fiction, while leaving a similar nearby section unchanged as a comparison. Select 20 unfamiliar authors whose books fit the store’s existing categories and place one small “Staff Discovery” card beside each title, using a consistent color and a brief, specific description such as “For readers who enjoy intimate family stories.” Keep the books alphabetized or arranged by the store’s normal genre system, and avoid creating a separate display that could make browsing harder. Track weekly unit sales of the featured books, sales of comparable nonfeatured books, and the number of customers who ask staff about the cards. At checkout, invite purchasers of featured titles to answer a one-question survey, either on paper or by QR code: “Did this card help you choose a new author?” Ask staff to record whether customers report finding the section easy or difficult to navigate. After four weeks, compare featured-title sales with their sales during the previous four weeks and with similar titles in the comparison section. Continue the experiment only if featured books show a meaningful sales increase, customers report improved discovery, and staff observe no increase in confusion or misplaced books. Rotate the cards or authors in a second round to check whether results reflect the approach rather than unusually appealing titles.
