Start by defining the outcome the team needs, rather than assuming a weekly meeting is necessary. Is the goal to share updates, make a decision, solve a problem, or build relationships? Routine updates usually work better in a shared document or message thread; meetings are more useful when people need rapid discussion, negotiation, or sensitive conversation. Consider the total cost in participants’ time and what work the meeting would displace. Ask whether the right people could achieve the same result asynchronously or through a shorter, less frequent discussion. If a meeting seems worthwhile, trial it with a clear agenda, only essential attendees, and explicit outcomes. After a few sessions, gather feedback and keep, change, or cancel it based on its value.
