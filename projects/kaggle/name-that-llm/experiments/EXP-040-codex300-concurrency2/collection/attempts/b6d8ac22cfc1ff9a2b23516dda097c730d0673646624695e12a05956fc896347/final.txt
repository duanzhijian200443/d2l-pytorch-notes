The workshop owner should compare the lower purchase price against the hidden costs created by unreliable delivery dates. A cheaper supplier may improve margins when materials arrive as expected, but late or inconsistent deliveries can stop production, leave employees idle, delay customer orders, and damage the workshop’s reputation. The owner should estimate how much safety stock would be needed to protect against delays, since extra inventory ties up cash and requires storage space. They should also consider whether urgent replacement purchases, expedited shipping, overtime, or lost sales could erase the apparent saving. The predictable supplier may charge more per unit but make scheduling easier, reduce disruption, and support dependable promises to customers. The right choice depends on the workshop’s tolerance for risk, the importance of delivery deadlines, the availability of substitute materials, and the supplier’s actual record of lateness. A mixed approach, such as using the reliable supplier for critical orders and the cheaper one for flexible demand, may balance cost control with operational stability.
