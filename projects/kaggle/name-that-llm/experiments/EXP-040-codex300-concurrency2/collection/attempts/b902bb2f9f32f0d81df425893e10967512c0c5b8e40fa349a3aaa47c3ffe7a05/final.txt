In a simple compost pile, vegetable scraps mix with dry leaves and other plant material. Bacteria, fungi, and small creatures break the scraps into smaller pieces and simpler substances, using moisture and oxygen. Their activity releases heat, and some of the original material becomes gases, such as carbon dioxide, and water vapor that enter the air. Much of what remains gradually turns into dark, crumbly compost with an earthy smell. The scraps have changed form rather than simply vanished.
