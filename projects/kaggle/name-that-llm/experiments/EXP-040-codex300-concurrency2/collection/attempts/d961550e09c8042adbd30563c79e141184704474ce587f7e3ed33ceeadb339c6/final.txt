Start by defining the specific problem the weekly meeting would solve: a decision that needs joint discussion, a dependency that repeatedly causes delays, or information that cannot be conveyed clearly in writing. If the purpose is status reporting, announcements, or routine updates, use an asynchronous channel such as a shared dashboard, brief written update, or project board instead. Estimate the cost honestly by multiplying meeting length by attendees, preparation time, and context switching, then compare it with the expected benefit. A meeting is justified when real-time conversation will materially improve decisions, coordination, or relationships. If one is needed, invite only essential participants, set a clear agenda and decision owner, keep it short, and review after several weeks whether it still earns its place.
