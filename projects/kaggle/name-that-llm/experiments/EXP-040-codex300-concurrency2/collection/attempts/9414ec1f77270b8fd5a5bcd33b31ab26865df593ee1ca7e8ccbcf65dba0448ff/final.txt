A digital photo is made of tiny colored squares called pixels. On a phone screen, the image is displayed at a small size, so those pixels are packed closely together and the picture looks sharp. When the same photo is enlarged to poster size, the available pixels must cover a much larger area. Each pixel becomes more noticeable, and the printer or software may have to guess what should fill the gaps between them. This can make edges look soft, details disappear, and the whole image seem blurry or blocky. The problem is not necessarily the camera’s focus; it is often that the photo does not contain enough resolution for the desired print size. A higher-resolution original can provide more detail when enlarged.
