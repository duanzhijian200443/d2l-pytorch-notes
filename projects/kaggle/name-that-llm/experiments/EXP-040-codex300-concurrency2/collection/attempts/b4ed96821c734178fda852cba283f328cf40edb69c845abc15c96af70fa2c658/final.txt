A battery stores usable energy as chemical potential energy in the materials arranged inside it. Chemical reactions at its two electrodes tend to move electric charge through an external circuit, creating a voltage between the terminals. The battery does not contain a supply of electricity waiting to be poured out; instead, its chemistry can drive charges already present in the wires and components. When a small flashlight is switched on, the circuit is completed, and charges move through the battery, wires, switch, and bulb or LED. Inside the battery, chemical reactions consume some reactants and maintain the voltage for a while. The electrical energy delivered to the flashlight becomes light and heat, while the battery’s chemical composition changes and its remaining ability to drive charge gradually decreases.
