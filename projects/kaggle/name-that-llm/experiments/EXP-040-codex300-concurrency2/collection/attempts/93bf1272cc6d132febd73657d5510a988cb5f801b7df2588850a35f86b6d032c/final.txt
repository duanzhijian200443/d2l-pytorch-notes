A seed can be alive without actively growing. For example, a dry bean seed contains a living embryo, along with stored food protected by a seed coat. While the seed is dormant, its cells carry out only a very low level of metabolism, so it may look lifeless and remain unchanged for months or even years. It is still alive because, under suitable conditions, the embryo can resume normal activity. When the bean seed receives enough water, oxygen, and warmth, it begins germinating. It absorbs water, swells, and uses its stored food for energy. Soon, a root pushes downward and a shoot grows upward toward the light. Thus, being alive means retaining the capacity for life processes, while actively growing means producing new cells and increasing in size.
