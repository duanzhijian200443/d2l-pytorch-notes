Mara had spent the afternoon photographing the marsh at the edge of town, and nearly every picture seemed worth keeping. The heron lifting from the reeds was sharp enough to show each feather, the sunset spread gold across the water, and even the sleepy dog beside the trail looked ready for a magazine cover. Only one photograph appeared ruined. It showed a small, dark shape near the old footbridge, smeared by motion and washed with pale light. Mara almost deleted it, but something made her pause. She remembered hearing a child laugh while she took the picture, though no one had been visible on the empty path. That evening, she enlarged the image. The blur did not become clearer, but behind it she noticed a row of tiny reflections, like lanterns floating in the river. The next morning, she returned to the bridge and found no footprints, lanterns, or signs of anyone. Still, she printed the photograph and pinned it above her desk. Months later, when a local historian saw it, he recognized the footbridge as the site of a village washed away by a flood a century earlier. Mara never learned what her camera had captured. She only knew that the worst picture from her perfect outing was the one she could never bring herself to lose.
