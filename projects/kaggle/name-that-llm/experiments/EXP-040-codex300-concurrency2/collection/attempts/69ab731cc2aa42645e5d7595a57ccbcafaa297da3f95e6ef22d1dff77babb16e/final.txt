Restoring missing parts of an ancient object means adding material to recover a more complete form, stabilize weakened areas, or make its original function easier to understand. Such additions may be carefully matched in color and shape, but responsible conservation keeps them reversible and distinguishable on close inspection so they are not mistaken for original fabric. Displaying gaps openly takes the opposite visual approach: losses remain visible, sometimes supported or outlined without being filled. This preserves direct evidence of the object’s age, damage, excavation history, and uncertain interpretation. Open gaps can appear fragmentary, yet they avoid speculative reconstruction. The choice depends on condition, intended use, available evidence, and museum ethics; both approaches protect authenticity but communicate completeness and uncertainty differently.
