Mara returned the borrowed ladder just before dusk, carrying it across Mr. Vale’s garden while rain tapped softly on the rhubarb leaves. She had used it to rescue a kite from the old oak, but the kite was gone now, swept toward the river with its red tail snapping behind it. Mr. Vale met her at the gate, surprisingly cheerful. He said the ladder had once belonged to his wife, who climbed it every spring to hang lanterns for the neighborhood picnic. From his pocket, he produced a small brass lantern, polished but unlit. “We’re hanging them again Saturday,” he said. Mara looked toward the darkening oak and imagined warm lights beneath its branches, the lost kite perhaps resting somewhere nearby. Smiling, she accepted his invitation.
