Try a “future joy” test rather than judging yourself for unfinished projects. Gather all the supplies in one place, then consider each hobby separately. Keep it if you still feel genuine curiosity, can name a realistic time to use it, and would choose it over a new purchase today. Pause it if you like the idea but lack time, space, or energy; place the supplies in a clearly labeled box, write down the next tiny step, and set a review date three or six months away. Give it away if the hobby mainly creates guilt, no longer fits your interests, or would be expensive and inconvenient to restart. Before donating, take a photo, keep any especially meaningful item, and offer usable materials to a friend, school, community group, or local swap. Limit the number of active hobbies—perhaps one or two—and remember that supplies are not obligations. Your past enthusiasm was real even if your interests changed. The goal is not to finish everything, but to make your home support the life you want now.
