Dividing a project by task count can create the illusion of equality because tasks are not always comparable in size, difficulty, or resource requirements. One team may complete ten routine tasks that each take a few hours, while another completes five complex tasks requiring specialized knowledge, extensive research, problem-solving, coordination, and repeated revisions. The second team may therefore spend substantially more time and mental effort despite finishing fewer tasks. Difficulty can also involve uncertainty and risk: challenging tasks may reveal unexpected problems, require experimentation, or depend on decisions that affect the entire project. In addition, some tasks produce key deliverables or serve as bottlenecks, making them more valuable than several minor tasks combined. A fair division should consider factors such as estimated effort, complexity, expertise required, deadlines, dependencies, responsibility, and the likelihood of rework. Measuring work by time, weighted task values, or expected contribution gives a more accurate picture than simply counting completed items. Equal numbers of tasks do not necessarily represent equal workloads or equal contributions.
