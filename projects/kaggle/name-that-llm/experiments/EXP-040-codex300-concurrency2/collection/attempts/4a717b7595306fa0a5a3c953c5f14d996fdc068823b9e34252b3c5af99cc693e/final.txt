Twenty minutes before the chamber concert, the house manager noticed one black orchestra chair missing from the front row. The audience waited while ushers searched closets, loading docks, and the café, provoking jokes about a chair taking an intermission early. The delay seemed absurd until the fire inspector, already present for the sold-out evening, explained that the seating plan had been approved with a precisely measured evacuation aisle. Replacing the chair with a wider folding model would narrow the aisle by three inches and invalidate the permit. The manager remembered that the missing chair had been loaned that afternoon to an elderly volunteer sorting programs. They found it in the basement, beside her coat, with a neatly labeled emergency inhaler tucked underneath. She had left quickly after feeling unwell, and the chair’s familiar brass inventory tag let staff identify her and call her daughter. Paramedics reached her apartment in time; she was having a serious asthma attack. When the concert finally began, no one complained about the delay.
