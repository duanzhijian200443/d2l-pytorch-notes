Test the display with a simple before-and-after experiment focused on borrowing behavior, not attention. For two comparable four-week periods, record circulation of the featured titles, similar titles kept on ordinary shelves, and total borrowing in the relevant genre. Rotate displays across locations or use a matched control group of books so differences in season, school assignments, and popularity are less likely to mislead. Place a small, optional checkout prompt asking borrowers how they found the book, with choices such as display, staff recommendation, catalog, or browsing. Staff can also make brief observation samples, counting people who stop, pick up a book, read its cover, and ultimately take it to the desk, distinguishing glances from meaningful engagement. Compare display-title checkouts, holds, renewals, and first-time borrowers with the control period. Afterward, ask a few borrowers whether the display helped them choose something they wanted to read. A useful display should increase completed loans and reported discovery, not merely handling or visual notice.
