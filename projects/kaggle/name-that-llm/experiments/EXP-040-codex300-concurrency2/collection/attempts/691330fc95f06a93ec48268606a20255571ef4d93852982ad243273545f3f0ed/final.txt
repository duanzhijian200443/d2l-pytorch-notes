A visually uniform central square creates order, coherence, and a strong shared identity. Consistent colors, materials, lettering, and sign sizes can make historic architecture more prominent, reduce visual clutter, and give visitors a calm, memorable sense of place. Such regulation may also signal civic care and prevent wealthier businesses from dominating attention. By contrast, distinctive shop signs contribute personality, creativity, and commercial vitality. They help customers identify businesses quickly, allow owners to express local traditions or individual character, and can make the square feel lively and surprising. Yet complete uniformity may seem sterile, while unrestricted signage can become chaotic or obscure the square’s architectural qualities. A balanced policy could establish common limits on scale, lighting, and placement while leaving room for varied colors, typography, craftsmanship, and imagery.
