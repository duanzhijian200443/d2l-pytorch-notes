Dividing a project by task count assumes that every task requires roughly the same amount of effort, skill, time, risk, and responsibility. In practice, this is rarely true. One team may receive many short, routine tasks, such as formatting documents, updating labels, or making simple configuration changes, while the other receives fewer but far more demanding tasks, such as designing an architecture, resolving complex technical failures, integrating systems, or validating security and performance. Although both teams may complete the same number of tasks, the second team may spend substantially more hours researching, testing, revising, and handling uncertainty. Harder tasks can also require specialized expertise and create dependencies that delay other work, adding pressure beyond the direct time required. A fairer division should therefore consider estimated effort, complexity, required knowledge, deadlines, risk, and the consequences of errors. Task count is easy to measure, but it is an incomplete measure of contribution because it ignores important differences in the actual work needed to finish each task.
