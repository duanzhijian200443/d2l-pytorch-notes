A battery stores usable energy primarily as chemical potential energy in materials arranged to undergo controlled reactions. These reactions can separate charge and maintain a voltage between the terminals, but the battery is not a container filled with electricity. When a flashlight switch closes the circuit, chemical reactions at the battery’s electrodes drive electrons already present in the metal parts of the circuit to move. The resulting electric field spreads through the circuit and transfers energy from the battery to the bulb. In an incandescent flashlight, the filament resists electron motion, becomes hot, and emits light; some energy also becomes heat in the battery, wires, and bulb. As reactants are consumed and products accumulate, the battery’s ability to maintain voltage and deliver useful energy gradually decreases.
