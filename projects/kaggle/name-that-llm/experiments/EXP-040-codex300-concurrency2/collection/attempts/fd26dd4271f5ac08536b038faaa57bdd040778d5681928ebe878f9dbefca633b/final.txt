A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading rather than require everyone to finish before joining the discussion. Strict completion rules can make the group feel exclusive, especially for people with demanding schedules, different reading speeds, or legitimate reasons for abandoning a book. Those members may still offer valuable observations about the opening chapters, the writing style, or why the book failed to hold their attention. However, discussion quality benefits when many participants finish, since they can analyze the complete plot, character development, structure, and ending without relying on incomplete impressions. A practical compromise is to encourage finishing but make it optional: announce the intended stopping point, ask those who have not finished to avoid spoilers, and divide the conversation into spoiler-free and full-book portions. Members who quit can explain where and why they stopped, while completers can explore the work’s resolution. This approach preserves thoughtful, detailed discussion without turning participation into a test of commitment, making the club both intellectually rewarding and welcoming.
