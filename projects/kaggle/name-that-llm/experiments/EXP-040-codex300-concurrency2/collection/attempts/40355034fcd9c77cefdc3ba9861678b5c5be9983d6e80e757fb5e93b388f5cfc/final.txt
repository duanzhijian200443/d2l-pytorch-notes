Restoring missing parts of an ancient object involves reconstructing damaged or absent elements so the artifact appears more complete and can be understood as a whole. This approach may improve readability, stability, or visual coherence, but it risks introducing guesses, disguising uncertainty, or blurring the boundary between original material and modern intervention. Displaying the gaps openly takes the opposite approach: it preserves the object’s incomplete condition and makes loss, damage, and uncertainty visible to viewers. Rather than pretending to recover what is unknown, this method emphasizes historical authenticity and invites interpretation. The choice depends on purpose and ethics: reconstruction may support use or comprehension, while openness about gaps respects the artifact’s surviving evidence and prevents imagined additions from being mistaken for historical fact.
