Restoring missing parts of an ancient object aims to recover a sense of its original form, function, or visual unity. Conservators may add carefully distinguishable materials, guided by surviving evidence, so viewers can understand how the object once appeared. This approach can improve stability and legibility, but it risks introducing speculation or making modern additions seem authentic. Displaying gaps openly takes a different approach: losses, fractures, and absences remain visible as part of the object’s history. Rather than completing the past, it emphasizes survival, damage, and the limits of present knowledge. Open gaps can encourage viewers to imagine what is missing while recognizing uncertainty. Both methods involve interpretation; the key difference is whether absence is visually repaired or deliberately preserved as meaningful evidence.
