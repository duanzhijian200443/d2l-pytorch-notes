A point-like light source produces nearly parallel rays from a single direction, so an object blocks them cleanly and creates a sharp-edged shadow. Real lights have size, however, so different parts of the source send rays around the object at slightly different angles. Close to the object, these angles have little room to separate, leaving a relatively narrow transition region between full shadow and full illumination. Farther away, the rays from opposite sides of the light source spread apart more. Some parts of the source become visible while others remain blocked, creating a widening penumbra, or partial shadow. Thus, a larger light source makes the fuzzy edge broader, while a smaller or more distant source makes shadows sharper.
