A small library can test whether a new display helps readers discover books by comparing it with the usual arrangement over several similar time periods. Choose comparable sections or rotate the display among locations, then track meaningful behaviors: how many people stop, pick up a book, read its cover or first page, ask about it, borrow it, or later return for another title by the same author or on the same subject. Brief observation can measure attention, but borrowing and follow-up interest show whether glances become discovery. Library staff can also ask a simple, consistent question at checkout, such as, “How did you find this book?” and offer a short optional survey about whether the display introduced them to something new. Compare results while accounting for day, time, programming, season, and display location. A useful test might define success as increased borrowing of featured or related books, especially among patrons who had not planned to seek them. Review both numbers and comments, and revise the display based on what leads readers beyond looking to choosing.
