Use a hybrid schedule that reserves about 70 percent of each day for appointments and leaves 30 percent as flexible capacity for walk-ins, urgent repairs, and jobs that run longer than expected. Book appointments in realistic service blocks based on repair type, require a small deposit or confirmation for longer work, and ask customers to arrive within a defined window. For walk-ins, use a first-come, first-served queue after a quick triage: safety-critical or simple, fast repairs can be prioritized, while larger jobs receive a same-day estimate and the next available appointment. Display expected wait times clearly in the shop and by text message, and set a daily cutoff once flexible capacity is full. Technicians should update the front desk when work finishes early or expands so open time can be reassigned promptly. The main compromise is that neither group receives perfect convenience: appointment customers may occasionally face a modest delay when urgent walk-ins consume buffer time, while walk-in customers cannot be guaranteed immediate service and may need to return later. However, the policy protects predictable scheduled work while preserving enough flexibility to capture walk-in business without allowing the queue to overwhelm the shop.
