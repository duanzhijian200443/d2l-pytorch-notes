Thank your friend warmly and make it clear that you value both their generosity and their time. You might say, “That is incredibly kind of you to offer, and I really appreciate how much thought you’ve put into it. For what I need right now, though, a small piece of help would be more than enough.” Be specific about the limited request so they have an easy way to contribute without guessing: “Could you help me for an hour on Saturday?” or “Would you be willing to look over this one document?” Framing the boundary around your needs, rather than criticizing their offer, keeps the tone appreciative. If they seem disappointed, reassure them that their simpler help is genuinely useful: “Honestly, having you handle this one part would take a real weight off my shoulders.” You can also leave room for their goodwill without committing to a larger arrangement by saying, “If I need more later, I’ll definitely ask.” Avoid vague acceptance if you do not want the full favor, since it can create obligations or confusion. A clear, grateful response respects their desire to help while protecting both of you from an arrangement that becomes bigger than necessary.
