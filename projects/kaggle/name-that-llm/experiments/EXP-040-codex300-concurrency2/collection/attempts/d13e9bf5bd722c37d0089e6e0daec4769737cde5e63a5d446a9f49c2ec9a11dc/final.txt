A sealed bag of chips can puff up in the mountains because the air pressure outside the bag decreases as altitude increases. Near sea level, a taller column of air lies above you, and its weight produces greater atmospheric pressure. Higher in the mountains, less air is overhead, so the surrounding atmosphere pushes inward on the bag less strongly. The gas trapped inside the bag was sealed at the packaging location’s pressure. As the outside pressure drops, that gas expands, pushing the flexible sides outward until the inward and outward forces balance again. No extra air needs to enter: the same number of gas molecules simply occupies a larger volume. This illustrates Boyle’s law, which says that, at a constant temperature, a fixed amount of gas expands as its pressure decreases. Temperature changes can also affect how much the bag swells, but the drop in atmospheric pressure is usually the main reason it puffs up during a climb into the mountains.
