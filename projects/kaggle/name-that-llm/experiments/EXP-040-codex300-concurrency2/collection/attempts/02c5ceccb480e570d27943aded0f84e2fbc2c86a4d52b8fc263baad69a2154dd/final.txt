At the Saturday community garden, Maya found a small wooden sign lying beside the tomato beds. Its label had vanished, leaving one stubborn plant unnamed. She asked the older man watering nearby if he knew what it was. “I planted it,” he said, “but I’ve forgotten too.” They laughed, then began guessing: pepper, eggplant, perhaps an exotic weed. Each morning, they checked its growing leaves together, sharing coffee and stories while the mystery unfolded. When a cluster of bright yellow flowers appeared, Maya searched the library and discovered it was a ground cherry. The man, Luis, had never tasted one. They waited for the first fruit to ripen, split it carefully, and grinned. The missing label had introduced them before the plant could.
