The team should treat the enthusiastic comments as evidence that the feature is appealing in concept or creates a strong first impression, not as proof that it delivers lasting value. Repeat usage is a stronger behavioral signal: users may enjoy the novelty, praise the idea, or want to encourage the team while still finding the feature unnecessary, inconvenient, or poorly integrated into their routines. The gap between sentiment and retention should therefore be investigated rather than averaged away. Confirm that analytics correctly capture repeat use, then examine cohorts, user segments, activation paths, frequency of the underlying need, and the point at which users stop returning. Some features are naturally used rarely, so success should be measured against the expected cadence and intended outcome, not an arbitrary daily or weekly benchmark. Follow up with first-time users and non-returners to learn what they expected, what value they received, and what friction remained. In short, comments reveal perception; repeated behavior reveals durable utility. Both matter, but retention should carry more weight when judging product-market value.
