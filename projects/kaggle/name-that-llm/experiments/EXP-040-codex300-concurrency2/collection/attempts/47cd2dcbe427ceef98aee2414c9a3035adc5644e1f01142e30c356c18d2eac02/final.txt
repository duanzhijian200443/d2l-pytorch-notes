A historian may use several incomplete sources because no single account can capture every aspect of the past. Each source reflects its creator’s knowledge, purpose, perspective, and circumstances, so even a detailed account may contain errors, omissions, or bias. Comparing different sources allows historians to check claims, identify contradictions, and fill gaps in the evidence. For example, an official report might describe government decisions, while letters or diaries reveal how ordinary people experienced their effects. Agreement between independent sources can strengthen a conclusion, while disagreement can expose competing viewpoints or uncertainties. Rather than searching for a supposedly perfect account, historians build the most convincing interpretation available by assessing sources critically and acknowledging what the evidence cannot establish with confidence.
