An item priced at 100 units costs 90 units after a ten-percent discount, because ten percent of 100 is 10. Adding ten percent to the reduced price adds only 9 units, since the increase is calculated from 90 rather than 100. The final price is therefore 99 units, not 100. The two percentages do not cancel because they apply to different amounts: the discount removes 10 units, while the later increase restores only 9, leaving the price one unit below the original.
