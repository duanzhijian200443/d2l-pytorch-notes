A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading, while setting clear expectations about spoilers and discussion depth. Requiring everyone to finish may improve conversation: members can analyze the full narrative, compare early impressions with the ending, and avoid limiting comments to the opening chapters. It also encourages commitment and gives quieter readers a shared foundation for contributing. However, a strict completion rule can exclude busy people, slower readers, those with disabilities, or anyone who found the book upsetting, inaccessible, or simply unengaging. Their reasons for stopping can themselves produce valuable discussion about pacing, style, representation, or reader expectations. A balanced approach is to invite everyone, announce that the meeting will include unrestricted spoilers, and perhaps begin with a short spoiler-free segment before discussing the entire book. Participants who did not finish should be encouraged to explain their reactions without dominating questions about events they missed. This policy protects discussion quality while recognizing that thoughtful engagement does not always require reaching the final page.
