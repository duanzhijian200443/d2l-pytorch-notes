The workshop owner should compare the supplier’s purchase price with the full cost of unreliable delivery. The cheaper materials may improve margins and free cash for other needs, but uncertain arrival dates can interrupt production, leave employees or machines idle, delay customer orders, and damage the workshop’s reputation. To reduce those risks, the owner might need to hold more safety stock, place orders earlier, pay for extra storage, or occasionally buy emergency supplies at higher prices. These measures can erase the apparent savings. The more dependable supplier may charge more, yet enable leaner inventory, clearer production schedules, more reliable promises to customers, and less management time spent chasing shipments. The decision should therefore reflect demand variability, the cost of downtime, storage capacity, cash flow, customer tolerance for delays, and how quickly substitute materials can be obtained. The owner can estimate each supplier’s total expected cost, including disruption risk, rather than comparing unit prices alone. A mixed strategy may also work: use the cheaper supplier for routine stock while reserving the reliable supplier for urgent or critical orders.
