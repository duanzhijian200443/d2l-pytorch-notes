A book club should encourage members to finish each selection without making completion a condition of participation. Reading the whole book usually leads to richer discussion: members can evaluate character development, consider the ending, and judge whether the author fulfills early promises. However, a strict rule may exclude people with demanding schedules, reading difficulties, or strong objections to the material. Those who stopped reading can also contribute useful insights by explaining where their interest faded or why a particular scene felt unconvincing. The key is to balance openness with clear expectations. Participants should say how far they read, avoid presenting partial impressions as judgments of the entire work, and accept that spoilers are unavoidable. A moderator can invite their perspectives while ensuring that readers who finished have time to explore the full text. Ultimately, a successful book club values thoughtful engagement rather than mere completion, but inclusion should enrich, not limit, the conversation about the book as a whole.
