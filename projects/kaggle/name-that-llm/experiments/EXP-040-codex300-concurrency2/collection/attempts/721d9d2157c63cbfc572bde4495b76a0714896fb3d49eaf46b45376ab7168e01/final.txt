A small library could test the display by comparing discovery during several similar weeks with and without it, alternating conditions to reduce the influence of holidays or unusually busy days. Keep the featured books, opening hours, and promotion as consistent as possible, and record visitor numbers so results can be compared fairly. Define discovery as more than looking: a reader might pick up an unfamiliar book, borrow it, reserve it, or note its title for later. Staff could count browsing and borrowing without identifying readers, while a brief optional exit question asks whether visitors found a book they had not previously known about and what led them to it. Track loans of featured titles alongside total borrowing to see whether the display expands interest or simply shifts loans from other shelves. Compare discovery rates per hundred visitors, not just raw totals. After several cycles, use the counts and reader comments to decide whether to retain, revise, or remove the display, acknowledging uncertainty if participation is low.
