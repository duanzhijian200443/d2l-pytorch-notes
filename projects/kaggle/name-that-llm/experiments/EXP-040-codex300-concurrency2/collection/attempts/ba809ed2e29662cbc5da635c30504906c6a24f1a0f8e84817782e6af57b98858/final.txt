Collecting objects as souvenirs is primarily a personal and emotional activity. Souvenirs are chosen because they remind someone of a place, event, or experience, and their value often comes from the memories, feelings, and stories attached to them. They may be inexpensive, mass-produced, or commonplace, since what matters is their connection to the collector’s life. Collecting objects as historical evidence, however, is an effort to preserve and interpret information about the past. Historical evidence is valued for what it can reveal about people, cultures, technologies, economies, beliefs, or significant events, rather than simply for the collector’s personal memories. Its importance depends on factors such as authenticity, age, provenance, condition, context, and how reliably it represents a particular time or experience. The same object can serve both purposes: a ticket kept from a journey may be a treasured souvenir for one person, while a museum may study similar tickets to understand transportation, tourism, or social behavior in a specific period. Souvenir collecting is therefore usually subjective and centered on personal meaning, whereas collecting historical evidence is more systematic and concerned with documentation, interpretation, and public knowledge. Nevertheless, both practices preserve connections to the past, though they do so for different audiences and with different standards of value.
