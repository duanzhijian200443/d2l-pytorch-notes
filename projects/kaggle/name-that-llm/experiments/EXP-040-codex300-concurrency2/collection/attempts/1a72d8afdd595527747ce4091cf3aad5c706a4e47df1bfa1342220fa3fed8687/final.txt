Two cultures may divide the day differently because time is shaped not only by nature but also by social needs, beliefs, work patterns, and technology. One culture might organize periods around agricultural tasks, prayer, meals, or changing daylight, while another might emphasize trade, government schedules, or clock-based routines. Geography also matters: differences in latitude, seasons, and climate affect how useful certain divisions are. Thus, people experience the same cycle of light and darkness but assign meaning to it according to their environment and traditions.
