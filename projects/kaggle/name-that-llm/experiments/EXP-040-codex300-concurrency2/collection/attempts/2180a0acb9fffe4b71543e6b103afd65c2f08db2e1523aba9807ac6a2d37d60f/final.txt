The candle’s height decreases at a constant rate because it burns down by the same amount every hour. It starts at 20 centimeters and measures 14 centimeters after two hours, so it has lost 20 − 14 = 6 centimeters during those two hours. Dividing 6 centimeters by 2 hours gives a burn rate of 3 centimeters per hour. After five hours, the total amount burned would therefore be 5 × 3 = 15 centimeters. Subtracting that total loss from the original height gives 20 − 15 = 5 centimeters. Equivalently, the height follows the linear rule h = 20 − 3t, where h is height in centimeters and t is time in hours. Substituting t = 5 gives h = 20 − 3(5) = 5. Thus, under the uniform-burning assumption, the predicted height after five hours is 5 centimeters.
