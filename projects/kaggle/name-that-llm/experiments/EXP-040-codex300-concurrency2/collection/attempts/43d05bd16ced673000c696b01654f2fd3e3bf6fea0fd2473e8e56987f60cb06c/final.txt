A historian may use several incomplete sources because no single account is entirely objective, accurate, or comprehensive. Every source reflects its author’s perspective, purpose, knowledge, and limitations. A government report may omit uncomfortable facts, while a diary may reveal personal experiences but misunderstand wider events. Comparing different sources allows historians to identify agreements, contradictions, biases, and gaps. These differences can provide valuable evidence about how people understood events and what they wanted others to believe. By combining letters, official records, newspapers, artifacts, oral testimonies, and other evidence, historians can construct a more nuanced interpretation. The goal is not to find a perfectly complete account, which may not exist, but to evaluate evidence carefully and develop the most convincing explanation possible.
