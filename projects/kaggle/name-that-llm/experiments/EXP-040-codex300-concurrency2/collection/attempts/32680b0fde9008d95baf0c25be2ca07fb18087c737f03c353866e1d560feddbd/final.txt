Start by acknowledging the generosity behind the offer: “That’s incredibly thoughtful of you, and it means a lot that you’re willing to do so much.” Then clearly describe the smaller help you actually need, framing the limit as a way to make things easy rather than as a rejection: “Honestly, the most helpful thing would be just a quick ride to the appointment; I can handle everything else.” If appropriate, give a specific boundary around time, cost, or effort: “I’d only need about twenty minutes,” or “Please don’t buy anything—I already have the supplies.” You can also offer an easy way for your friend to decline or adjust: “If that timing is inconvenient, no worries at all.” This lets you accept their kindness without creating an arrangement that feels excessive or difficult to reciprocate. If they continue offering more, warmly repeat your preference: “I really appreciate it, but keeping it simple would make me feel most comfortable.” Avoid overexplaining or apologizing; a clear, appreciative response is usually kinder than reluctantly agreeing to something you do not want. Afterward, thank them specifically for the help they provided, perhaps with a message or a small gesture that feels natural. The goal is to receive the care behind the offer while shaping the practical details into something manageable for both of you.
