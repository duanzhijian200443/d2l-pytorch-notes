Accept the kindness behind the offer while being specific about the amount of help you actually want. Start with appreciation, then name a smaller, concrete version of the favor: “That’s so thoughtful of you. I don’t need the whole afternoon, but a quick ride to the appointment would be a huge help.” This lets your friend know their support matters without committing either of you to an elaborate plan. Keep the boundaries practical: clarify the task, timing, and any limits before arrangements grow. You might say, “If you’re free between two and three, that would be perfect; I can handle everything after that.” Avoid apologizing excessively or giving a long defense of why you need less. A warm, straightforward explanation is enough: “Keeping it simple would work best for me.” Give them room to decline the smaller request, too, especially if their original offer depended on a different schedule. If they keep adding extras, repeat your preference kindly: “I really appreciate it, but just the ride is all I need.” You can thank them afterward without treating their generosity as a debt that requires an equally elaborate favor. Accepting help doesn’t mean accepting every part of the offer; clear limits can make the exchange easier and more comfortable for both of you.
