Restoring missing parts of an ancient object aims to recover its original appearance, making its shape, function, or imagery easier to understand. Reconstructed sections can help viewers imagine the object as a complete work, but they may introduce uncertain interpretations or blur the distinction between ancient material and modern additions. Displaying gaps openly instead emphasizes authenticity and the object’s history, including damage, decay, and survival over time. This approach allows viewers to see what remains without presenting speculation as fact, although the object may be harder to interpret. Neither choice is automatically superior: conservators weigh available evidence, educational goals, and ethical responsibilities. Many museums combine these approaches by using restrained, reversible additions that remain distinguishable from the original material.
