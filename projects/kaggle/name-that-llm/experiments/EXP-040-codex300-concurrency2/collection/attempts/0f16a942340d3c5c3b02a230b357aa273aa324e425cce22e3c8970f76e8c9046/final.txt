On a bright Saturday, Mara noticed that the hand-painted label beside her tomato seedlings had vanished, leaving only a crooked stick in the soil. Certain someone had moved it, she asked the gardener in the next plot, a quiet man named Ellis, if he had seen it. He smiled and held up the sign, its letters smudged by last night’s rain. “I found it by the compost,” he said, “but I couldn’t tell whether it said tomatoes or something more interesting.” Together they cleaned it and rewrote the name, adding a tiny red tomato beside the words. As they worked, Ellis admitted he was new to gardening and lonely in the neighborhood. Mara handed him a spare basil seedling, and by noon they had planned a shared herb bed.
