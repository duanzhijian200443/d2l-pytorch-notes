Historians use several incomplete sources because no single account can fully capture a past event. Each source reflects its creator’s position, purpose, knowledge, and possible biases: an official report may omit dissent, while a personal letter may reveal feelings but misunderstand wider events. Comparing different materials—such as records, newspapers, objects, photographs, and oral testimony—helps historians identify agreements, contradictions, and gaps. These differences are useful evidence rather than simply problems, since they show how people experienced or interpreted events differently. By cross-checking sources and considering who produced them and why, historians can build a more balanced, carefully supported interpretation than they could from one account that only appears perfect.
