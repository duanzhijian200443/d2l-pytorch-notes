The orchestra was ready to begin when the conductor noticed one empty chair in the second row. It belonged to Mara, the principal cellist, who had died unexpectedly that morning. The concert was supposed to be dedicated to her, but nobody understood why her chair mattered so much until the stage manager found a folded envelope taped beneath it. Mara had arranged for her estranged daughter, Elise, to attend secretly, hoping the performance would give them a chance to reconcile. Inside the envelope was a letter and a small brass key. The letter explained that Elise’s late father had left her a recording of Mara’s first audition, along with instructions to listen after the concert. Without the chair, the envelope would remain hidden and Elise might leave, believing her mother had never wanted to see her. The orchestra waited while staff searched the storage rooms. At last, they found a spare chair, carried it onto the stage, and recovered the envelope. Elise read the letter in the lobby, then sat down beside Mara’s stand. Only then did the concert begin.
