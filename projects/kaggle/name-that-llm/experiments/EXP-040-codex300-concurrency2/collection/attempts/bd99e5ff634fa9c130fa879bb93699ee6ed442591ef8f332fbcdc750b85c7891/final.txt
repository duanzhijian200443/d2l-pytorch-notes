In a simple compost pile, vegetable scraps are gradually broken down by bacteria, fungi, insects, and other small organisms. These decomposers chew, digest, and chemically transform the scraps, while air, moisture, and warmth support the process. Soft leaves, peels, and stems lose their original shapes and become darker, finer, and more crumbly. Some material becomes carbon dioxide, water vapor, and heat, while the remaining nutrients and organic matter combine into a rich, earthy substance called compost.
