A workshop owner should weigh the cheaper supplier’s savings against the costs and risks of unreliable delivery. Lower material prices can improve profit margins or allow more competitive pricing, but those benefits may disappear if late deliveries leave workers idle, interrupt production, or require expensive emergency purchases. Delays can also cause missed customer deadlines, damage the workshop’s reputation, and reduce repeat business. The owner should consider how often deliveries are late, how serious those delays would be, and whether jobs have flexible schedules or firm completion dates. Keeping extra materials in stock could reduce disruption, but it ties up cash and increases storage costs. A more expensive, dependable supplier may therefore offer better overall value, especially for urgent orders or materials essential to daily operations. Alternatively, the owner could use the cheaper supplier for routine stock and the reliable one for time-sensitive work. The best choice depends on total operating costs and the workshop’s ability to absorb uncertainty, not simply the purchase price.
