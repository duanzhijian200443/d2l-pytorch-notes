Every clock in Mara’s shop left ticking precisely, except the brass carriage clock above her bench. It had belonged to her husband, who always set it five minutes slow so he could linger over breakfast before catching the train. After his death, Mara cleaned its gears and found the fault easily, but adjusted it only enough to keep that familiar delay. Each evening, when it chimed six after the street clocks had finished, she felt granted five quiet minutes in which he was merely late coming home.
