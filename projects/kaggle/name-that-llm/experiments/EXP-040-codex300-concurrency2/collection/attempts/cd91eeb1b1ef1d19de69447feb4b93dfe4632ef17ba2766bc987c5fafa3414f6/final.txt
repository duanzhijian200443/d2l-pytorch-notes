In a simple compost pile, bacteria, fungi, insects, and worms break vegetable scraps into smaller pieces while using oxygen and moisture. Peels, leaves, and stems soften, darken, and lose their original shapes as their carbon-rich materials are rearranged. Some material becomes gases and water that move into the air, while the remaining solids mix with soil particles and organisms. Over time, the pile turns into dark, crumbly compost, a nutrient-rich material that can help plants grow.
