Because the candle burns down by the same amount each hour, its height decreases at a constant rate. It begins at twenty centimeters and is fourteen centimeters tall after two hours, so it has lost six centimeters during those two hours. Dividing six centimeters by two hours gives a burning rate of three centimeters per hour. Under the constant-rate assumption, the candle’s height after t hours can be represented by the linear expression 20 − 3t, where 20 is its initial height and 3t is the total amount burned. After five hours, the total decrease is 3 × 5 = 15 centimeters. Subtracting this decrease from the initial height gives 20 − 15 = 5 centimeters. Therefore, the predicted height of the candle after five hours is five centimeters. This prediction assumes that the candle continues burning uniformly throughout the entire period, with no change caused by variations in the wick, wax, airflow, or other physical conditions.
