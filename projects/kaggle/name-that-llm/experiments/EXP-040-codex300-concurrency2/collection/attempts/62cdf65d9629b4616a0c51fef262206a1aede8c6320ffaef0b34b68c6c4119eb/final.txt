A sealed bag of chips can puff up in the mountains because air pressure outside the bag decreases as altitude increases. When the bag was sealed at a lower elevation, it trapped a fixed amount of gas at the higher atmospheric pressure found there. During the climb, nobody adds air, and the number of gas molecules inside remains essentially unchanged. However, the thinner mountain atmosphere pushes against the bag less strongly than the atmosphere at the original elevation. The trapped gas therefore expands, stretching the flexible bag until its internal pressure is closer to the lower external pressure. This behavior is described approximately by Boyle’s law: for a fixed amount of gas at a nearly constant temperature, pressure decreases as volume increases. Temperature can also affect the result, since warming makes gas expand and cooling makes it contract, but the change in atmospheric pressure is usually the main reason for the puffing. If the bag returns to a lower elevation without leaking, stronger outside pressure will compress it again.
