An item priced at 100 units receives a ten-percent discount, reducing its price by 10 units to 90 units. The subsequent ten-percent increase is calculated from this reduced price, not from the original price. Ten percent of 90 is 9, so adding it raises the price to 99 units. The result is therefore 1 unit less than the original price. The percentages are equal, but their monetary amounts differ because they are applied to different starting values.
