A digital photograph is made of tiny colored squares called pixels. On a phone’s small screen, those pixels are packed closely together, so your eyes see a smooth, sharp image. When the same photo is enlarged into a poster, its existing pixels must cover a much bigger area. The photo does not automatically gain more detail, so edges can look soft, and individual squares may become visible. Software can add extra pixels by estimating colors between the original ones, but it cannot reliably recover details the camera never captured. For a sharp poster, you generally need a higher-resolution original with more pixels. Viewing distance also matters: a poster that looks blurry up close may look clear from several feet away.
