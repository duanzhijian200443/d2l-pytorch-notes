A pilot project often benefits from an enthusiastic volunteer team that is motivated, adaptable, and willing to invest extra time. Their commitment can hide weaknesses, such as complicated processes, insufficient training, limited resources, or dependence on a few highly capable individuals. When the project expands, participants may have different skills, priorities, and levels of support, while managers face greater coordination and resistance. The original results may therefore reflect exceptional conditions rather than a repeatable model, requiring refinement, clear communication, resources, and broader stakeholder involvement before full implementation.
