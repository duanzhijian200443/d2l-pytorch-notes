Mara returned the borrowed ladder just before dusk, carrying it sideways through the gate like an awkward apology. She had kept it for three months, ever since her father’s roof stopped leaking and his hospital room became his permanent address. Mr. Bell opened his shed without mentioning the delay. “Did it do the job?” he asked. She nodded, then shook her head, surprised by the tears. He took the ladder and leaned it among rakes and fishing rods. From his kitchen came the smell of onions, warm bread, something simmering. “I always make too much soup,” he said. Mara knew he lived alone. For a moment, neither pretended otherwise. “Would you stay for supper?” She wiped her cheek. “Yes,” she said. “I’d like that.”
