The candle decreases in height by the same amount every hour, so its rate of burning can be found from the first two hours. It starts at 20 centimeters and measures 14 centimeters after two hours, meaning it has lost 6 centimeters in that time. Dividing 6 centimeters by 2 hours gives a constant loss of 3 centimeters per hour. To predict its height after five hours, multiply the hourly loss by five: 3 × 5 = 15 centimeters. Subtract this total loss from the original height: 20 − 15 = 5 centimeters. Therefore, under the assumption that the candle burns down at a steady rate, its predicted height after five hours is 5 centimeters. This prediction treats the burning rate as perfectly constant throughout the entire period. In reality, a candle might not burn uniformly because of changes in wax, wick behavior, airflow, or temperature, but the stated assumption requires a simple linear model.
