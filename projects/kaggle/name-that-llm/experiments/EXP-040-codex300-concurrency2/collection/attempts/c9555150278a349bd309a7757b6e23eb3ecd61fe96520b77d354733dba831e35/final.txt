A book club should welcome participants who stopped reading while still encouraging members to finish whenever possible. Requiring completion can protect discussion quality, since people who know the full arc can examine themes, structure, endings, and authorial choices with greater depth. Yet a strict rule may exclude people whose time, attention, access needs, or reaction to difficult material prevented them from finishing. Non-finishers can offer valuable insight into pacing, clarity, representation, or the reasons a book failed to engage them; those responses are part of a book’s impact. The best approach is to set clear expectations: announce whether spoilers will be discussed, invite everyone to share their reading point, and reserve substantial time for both early impressions and full-book analysis. A facilitator can ask non-finishers to explain where and why they stopped, then guide them away from speculation about later events. This creates a hospitable club without letting the conversation become shallow. Completion should be an aspiration, not a condition of belonging.
