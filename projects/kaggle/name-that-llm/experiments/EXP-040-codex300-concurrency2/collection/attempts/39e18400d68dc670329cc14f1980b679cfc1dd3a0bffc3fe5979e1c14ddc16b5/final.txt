A digital photograph is made from tiny colored squares called pixels. On a phone, the image is displayed at a relatively small size, so those pixels are packed closely together and the picture appears sharp. When you enlarge the same image into a poster, the pixels must be spread over a much larger area. The computer can create extra pixels by guessing what colors should go between the originals, but it cannot invent the fine details that were never captured. As a result, edges may look soft, textures disappear, and the image can appear blurry or blocky. A camera with higher resolution, or an original image designed for large printing, contains more information and will usually remain clearer when enlarged.
