Try a gentle “interest, not obligation” check: gather your hobby supplies and ask of each activity, “If I hadn’t already spent money on this, would I want to try it this month?” Keep supplies for one or two hobbies that spark genuine curiosity, and make them easy to reach. For hobbies you still like but lack time or energy for, create a small pause box, label it with a review date three months away, and put it out of sight. Pausing is a decision, not a failure. Give away supplies tied mainly to guilt, an imagined future self, or enjoyment you no longer feel; consider a friend, school, or community group that could use them. If you’re unsure, try one short session before deciding, without requiring yourself to finish anything. The money already spent doesn’t need to be repaid with your free time. Your supplies should support your life now, not serve as a backlog of promises.
