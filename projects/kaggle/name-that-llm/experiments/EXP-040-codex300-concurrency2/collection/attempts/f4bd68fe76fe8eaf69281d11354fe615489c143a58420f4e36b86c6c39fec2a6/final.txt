For your next revision, focus on making the article’s main point clear and organizing the material around the strongest example. Start by writing a one-sentence statement of what you want readers to understand or do after reading. Then move the interesting example near the opening so readers can see why that point matters before they encounter the background. Cut or relocate any background that isn’t necessary to understand the example, and introduce the remaining context only when it answers a question the reader is likely to have. As you revise the middle, check that each paragraph advances the central point rather than opening a separate discussion; add transitions where the connection isn’t obvious. Finally, rewrite the ending to return to the opening example and explain what readers should take away from it, rather than introducing a new direction. A useful first step would be to draft a new opening and ending, then use those to decide what belongs in between.
