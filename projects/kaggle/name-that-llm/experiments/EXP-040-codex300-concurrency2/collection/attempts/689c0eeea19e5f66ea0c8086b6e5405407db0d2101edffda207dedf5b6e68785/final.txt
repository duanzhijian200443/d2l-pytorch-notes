A seed can be alive without actively growing. For example, a dry bean seed may contain a living embryo that is dormant, meaning it is waiting for suitable conditions such as water, warmth, and oxygen. Although it looks inactive, the seed is still alive and can begin development later. Once the bean absorbs water, its tissues become active, stored food is broken down, and the embryo begins producing a root and shoot. At this stage, the seed is actively growing. If a seed is dead, it cannot germinate even when conditions are favorable. Thus, being alive means having the potential to grow, while actively growing means carrying out the biological processes that produce new plant tissues.
