Before scheduling a weekly meeting, clarify the problem it is meant to solve. Ask whether the team needs shared decisions, rapid coordination, problem-solving, or relationship-building that cannot be handled effectively through written updates or an occasional meeting. Define the specific outcomes participants should achieve and identify who genuinely needs to attend. Estimate the meeting’s total cost, including preparation and lost focus time, then compare it with the expected benefits. Consider lighter alternatives first, such as an asynchronous status update, shared dashboard, decision log, or shorter meeting triggered only when issues arise. If a meeting seems justified, run a time-limited trial with a clear agenda, required preparation, strict duration, and an explicit cancellation rule. Review afterward whether it produced valuable outcomes and adjust or stop it if not.
