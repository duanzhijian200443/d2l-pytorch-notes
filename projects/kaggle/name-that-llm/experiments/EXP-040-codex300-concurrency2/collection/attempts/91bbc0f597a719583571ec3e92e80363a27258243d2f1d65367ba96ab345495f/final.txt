The claim that a good explanation should always avoid technical vocabulary is too absolute. Good explanations make ideas understandable, but what counts as understandable depends on the audience, purpose, and context. For beginners, unfamiliar terminology can create unnecessary barriers, especially when several new terms appear without definitions. Everyday language, concrete examples, and analogies often provide a better starting point. However, technical vocabulary can also improve an explanation by making it more precise and efficient. A doctor explaining a diagnosis to another doctor, for instance, can communicate more accurately through established medical terms than through lengthy informal descriptions. Even for non-specialists, learning a technical term may be part of understanding the subject. An explanation of evolution that avoids “natural selection” entirely could leave readers less prepared to explore the topic further. The important distinction is between terminology that serves understanding and jargon that merely displays expertise. A useful approach is to introduce necessary terms gradually, define them clearly, and connect them to familiar ideas. Analogies should also be used carefully, since simplifying language can sometimes distort the underlying concept. Therefore, a good explanation should not always avoid technical vocabulary; it should avoid unnecessary difficulty while giving its audience the language needed to understand accurately.
