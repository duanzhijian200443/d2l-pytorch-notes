Starting with an item priced at 100 units, a ten-percent discount removes 10 units, leaving a reduced price of 90 units. Adding ten percent to this reduced price means adding 9 units, not the original 10 units, because the increase is calculated from 90 rather than 100. The final price is therefore 99 units. The percentages are applied to different bases: the discount is based on the original price, while the increase is based on the discounted price, so they do not cancel each other out.
