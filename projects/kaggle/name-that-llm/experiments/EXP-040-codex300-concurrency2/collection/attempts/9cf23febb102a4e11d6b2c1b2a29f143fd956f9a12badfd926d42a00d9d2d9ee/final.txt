A battery stores usable energy in the chemical arrangement of its materials, not as a supply of electricity. Its electrodes and electrolyte contain substances that can react to form a lower-energy arrangement. When a flashlight’s switch closes the circuit, these reactions drive electrons through the external wires while ions move within the battery, completing the circuit. The battery maintains a voltage, allowing energy to be transferred electrically to the light source. In an LED flashlight, some of that energy becomes visible light and the rest becomes heat. As the battery operates, its chemical composition changes and its ability to maintain voltage declines. A rechargeable battery can largely reverse these chemical changes when an external power source supplies energy.
