A sealed bag of chips can puff up in the mountains because the air pressure outside the bag decreases as elevation increases. At sea level, the air trapped inside the bag and the surrounding atmosphere exert roughly similar pressure, so the flexible packaging stays relatively flat. During a climb, there are fewer air molecules above the bag, so the outside atmospheric pressure becomes lower. The gas already sealed inside has not increased in amount, but it now pushes outward more strongly relative to the reduced external pressure. Because the bag is flexible, it expands until the pressure difference is balanced by the tension of the packaging material. This follows the general gas-law relationship between pressure, volume, and temperature: with the same amount of gas, a lower surrounding pressure permits a greater volume. Temperature can also affect the result; warming the bag makes the trapped gas expand further, while cooling can reduce puffiness. The chips themselves do not create the expansion—the changing pressure environment does.
