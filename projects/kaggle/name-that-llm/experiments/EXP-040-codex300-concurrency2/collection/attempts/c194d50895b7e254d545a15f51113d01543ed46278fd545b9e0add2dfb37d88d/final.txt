Mara stood over the community garden’s newest bed, holding a blank wooden stake. “Either I planted carrots,” she said, “or I’m lovingly watering weeds.” From the next plot, Mr. Alvarez lowered his newspaper. In three months, they had exchanged only nods. “Definitely weeds,” he said. Mara’s face fell. “Excellent weeds, though. Very confident.” She laughed, and he shuffled over with two folding stools. Together they searched the leaves for clues, arguing cheerfully about parsley, parsnips, and the unreliable promises of seed packets. By noon, they had identified nothing, but Mara knew his late wife had loved sunflowers, and he knew Mara hated eating alone. “Lunch?” she asked. He tucked the blank stake beside the mystery seedlings. “Let’s give these another week.”
