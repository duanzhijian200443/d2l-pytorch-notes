A battery stores usable energy in the chemical arrangement of materials at its two electrodes and in the electrolyte between them. Chemical reactions can lower the system’s energy by moving electrons through an external circuit while ions move inside the battery to maintain charge balance. When a small flashlight is switched on, the circuit closes, allowing the battery’s voltage to drive electrons already present in the wires through the bulb or LED. In a bulb, electrical energy becomes heat and light; in an LED, it is converted more directly into light, with some heat. Meanwhile, reactions change the electrodes’ chemical composition and reduce the difference in chemical potential that produces the voltage. Eventually, the battery can no longer maintain enough voltage to power the flashlight effectively.
