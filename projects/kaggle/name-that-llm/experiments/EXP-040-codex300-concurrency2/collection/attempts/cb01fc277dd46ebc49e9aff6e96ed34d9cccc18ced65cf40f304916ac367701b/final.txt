In a compost pile, vegetable scraps are broken down by bacteria, fungi, worms, and other small organisms. As they feed, they turn the scraps’ complex materials into simpler substances, releasing some carbon as carbon dioxide and some water as vapor or liquid. The remaining matter mixes with microbes, minerals, and partially decomposed plant material. Over time, recognizable peels and stems become dark, crumbly compost. The scraps have not vanished; their matter has been rearranged and distributed among compost, organisms, air, and water.
