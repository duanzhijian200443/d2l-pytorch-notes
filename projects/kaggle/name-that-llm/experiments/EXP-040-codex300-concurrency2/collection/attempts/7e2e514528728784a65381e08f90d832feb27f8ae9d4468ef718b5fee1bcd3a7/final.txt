Decide by starting with the outcome the team needs, not with the calendar slot. A meeting is appropriate when the work requires real-time discussion, rapid clarification, sensitive conversation, collaborative problem-solving, or a decision that depends on several perspectives. Before scheduling, ask whether the same result could be achieved through a shared document, project update, chat thread, dashboard, or short recorded message. Estimate the meeting’s total cost by multiplying its length by the number of attendees, and invite only people who must contribute or decide. If a weekly cadence is proposed, test whether the need is genuinely recurring; otherwise, meet only when specific issues arise. Define an agenda, owner, expected decisions, and time limit. After a trial period, cancel or redesign the meeting if it produces little value.
