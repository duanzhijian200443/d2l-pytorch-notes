Dividing a project by task count assumes that every task requires roughly the same effort, time, and skill. In practice, tasks can differ greatly in complexity, so completing half the tasks does not necessarily mean completing half the work. For example, one team might handle ten routine updates that each take an hour, while the other tackles ten technical problems that each require a full day of research, development, and testing. Both teams complete the same number of tasks, but their workloads are clearly unequal. Harder tasks may also involve greater uncertainty, more coordination, specialized expertise, and a higher risk of needing revisions. These demands are not visible in a simple count. A fairer division would consider estimated effort, complexity, dependencies, and the resources available to each team. Teams could then distribute tasks according to their overall demands rather than their number alone. Equal task counts measure how many items are completed, not how much work completing those items actually requires.
