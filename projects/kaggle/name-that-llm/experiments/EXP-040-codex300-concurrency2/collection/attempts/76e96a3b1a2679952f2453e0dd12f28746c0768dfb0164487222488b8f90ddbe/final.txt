A shadow’s edge depends on the apparent size of the light source and the distance between the object and the surface receiving the shadow. An extended source, such as a lamp bulb or the Sun, sends light toward the object from different directions. Near the object, the shadows cast by different parts of the source overlap closely, making the edge sharp. Farther away, these shadows spread apart, creating a wider region where only some of the light is blocked. This partially shaded region is called the penumbra, and it makes the edge look fuzzy. A larger light source produces a wider penumbra and softer edges, while a smaller source produces sharper edges. An ideal point source produces sharp edges at any distance.
