Dividing a project by task count assumes that every task requires roughly the same amount of effort, skill, time, and risk, which is often untrue. One team may receive half of the listed tasks yet face complex research, unclear requirements, technical dependencies, extensive testing, or specialized work. Meanwhile, the other half may consist of routine, well-defined tasks that can be completed quickly. Task count also ignores differences in task size: implementing a difficult system integration may demand more work than completing several minor documentation updates. In addition, harder tasks are more likely to involve setbacks, coordination, experimentation, and rework, increasing their true cost. Therefore, an equal number of tasks does not necessarily create an equal workload or an equitable division of responsibility. A fairer approach would estimate effort using factors such as expected hours, complexity, required expertise, uncertainty, dependencies, and potential impact. Teams could assign weighted values or effort points to tasks and then divide the total estimated workload rather than simply splitting the task list in half.
