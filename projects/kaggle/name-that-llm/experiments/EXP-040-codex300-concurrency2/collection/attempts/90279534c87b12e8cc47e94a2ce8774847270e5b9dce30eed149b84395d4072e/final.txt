A sealed bag of chips can puff up as it travels into the mountains because the air inside the bag was trapped at the higher atmospheric pressure found at lower altitude. As the bag rises, the surrounding atmospheric pressure decreases. The air inside then pushes outward more strongly relative to the air outside, causing the flexible bag to expand. This does not mean that new air has entered; the same trapped air simply occupies a larger volume when the external pressure is lower, consistent with Boyle’s law. Temperature can also contribute: if the bag warms during the journey, the air molecules move faster and exert more pressure, encouraging additional expansion. The chips may seem to have created extra air, but the bag’s contents and trapped air were already present. Because the packaging is flexible rather than rigid, it visibly balloons until the pressures inside and outside become more nearly balanced or the material reaches its limit. At very high elevations, the difference can be especially noticeable.
