Before electronic communication became common, public squares served as the social and informational heart of a town. People gathered there to exchange news, discuss local affairs, and learn about events beyond their community from travelers, traders, and official announcements. Market days brought farmers, craftspeople, and customers together, making commerce an opportunity for conversation and the building of relationships. Squares also hosted festivals, religious celebrations, political speeches, and public ceremonies, helping residents develop a shared identity and sense of belonging. In everyday life, they offered places to meet friends, watch passersby, or enjoy street performances. Children played while adults talked, allowing different generations to share a common space. Public squares could also reveal social divisions: access, influence, and participation were not always equal, and disputes sometimes became public confrontations. Nevertheless, their central location and regular activity made them essential meeting grounds, where information circulated through personal contact and the rhythms of community life became visible to residents and visitors alike.
