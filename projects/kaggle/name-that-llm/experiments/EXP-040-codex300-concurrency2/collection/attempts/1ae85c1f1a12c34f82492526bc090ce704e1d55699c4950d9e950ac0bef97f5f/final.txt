A seed can be alive without actively growing. For example, a dry bean seed contains a living embryo, a tiny young plant, protected by a seed coat. While the seed is dormant, its life processes occur extremely slowly, and it does not noticeably increase in size or develop new parts. Stored food helps keep it ready to grow when conditions are suitable. When the bean receives enough water, oxygen, and warmth, it begins germination. Its cells become more active, use stored food, and divide and enlarge. A root emerges first, followed by a shoot that grows toward the light. Thus, being alive means the seed maintains living cells; actively growing means those cells are producing visible development into a seedling.
