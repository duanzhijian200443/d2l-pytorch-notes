A phone screen is small, so its pixels are packed closely together and your eyes cannot easily see the individual dots that make up the image. When the same photo is enlarged for a poster, those limited pixels must cover a much larger area. Each pixel becomes larger, and the image may look soft, blocky, or blurry because there is not enough recorded detail for the new size. Software can enlarge the file by creating extra pixels between the original ones, but it is estimating rather than recovering real detail. A photo that looks excellent on a phone can therefore be too low-resolution for a sharp poster, especially when viewed up close.
