A seed is alive when its cells can carry out slow life processes and still have the potential to produce a new plant, even if it appears dry, still, and unchanged. A bean seed stored in a packet may be alive for months because its embryo remains viable, using very little energy while protected by its seed coat. It is not actively growing, however, because it has not yet begun making new roots, stems, or leaves. When the bean receives enough water, warmth, and oxygen, it germinates: the embryo resumes faster metabolism, the seed coat splits, and a root pushes downward. At that point, the seed is both alive and actively growing. A dead seed may look similar, but it cannot germinate under suitable conditions.
