Explaining a historical person’s choices in context means examining the conditions that shaped their decisions: prevailing beliefs, political pressures, available knowledge, personal interests, and the realistic alternatives they faced. This approach helps us avoid judging the past as though its actors possessed today’s assumptions and information. Yet explanation is not the same as exoneration. A choice can be understandable within its setting while still causing grave harm, reinforcing injustice, or violating standards that some contemporaries also recognized. Context clarifies responsibility; it does not erase it. Nor should sympathy for constraints silence the experiences of those who suffered the consequences. Responsible historical judgment therefore holds two ideas together: people act within worlds they did not create, but they still make choices within those worlds, and those choices remain open to moral evaluation.
