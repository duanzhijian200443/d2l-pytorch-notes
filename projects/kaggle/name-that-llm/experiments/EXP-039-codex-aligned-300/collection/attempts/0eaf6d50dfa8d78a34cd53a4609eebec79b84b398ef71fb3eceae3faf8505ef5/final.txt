She lifted the kiln door, and a breath of heat rolled over her hands. Inside, the bowls waited in their rows, glazed in the familiar deep blue she had come to trust. Her eyes moved across them, checking rims, searching for cracks, and then stopped. One bowl near the back was green. Not the pale, uncertain green she had imagined during testing, but a rich, mossy color that seemed to hold its own light. She reached for it carefully, afraid that touching it might undo the surprise. The bowl was warm against her palms. She turned it once, then again, studying the smooth curve, the small thumbprint hidden beneath the glaze. A laugh escaped her—soft, startled, almost embarrassed. She set the blue bowls aside and carried the green one through the workshop. At the front window, she placed it where the morning sun could find it. When the light entered the room, the bowl brightened, and she stood watching, suddenly unwilling to let anyone else see it yet.
