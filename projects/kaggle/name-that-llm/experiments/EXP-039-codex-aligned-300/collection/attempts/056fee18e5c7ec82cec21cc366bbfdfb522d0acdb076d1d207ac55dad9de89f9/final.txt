She opened the kiln and leaned close, already counting the bowls she could have ready for Saturday. Blue, blue, blue—the familiar glaze had deepened nicely around the rims. Then she saw the green one. Her hand stopped above it. She checked the bowl beside it, as if the mistake might belong elsewhere, then lifted it out and turned it slowly. Green pooled beneath the lip and thinned to the color of pale grapes along one side. She frowned. She had measured everything herself. Setting it apart on the workbench, she went back to unloading, but twice she caught herself looking over her shoulder. When the kiln was empty, she wiped her hands on her apron and picked up the bowl again. There was no blue in it, even underneath. She carried it to the front window, where the morning light reached through the glass. After a moment, she moved the price card out of its way.
