Mara lifted the kiln lid and stepped back into the warm breath of clay and ash, her gloved hand hovering as the shelves emerged from the dimness. Blue bowls waited in neat rows: deep cobalt at their rims, paler where the glaze had thinned, exactly as she had planned. Relief loosened her shoulders. Then she saw the green one. It sat among the blue like a leaf dropped into water, its surface bright jade with a dark, mossy pooling near the foot. For a moment she only stared. She reached for it carefully, expecting a flaw to reveal itself—a crack, a blister, some explanation—but the bowl was smooth and whole. “Well,” she murmured, though no one was there. She set it apart on the worktable and continued unloading, yet kept looking back at it. The blue bowls felt suddenly rehearsed. At last she carried the green bowl to the front window and placed it in the morning light, where it seemed to glow on its own.
