When Mara opened the kiln, heat breathed against her face, carrying the dry, mineral smell she knew as well as her own kitchen. She leaned closer, eager but cautious, and began counting the bowls by their familiar blue rims. Cobalt, midnight, rain-darkened sky—each glaze had settled almost exactly as she had hoped. Then she saw the green one. Her hand stopped above it. The bowl was the color of moss after a storm, deep at the base and bright along one uneven edge. Surprise tightened her mouth before a small laugh escaped. She turned it slowly in her padded hands, searching for a mistake: mislabeled glaze, stray ash, some careless touch. Finding no answer, she felt irritation flicker, then curiosity warm beneath it. She set the bowl apart while unloading the others, yet her gaze kept returning to it. At last she lifted it again, cradling it more gently than the blues, and carried it to the front window, where sunlight made the green glow.
